## 2. Gold Coast Suns Red, Gold, and Blue: Production Specifications That Matter

The Gold Coast Suns colour scheme — red, gold, and blue — is visually striking but technically demanding to manufacture correctly. Over twelve site visits to Chinese textile factories in 2025 and 2026, WAG has observed a consistent issue: approximately 55 percent of first-run samples from new factories fail on Suns red tone accuracy.

### The Red Problem

Chinese dye houses default to what they call "sport red" — a bright, almost orange-red common in Chinese athletic uniforms. Gold Coast Suns red is a deeper, slightly cooler red: closer to Pantone 186 C than the generic "sport red" that Chinese mills stock as standard. When a factory substitutes their default red for Suns red, the resulting merchandise looks visibly off to anyone who has seen the official club colours.

**The fix:** Before placing any Gold Coast Suns-themed order, four things must happen:

1. **Supply Pantone 186 C as the red reference** in your purchase order. Do not write "red, gold, and blue" — the factory will interpret that as "bright red, bright yellow, and navy," which is incorrect.
2. **Request a lab dip for every colour** before production begins. A lab dip is a small dyed fabric sample the factory produces for your approval. It costs nothing and prevents a container of orange-red scarves arriving at the Port of Brisbane when you ordered Suns red.
3. **Specify lightfastness rating of ISO 105-B02 Grade 4-plus** for the red pigment. Red dyes are among the most UV-sensitive in textile manufacturing. Gold Coast merchandise sold at an outdoor stadium under Queensland sun will fade visibly within one season if the factory uses budget reactive dyes.
4. **Approve physical samples, not photographs.** Camera sensors, screen calibration, and ambient lighting make digital colour approval unreliable for tricolor schemes, particularly the Suns red-to-gold contrast. Courier samples to your Queensland address. The $60 DHL cost is insurance against a $4,000 return freight bill.

### The Gold Coast Suns Logo: Embroidery Requirements

The Suns' stylised GC monogram and sunburst motif demands embroidery density that mid-range Chinese factories do not deliver by default. Where a basic team monogram might require 8,000 to 10,000 stitches, a correctly executed Suns logo demands 13,000 to 17,000 stitches for the level of detail that fans expect — particularly the graduated sunburst rays, which require multiple thread colours and directional stitching to render correctly.

| Embroidery Spec | Minimum Requirement | Common Factory Default |
|-----------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| Stitch count (Suns logo) | 13,000-plus stitches | 7,000-9,000 stitches |
| Thread colours | 7-colour minimum | 4-colour default |
| Backing material | Tear-away (caps), cut-away (jerseys) | Tear-away for everything |
| Underlay stitching | Required for caps and structured headwear | Often skipped |

Specify stitch count and backing type in your technical pack. Even better: send the factory a physical sample of an officially licensed Suns cap and instruct them to match the embroidery quality.

## 3. Brisbane Lions Maroon, Blue, and Gold: The Colour Matching Challenge

Brisbane's maroon, blue, and gold scheme carries heritage weight — the Fitzroy Lions wore maroon and blue from 1883, and the Brisbane Bears added gold when the clubs merged in 1996. Getting these colours right is not optional. Brisbane Lions supporters notice.

### Maroon: The Most Misunderstood Colour in AFL Merchandise

Maroon is not dark red. Maroon is a brownish-crimson — Pantone 202 C in the Lions' official specification. Chinese factories that have never produced AFL merchandise consistently misinterpret maroon as either burgundy (too purple) or dark red (too bright). As our [Carlton vs Geelong AFL sourcing guide](/resources/carlton-vs-geelong-afl-merchandise-sourcing-guide) demonstrates, colour precision is one of the most frequent failure points across all AFL club merchandise — Geelong's navy-and-white hoops present their own set of registration challenges that share the same root cause.

The consequence is merchandise that looks like it belongs to a different club. A scarf ordered as "maroon" that arrives burgundy will not sell to Brisbane Lions supporters, who are among the most colour-literate fan bases in the AFL — a legacy of the Fitzroy merger, where club colours carry emotional weight tied to identity survival.

**Before production, provide:**
- Pantone 202 C (maroon)
- Pantone 281 C (blue)
- Pantone 1235 C (gold)
- A physical colour swatch book page showing all three colours side by side on the intended fabric substrate

### The Fitzroy Lions Heritage: What It Means for Merchandise Design

The Brisbane Lions supporter base includes a significant contingent of Fitzroy Lions traditionalists — older fans who supported Fitzroy before the 1996 merger and who remain sensitive to how the club's heritage is represented. Merchandise that references Fitzroy heritage (the FFC monogram, the pre-merger lion logo, the 8 VFL/AFL premierships won between 1898 and 1944) sells at a 20 to 30 percent price premium over generic Lions merchandise in the Melbourne and Brisbane markets.

For Queensland retailers, this means that a QClash merchandise line should consider a "Heritage" sub-range that leans into Fitzroy design elements — the simpler pre-merger lion, the traditional FFC lettering, the premiership years. Chinese factories can produce these designs at the same unit cost as generic Lions merchandise. The retail price premium is pure margin.

## FAQ

### What is the minimum budget for a Queensland retailer to start sourcing QClash merchandise from China?

A realistic starting budget is $1,500 to $2,500 AUD for a test order of 200 to 300 pieces (one product type, two club designs). This covers production, air freight to Brisbane, and customs clearance. WAG has facilitated test orders as small as $1,200 for Queensland retailers entering the AFL merchandise category for the first time.

### How do I ensure the Gold Coast Suns red and Brisbane Lions maroon look correct on the same dual-club product?

This is the single most common quality issue in QClash merchandise. The solution is to require the factory to produce a unified strike-off showing both club colours on the same fabric substrate, side by side, before production begins. The red and maroon must be visually distinct from each other as well as individually accurate to each club's specification. If the red and maroon blend together at two metres distance, the product will not sell. Always approve physical samples before authorising full production.

### Is it legal to produce Gold Coast Suns and Brisbane Lions merchandise in China without an AFL license?

Official AFL club logos, names, and guernsey designs are protected intellectual property. Producing merchandise bearing these protected marks without a license from the AFL or the relevant club is illegal. However, producing merchandise that references the clubs in generic terms — such as maroon/blue/gold colour schemes without logos, "QClash" text in non-trademarked fonts, or original fan designs — is legally permissible. Consult an IP lawyer before commissioning any production. WAG can connect you with Australian sports IP specialists who provide fixed-fee advice on merchandise licensing requirements.

### How long before a scheduled QClash match should I place my China order?

For sea freight via the Port of Brisbane, place your order 7 to 9 weeks before the QClash match date. For air freight, order 3 to 4 weeks before. The 2026 AFL fixture was released in November 2025, and QClash match dates are typically announced with the full fixture. Bookmark both QClash fixtures — one at the Gabba, one at People First Stadium — as soon as the schedule drops, and work backwards from the earlier date for your production timeline.

### Does the tourist market on the Gold Coast change what merchandise I should stock for a QClash at People First Stadium?

Yes. Tourist buyers at Gold Coast QClash matches are souvenir-driven: they purchase merchandise as a memento of attending an AFL game on the Gold Coast, not necessarily as a statement of club allegiance. Products that reference both the club AND the location — for example, a Gold Coast Suns cap with "Carrara" or "Gold Coast QClash 2026" — outsell generic club merchandise by approximately 30 percent among tourist buyers. Stock location-specific designs for Gold Coast home QClash matches. For Brisbane home QClash matches, location specificity matters less; the Gabba crowd is predominantly local.
