## 2. Colour Production: Why Both Club Palettes Challenge Chinese Factories

The Broncos and Titans colour schemes present very different manufacturing challenges. Understanding both — and specifying correctly in your purchase order — is the difference between a product fans buy and one they return.

### Brisbane Broncos: The Maroon That Launched a Million Arguments

Broncos maroon is approximately Pantone 202C — a deep, saturated burgundy-leaning maroon that must appear rich under both Queensland daylight and Suncorp Stadium floodlights. Chinese dye houses default to what they call "sport maroon" — a flat, brownish dark red that looks acceptable under factory fluorescent lighting and completely wrong at a 6pm kick-off.

Three specifications that prevent maroon failure:

1. **Require lab dips on the actual production fabric.** A cotton swatch dyed maroon tells you nothing about how 240 GSM polyester knit holds the same dye. The lab dip must be on your production substrate.
2. **Specify ISO 105-B02 Grade 4+ lightfastness.** Queensland sun fades poorly dyed maroon to a muddy brown within one season. This is the most common quality complaint from Broncos fans purchasing replica jerseys.
3. **Approve under multiple light sources.** Request photos of the approved lab dip under daylight (6500K), warm LED (3000K), and cool fluorescent (4000K). Stadium lighting varies, and Broncos maroon shifts dramatically under different colour temperatures.

### Gold Coast Titans: Sky Blue Is Harder Than It Looks

Titans sky blue appears simple. It is not. The official club blue sits in a narrow band between cyan and cerulean. Factories that substitute a generic "light blue" produce a washed-out pastel that looks closer to a baby shower decoration than an NRL jersey.

The harder problem is the Titans' secondary palette. The club uses gold (approximately Pantone 1235C) as an accent colour alongside navy. A three-colour scheme — sky blue, navy, and gold — demands clean colour separation in both dye and print processes. Cheap sublimation causes bleeding at the blue-navy boundary, producing a muddy transition that is visible from five metres away.

| Colour Challenge | Broncos (Maroon + Gold) | Titans (Sky Blue + Navy + Gold) |
|-----------------|------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Primary colour difficulty | High — maroon is unforgiving | Medium — sky blue is narrow-band |

The practical implication: Titans merchandise costs 10 to 18 percent more to produce than single-colour or two-colour club merchandise at the factory level. That premium gets absorbed by the higher retail margin achievable through direct China sourcing — but only if you specify colour requirements precisely in the purchase order. ## 7. Build a Supply Chain That Serves Both Clubs Year-Round

The most common mistake Queensland retailers make is treating their Titans order as a one-off experiment — a single 200-unit scarf order to test the waters — rather than building a dual-club supply chain that operates continuously.

The retailers who succeed in the Queensland derby merchandise market follow a different model:

**Integrated order planning.** Instead of placing separate Broncos and Titans orders six months apart, they plan both clubs' seasonal merchandise as a single procurement exercise. The combined order volume (e.g., 800 Broncos items + 600 Titans items = 1,400 total units) strengthens negotiating position on per-unit pricing, factory priority, and payment terms.

## FAQ

### How long before the Broncos vs Titans derby should I place my China sourcing order?

Place your order 12 to 14 weeks before the target derby date. This allows 25 to 35 days for production, 14 to 18 days for sea freight from Shenzhen to Brisbane, 7 to 10 days for customs clearance and distribution to your store, and 14 days of buffer. If the derby is scheduled in the first six rounds of the season (March to mid-April), start the sourcing process in November when the NRL draw is released. For late-season derbies (Rounds 20-27), the extended lead time gives you flexibility to use slower, cheaper sea freight rather than paying the air freight premium.

### Do I need an NRL license to sell Broncos or Titans fan merchandise?

If you purchase from an NRL-licensed manufacturer, the license sits with the manufacturer and flows through to your product. If you produce custom merchandise using club-adjacent colours (maroon and gold, sky blue and navy) with original designs and supporter-focused messaging — without reproducing official club logos, the NRL shield, player names, or player likenesses — you generally do not need a license. Club colours alone are not trademarked. Club logos, wordmarks, and player images are. Many Queensland retailers use a hybrid strategy: club-colour blanks sourced from China at high margins, with optional locally-applied licensed finishing for stores that want the official branding. Consult a trademark attorney before commissioning production using any NRL-protected intellectual property.

### What is the minimum budget to test Titans merchandise for the first time?

A minimum viable first order of 200 to 300 Titans items across two product categories — such as 150 scarves and 100 caps — costs approximately $1,800 to $2,500 AUD landed from a Chinese manufacturer. At conservative sell-through rates and standard NRL fan merchandise pricing, this generates $5,000 to $7,500 in retail revenue. The key risk is unsold Titans inventory if the season's derby attendance underwhelms. Mitigate this risk by ordering the same quantity of Broncos merchandise in the same categories from the same factory — the combined order volume reduces per-unit cost while the Broncos line's reliable sell-through absorbs any Titans shortfall.

### Why do Titans colours cost more to produce than Broncos colours?

The Titans colour scheme uses three distinct, non-adjacent colours — sky blue, navy, and gold — that require separate dye baths, careful colour separation during sublimation printing, and more complex embroidery thread changes. Broncos merchandise uses two colours — maroon and gold — which are less technically demanding to separate. The per-unit production cost difference is approximately 10 to 18 percent. However, this premium is offset by the higher retail price that Titans merchandise commands due to supply scarcity. Titans fans are accustomed to paying a premium for club merchandise because it is harder to find — the scarcity itself supports the higher retail price.

### Which Chinese regions produce the best NRL fan merchandise?

For sublimated polyester jerseys and supporter apparel: Jinjiang and Quanzhou in Fujian province — the densest sportswear manufacturing cluster in China, where factories routinely produce for Australian and European sports brands. For knitted scarves: Zhangjiagang in Jiangsu province, where specialist knitting mills can execute the multi-colour jacquard patterns that NRL scarves require. For structured caps: Yangzhou in Jiangsu province, home to China's largest headwear manufacturing cluster. For flags and promotional accessories: Yiwu in Zhejiang province — the world's largest wholesale market for small commodities, where flag MOQs start as low as 100 units. For the Queensland market specifically, prioritise factories in Fujian and Guangdong that can ship via Shenzhen or Xiamen to Brisbane — this routing combination produces the lowest total landed cost for QLD importers.

### How do I handle quality control when ordering both Broncos and Titans merchandise from the same factory?

The primary quality risk in dual-club production is colour cross-contamination — maroon dye particles contaminating Titans sky blue fabric during production changeover. Mitigate this by: (1) requiring the factory to run separate dye baths and clean all equipment between club batches, (2) specifying in the purchase contract that each club's colours must pass independent colourimeter testing with a Delta E value of 2.0 or less versus the Pantone standard, (3) scheduling third-party quality inspection to coincide with the production of each club's batch rather than inspecting both batches together at the end, and (4) requesting that the factory produce a small Titans sample immediately after completing the Broncos run to verify no cross-contamination occurred. The incremental cost of split-batch inspection is approximately $180 to $250 and is recoverable in the first five returned jerseys it prevents.
