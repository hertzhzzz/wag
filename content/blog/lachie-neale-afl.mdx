## 3. Player-Specific Manufacturing — Name, Number, and Brownlow Detail

Producing player-specific AFL merchandise in China presents manufacturing challenges that club-general merchandise does not. A Brisbane Lions scarf with the club colours and logo is a single-design product that can be produced in a continuous run of thousands of units. A Lachie Neale #9 guernsey requires name and number application — heat-pressed vinyl or tackle twill — as a secondary production step, and every additional production step introduces a new failure point.

### The Name-and-Number Problem

Player name and number application on AFL guernseys is typically executed via heat-transfer vinyl (HTV) for supporter-grade products and tackle-twill stitching for premium-grade products. Chinese factories experienced in generic sports uniform production understand HTV application, but AFL-specific numbering comes with requirements that generic factories routinely miss.

AFL player numbers are positioned on the back of the guernsey at a standard 280 to 320 millimetre height and centred horizontally with a tolerance of 5 millimetres. The name is positioned above the number in a curved arc at 40 to 50 millimetre character height, centred above the number block. Chinese factories producing basketball or soccer jerseys default to different positioning standards — larger numbers, lower placement, or straight-line names instead of arched — that produce an immediately "off" result.

| Specification | AFL Standard | Generic Sportswear Default | Impact of Failure |
|--------------|-------------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| Back number height | 280-320mm | 250-350mm (wide variance) | Looks oversized or undersized |
| Number centering tolerance | +/- 5mm | +/- 12mm | Visibly off-centre to the wearer |
| Name arc radius | 180-220mm | Straight line (common default) | Looks like a soccer jersey |
| Name character height | 40-50mm | 30-35mm (too small) | Unreadable at distance |
| HTV weeding quality | Full weeding, no bridges | Partial weeding accepted | "NEALE" reads as "NFALE" |
| Number font | AFL-standard custom block | Generic athletic block | Wrong serif/stroke ratio |

### Brownlow Medallist Embroidery Detail

Merchandise that references Neale's Brownlow achievements — "2x Brownlow Medallist" text, a miniature Brownlow medal graphic, or commemorative stitching — requires embroidery precision that exceeds club-logo standards.

A Brownlow medal graphic rendered in embroidery at 25 to 35 millimetre diameter requires 8,000 to 12,000 stitches to reproduce the medal's fine detail: the central footballer figure, the surrounding text, and the ribbon detail. Factories that under-specify stitch count produce Brownlow graphics that read as a brown oval with no recognisable internal detail. The difference between 8,000 and 5,000 stitches is the difference between a premium commemorative product and a screen-printed alternative that costs less but sells for less.

| Embroidery Spec | Club Logo (Standard) | Brownlow Medal Graphic (Premium) |

The premium embroidery requirement adds $0.80 to $1.20 per unit to factory cost compared to a standard club-logo embroidery — but it supports a retail price premium of $10 to $15 per unit for "Brownlow medallist" merchandise over standard player merchandise. The embroidery investment pays for itself within the first five units sold.

### Colour Consistency for Dual-Club Player Merchandise

When a player is the subject of trade speculation between two clubs with different colour schemes, the colour management challenge multiplies. Brisbane Lions require maroon (Pantone 202 C), blue (Pantone 281 C), and gold (Pantone 1235 C). Collingwood requires black (Pantone Black 6 C) and white — arguably the simplest colour scheme in the AFL but one that is unforgiving of any white-yellowing or black-fading.

A factory producing Neale merchandise for both Brisbane and Collingwood audiences must maintain separate dye lots, separate embroidery thread sets, and separate quality control checklists for each club's colour scheme. A maroon that is acceptable on a Brisbane Lions scarf will read as wrong on anything associated with Collingwood, and vice versa. The simplest approach is to treat Brisbane Neale merchandise and Collingwood Neale merchandise as separate production runs with separate technical packs — even if they are produced in the same factory.
