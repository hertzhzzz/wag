Jersey production for NZ cricket merchandise requires specific attention to three areas that differ from general cricket jersey sourcing.

**Black colour fastness.** The single most common quality failure in NZ cricket merchandise is colour fade. Low-quality sublimation produces a black that fades to dark grey after 5-10 washes. ## The Licensing Question

A common concern for retailers considering individual athlete merchandise is licensing. Kane Williamson is a contracted professional athlete with commercial agreements, and using his name and image on commercial products without permission carries legal risk.

The practical approach for Australian retailers is two-tiered:

**Tier 1: No-license merchandise (immediately accessible).**
- Products that reference Williamson by name and general cricketing context without using his image or official NZ Cricket marks
- T-shirts with cricket-themed graphics and the text "KW" or "22" (Williamson's ODI/T20 number)
- Colour-matched Blackcaps supporter merchandise in black and silver with no player likeness
- Commemorative items that reference memorable innings (e.g., "The 2019 World Cup Final" without Williamson's image)

**Tier 2: Licensed merchandise (requires negotiation).**
- Products using Williamson's photograph, signature, or direct likeness
- Items featuring the NZ Cricket crest alongside Williamson's name
- Official replica jerseys with Williamson's name and number printed

For Tier 1 products, no licensing agreement is required. For Tier 2 products, the retailer should approach Williamson's management through NZ Cricket's commercial department. For small-to-medium Australian retailers, Tier 1 products represent 80% of the addressable market at zero licensing risk.
