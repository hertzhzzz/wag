## The劳务派遣 Framework and Australian Business Compliance

The Chinese term 劳务派遣 (labour dispatch / labour hire) describes a workforce management model that has parallels in the Australian employment services landscape. Understanding this framework helps Australian businesses appreciate how workforce services are structured in both markets and identify opportunities for cross-border knowledge transfer.

### Comparing Labour Dispatch Models

Australia and China both operate labour hire / dispatch systems, though the regulatory frameworks differ significantly:

**Australian labour hire regulation**: Each state and territory in Australia has developed or is developing labour hire licensing schemes. Queensland's Labour Hire Licensing Act 2017 establishes requirements for labour hire providers operating in Queensland, creating compliance obligations that affect how businesses structure their labour supply arrangements.

**Chinese劳务派遣 framework**: China reformed its劳务派遣 regulations in 2014, restricting the use of dispatch labour to temporary, auxiliary, or substitute positions and requiring equal pay and conditions for dispatched workers compared to direct employees.

Understanding these parallel regulatory evolutions provides context for how employment services policy develops and where best practices can transfer between jurisdictions.

### Compliance Implications for Australian Businesses

Australian businesses engaging with labour hire arrangements or employment service providers should maintain awareness of compliance obligations:

**Queensland labour hire licensing**: Businesses must verify that labour hire providers hold current Queensland Labour Hire Licenses before engaging their services. This verification requirement creates due diligence obligations that affect procurement decisions.

**National employment standards**: Regardless of how workers are engaged, Australian businesses must meet National Employment Standards, creating baseline compliance requirements that apply across all employment arrangements.

**Award and agreement coverage**: Enterprise awards and collective agreements apply to workers supplied through labour hire arrangements, meaning businesses using labour hire must ensure appropriate classification and pay outcomes.

## Workforce Training Equipment Procurement Best Practices

For Australian businesses sourcing workforce training equipment from China, establishing effective procurement practices reduces risk and improves outcomes.

### Supplier Qualification Approaches

Effective supplier qualification for workforce training equipment involves several stages:

**Capability assessment**: Evaluate Chinese manufacturers' ability to produce equipment meeting Australian standards, including quality certifications, production capacity, and export experience.

**Compliance verification**: Confirm that sourced equipment meets Australian-relevant standards, whether for workplace safety, training accreditation, or product quality requirements.

**Sample evaluation**: Obtain equipment samples before committing to bulk orders, evaluating physical quality, functionality, and packaging against specifications.

**Reference checking**: Contact other Australian importers who have sourced similar equipment, gathering intelligence on supplier reliability, communication quality, and dispute resolution history.

### Tariff and Import Consideration

When sourcing workforce training equipment from China, Australian businesses should account for:

**MFN tariff rates**: Most-favoured-nation tariff rates apply to goods not qualifying for preferential treatment under free trade agreements. Workforce training equipment tariff rates vary by product category.

**ChAFTA preferential access**: China-Australia Free Trade Agreement provides preferential tariff treatment for qualifying goods originating from China. Meeting rules of origin requirements enables access to reduced tariff rates.

**Customs classification**: Correct customs classification determines applicable tariff rates. Workforce training equipment may span multiple classification categories depending on product type.

**Duty calculation**: Import duty is calculated on the customs value of goods, including cost, insurance, and freight (CIF). Accurate valuation prevents both underpayment and overpayment of duties.
