## 4. Reds Maroon and Chiefs Tricolor: Production Specifications Chinese Factories Routinely Miss

Rugby union merchandise carries specific production requirements that differ materially from AFL and NRL gear. Over twelve site visits to Chinese textile factories in 2025 and 2026, WAG has identified consistent failure points that Queensland retailers must address before placing an order.

### The Reds Maroon Problem

Queensland Reds maroon is one of the most frequently misproduced team colours in Australian sport. Chinese dye houses default to what they call "sport maroon" — a purple-leaning shade used in Chinese university sportswear. The Reds' official maroon is a deeper, red-leaning tone: closer to Pantone 202 C than the generic maroon most factories stock.

**The fix, in four steps:**

1. **Supply the Pantone reference in your purchase order.** Specify Pantone 202 C for screen-printed applications or the equivalent textile Pantone code for dyed fabrics. The phrase "maroon" without a Pantone reference will produce a purple-leaning result approximately 70% of the time on first sampling.
2. **Request a lab dip before bulk production.** A lab dip is a small dyed fabric swatch produced at zero cost to you. Reject it until the maroon matches. This single step prevents a container of purple-tinted scarves arriving at the Port of Brisbane.
3. **Specify ISO 105-B02 Grade 4+ lightfastness.** Rugby union is a winter sport in Australia, but Queensland winter sun is still harsh. Maroon pigment degrades faster than navy or black — it can shift to a faded pink after 8-10 washes if the factory uses economy-grade reactive dyes.
4. **Approve physical samples, not photographs.** Camera white balance and screen calibration make digital colour approval unreliable for dark red tones. Have samples couriered to Brisbane. The $55 DHL cost is trivial compared to a $3,800 return freight bill.

### The Chiefs Tricolor Registration Challenge

The Chiefs' black, red, and yellow tricolor scheme presents a different problem: colour registration across seams. Unlike the Reds' predominantly solid-maroon jersey, Chiefs merchandise incorporates three distinct colour blocks that must align precisely. A 1.5mm misalignment at a side seam creates a visible colour step that is immediately noticeable.

## 6. Intellectual Property: How to Sell Chiefs vs Reds Merchandise Legally

This is the most commonly misunderstood aspect of rivalry merchandise. Here is what Queensland retailers need to know for the Australian market.

### What Is Protected

- The Queensland Rugby Union (QRU) logo, the Queensland Reds official crest featuring the koala, and the Reds' registered wordmark are trademarks
- The Chiefs Rugby Club logo, the chieftain head emblem, and the "Chiefs" wordmark in stylised form are registered trademarks in New Zealand and protected under Australian trademark law via international registration
- The Super Rugby Pacific competition logo is a registered trademark of SANZAAR
- Official playing jersey designs may be protected by design registration

### What Is Not Protected

- The colour maroon used generically (Queensland's sporting colour)
- The colours black, red, and yellow used generically (Chiefs' colour scheme)
- The words "Chiefs" and "Reds" used in plain text as team descriptors (they are common English words; the Reds' trademark covers the stylised logo, not the word itself in descriptive use)
- The word "Queensland" as a geographic descriptor
- Rugby-themed imagery (goal posts, rugby balls, scrum graphics) used in original designs
- The year "2026" and match descriptors such as "Chiefs vs Reds"

### The Recommended Approach: Colour-Association Design

The legally tested strategy for Australian SMEs producing sports rivalry merchandise is the colour-association approach — the same method used successfully by retailers in the AFL, NRL, and cricket merchandise markets.

Products use:
- Team-adjacent colour schemes (maroon for Reds, black-red-yellow for Chiefs)
- Original rugby-themed graphic designs (not copied from official logos)
- Match descriptors in plain, non-stylised fonts (e.g., "CHIEFS v REDS — SUNCORP STADIUM 2026")
- Supporter-focused messaging (e.g., "REDS SUPPORTER" rather than "OFFICIAL QUEENSLAND REDS MERCHANDISE")

This approach requires zero licensing negotiations, zero royalty payments, and zero legal risk provided your designs do not reproduce registered trademarks.

A Gold Coast-based rugby merchandise retailer adopted this strategy for the 2025 British and Irish Lions tour of Australia. They produced 1,400 units of colour-associated supporter merchandise across 5 product categories — generating approximately $42,000 in revenue with no licensing costs and zero legal challenges.

> **Important disclaimer:** This article provides general information, not legal advice. If you plan to scale beyond $50,000 in annual rugby merchandise revenue, engage an Australian intellectual property lawyer to review your specific designs before production. Trademark law is jurisdiction-specific, and Australian courts assess passing-off claims on a case-by-case basis.
