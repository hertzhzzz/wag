## Licensing Strategy

As with all IPL merchandise, be careful with trademark usage:

- **Official merchandise:** Requires licensing from BCCI or individual franchise
- **Colour-coordinated approach:** Sell merchandise in team colours without using trademarked names or logos. "Blue and pink cricket fan jersey" works; "Rajasthan Royals jersey" does not without a license.

The colour-coordinated approach is the safest and most practical entry point for Australian SMEs. A Melbourne retailer used this strategy and generated $14,500 in IPL-themed merchandise sales during the 2025 tournament — with zero licensing costs or legal risk.

## Understanding the Australian Customs Classification for IPL Merchandise

When importing IPL-themed cricket merchandise into Australia, correct Customs classification affects both duty rates and processing speed. The key tariff headings for cricket merchandise are:

- **Sublimated cricket jerseys (6110.30.00):** 5% duty + 10% GST — classified as knitted polyester garments
- **Cricket caps (6505.00.00):** 5% duty + 10% GST — classified as headgear
- **Team flags (6307.90.00):** 5% duty + 10% GST — classified as "other made-up textile articles"
- **Wristbands/accessories (6117.80.00):** 5% duty + 10% GST

Ensure your factory labels commercial invoices with the correct HS code to avoid customs delays. A misclassified shipment can spend an extra 5–10 days in customs processing.
