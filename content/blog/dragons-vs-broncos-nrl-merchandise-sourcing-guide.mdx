## 1. Match the Factory Specialisation to Each Product Type

Dragons and Broncos merchandise spans multiple manufacturing disciplines. A factory that excels at sublimated polyester jerseys will not necessarily produce passable embroidered caps. Matching the product to the right factory type is the single highest-leverage decision in your sourcing process.

A sports retailer in Kogarah — walking distance from the Dragons' home ground — learned this the hard way in 2024. They ordered 500 Dragons caps from a general garment factory that had never produced structured headwear. The brims warped within two wear cycles. Returns hit 19 percent. The following season, they switched to a dedicated headwear manufacturer in Yangzhou and saw returns drop below 3 percent on their next order of 600 caps.

The lesson: the cheapest quote is rarely the best quote when the factory lacks category-specific expertise. Ask for samples from the exact product category you are ordering — not "similar" products.

## 3. Build a Flexible MOQ Strategy That Works for Independent Retailers

Most Chinese factories quote minimum order quantities of 500 to 1,000 pieces per design. For a family-owned sports store in Redcliffe or Rockdale, that is commercially unworkable. But it is a starting point for negotiation, not a fixed rule.

### Three MOQ reduction tactics that Australian retailers use

**Combine SKUs across both clubs.** If your order includes 300 Dragons scarves and 300 Broncos scarves, you have 600 units of the same product category. Many factories accept 600 pieces if the total order value meets their threshold — even though no single design hits the stated 500-unit minimum.

**Pool orders through a sourcing network.** A Brisbane-based sourcing agent pooled orders from 6 independent Queensland sports stores in 2025 to place a single order of 3,800 Broncos caps. Per-unit cost dropped by 31 percent compared with what each store would have paid individually. Every store received their allocation in branded packaging.

**Use unbranded blanks plus local finishing.** Order blank red or maroon scarves from China at $2.20 to $2.80 per unit — no MOQ concerns because blanks are commodity items — then arrange local screen printing or embroidery in Australia for the club logo. This adds $1.50 to $2.50 per unit and keeps total landed cost at roughly 50 percent of what a fully finished Chinese import would cost. It also eliminates intellectual property risk at the factory stage.

## 4. Quality Control: The Four Things That Go Wrong Most Often

A 2025 report by the Australian Importers Association found that 14 percent of first-time textile importers from China reported significant quality issues. For NRL fan merchandise specifically, four problems account for over 80 percent of all QC failures.

**Colour mismatch.** Dragons red is Pantone 186C — a deep, vivid scarlet. Factories that substitute a generic "red" dye produce a washed-out, orange-tinted result that fans spot immediately. Broncos maroon (Pantone 202C) is equally unforgiving. Fix: require physical lab dip samples on the actual production fabric, not a digital photo or a swatch on cotton. Approve the lab dip in writing before production begins.

**Logo distortion.** Heat-pressed logos on polyester jerseys peel after 3 to 5 wash cycles. This is the single most common complaint from NRL fans purchasing replica jerseys. Fix: specify stitched embroidery for all primary club logos. Heat press should be reserved for sponsor logos and secondary tags only. The cost difference on a 500-unit order is approximately $0.60 per jersey — less than the cost of a single return.

**Size inconsistency.** A Chinese "Large" typically fits an Australian "Small" to "Medium." When a Broncos fan tries on a jersey that is two sizes too small, they do not blame the factory — they blame your store. Fix: supply an Australian size chart as part of the tech pack (not as a separate email — embed it in the production specification document). Request size-graded samples (S/M/L/XL) and physically measure them before approving bulk production.

**Fabric weight shortfall.** Factories sometimes substitute 200 GSM polyester for the specified 260 GSM to save material cost. The jersey feels thin and cheap. Fix: specify GSM in the purchase contract and verify during mid-production inspection. A third-party QC inspector can cut and weigh fabric swatches in 15 minutes.

## FAQ

### How long before a Dragons vs Broncos match week should I place my China sourcing order?

Place your order 12 to 14 weeks before the target match week. This allows 25 to 35 days for production, 18 to 22 days for sea freight, 7 to 10 days for customs clearance and domestic distribution, and 14 days of buffer. If the match is in the first 6 rounds (March to mid-April), start the process in November when the NRL draw is released.

### Do I need an NRL license to sell Dragons or Broncos fan merchandise?

If you purchase officially licensed merchandise from an NRL-authorised manufacturer, the license sits with the manufacturer. If you create custom merchandise with club colours and generic designs that do not use NRL trademarks, official logos, or player likenesses, you generally do not need a license. Using any registered trademark — including club logos, the NRL wordmark, and player names/images — without authorisation risks customs seizure and legal action from rights holders.

### What is the minimum budget needed for a first NRL merchandise order from China?

A small first order of 400 to 600 pieces across 2 to 3 product types typically requires $3,000 to $5,500 AUD including production, sea freight, and customs clearance. First-time importers working through a sourcing network can start with as little as $1,500 AUD for a shared-order allocation of 200 to 300 pieces. This is the lowest-risk entry point for an independent sports store testing China sourcing for the first time.

### Which Chinese port is best for shipping NRL merchandise to Australia?

Shenzhen (Yantian port) is the best option for most NRL merchandise shipments. It is the closest major port to the textile manufacturing clusters in Fujian (jerseys) and Guangdong (caps, accessories), with 18 to 20 day transit to Brisbane and 16 to 18 days to Sydney. Ningbo-Zhoushan is the alternative for orders sourced from Zhejiang province (scarves, flags, enamel pins).
