The China Import and Export Fair — known internationally as the Canton Fair — runs twice a year in Guangzhou: in April and October. With over 25,000 exhibitors across three phases, it is the largest trade fair in the world. For Australian businesses sourcing from China, it is the single most efficient way to build a shortlist of verified suppliers before committing to factory visits. Understanding [how factory sourcing works in China](/article/factory-sourcing-china) provides additional context for making the most of your fair visit.

This guide covers how to plan a Canton Fair visit, which phase to attend, how to combine it with on-ground factory tours, and what to do after the fair. For businesses new to China sourcing, our guide to [evaluating Chinese suppliers](/article/evaluating-chinese-suppliers) provides complementary frameworks for assessing supplier credibility.

## What Is the Canton Fair?

The Canton Fair is a government-hosted trade exhibition held at the China Import and Export Fair Complex in Guangzhou (Pazhou). It began in 1957 and has run continuously twice yearly, making it one of the longest-running trade events in the world.

Unlike private trade shows, the Canton Fair covers the full breadth of Chinese manufacturing: electronics, furniture, textiles, machinery, consumer goods, and everything in between.

Why Australian businesses use it: access to factories that do not market heavily online, face-to-face first contact with decision-makers, price benchmarking across dozens of suppliers for the same product, and discovery of new product categories.

## Canton Fair Phase Guide

The fair runs across three phases, each covering different product categories.

| Phase | Typical Dates | What It Covers |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Phase 1 | Mid April / October | Electronics, household appliances, machinery, hardware, building materials |
| Phase 2 | Late April / October | Consumer goods, gifts, furniture, home decor, luggage |
| Phase 3 | Early May / November | Textiles, footwear, bags, medicines, sportswear, office supplies |

For Australian businesses: sourcing electronics or smart home products — Phase 1. Sourcing furniture, home goods, or packaging — Phase 2. Sourcing apparel, footwear, or sportswear — Phase 3.

**Book your hotel in Pazhou** (near the fair grounds) during the fair period. Hotels across Guangzhou fill quickly and prices surge.

## How to Prepare Before the Fair

**Step 1: Register for Free.** Australian businesses can register for the Canton Fair at no cost. Use the official registration portal (cantonfair.org.cn) and select "Overseas buyer" registration. Bring your QR code confirmation to the fair entrance.

**Step 2: Research Exhibitor Lists Before You Arrive.** The official Canton Fair website publishes exhibitor lists 2-3 weeks before each fair. Download the exhibitor catalogue and identify which booths you want to visit before you arrive.

What to look for: direct manufacturer booths (avoid "trading company" unless they have a specific manufacturing relationship), ISO certifications displayed, export destination information, and product range that matches your sourcing needs.

**Step 3: Prepare Your Sourcing Brief.** Bring a one-page sourcing brief in English and Mandarin. This should include products you are looking to source, estimated order volumes, quality specifications or reference samples, target pricing, and delivery timeline expectations.

Do not hand your brief to every supplier you meet. Share it selectively with your top choices — it is a negotiation tool, not a general advertisement.

## Day-by-Day Fair Strategy

**Day 1: Orientation and Supplier Identification.** Download the Canton Fair app — it has a floor plan and exhibitor search function. Walk the halls relevant to your product category. Collect business cards and take photos of booth numbers. Do not commit to anything on Day 1 — observe, compare, shortlist.

**Days 2-3: Focused Supplier Meetings.** Return to your shortlisted booths for deeper conversation. Ask specific questions: export history, minimum order quantities, sample fees, tooling costs. Request catalogues and price lists. Ask for the supplier's WeChat or email for follow-up.

Key questions to ask every supplier: "What is your minimum order quantity for this product?" "Do you manufacture this yourself or use a partner factory?" "Have you exported to Australia before?" "Can I visit your factory after the fair?"

That last question is critical. Suppliers who confidently invite factory visits are typically genuine manufacturers. Suppliers who deflect should be noted as requiring verification before any commitment.

**Days 4-5: Factory Visits (Combined Trip).** If your schedule allows, combine your Canton Fair visit with on-ground factory visits in the Pearl River Delta. Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Dongguan, and Foshan are all within 2 hours of the fair grounds.

## After the Fair: The Critical Follow-Up Phase

The most common mistake buyers make after the Canton Fair is collecting dozens of contacts and then doing nothing with them.

**Week 1 After the Fair:** Sort your contacts by product category and supplier quality. Send WeChat messages or emails to your top 10 suppliers. Request formal quotations based on the specs discussed at the fair.

**Weeks 2-4:** Evaluate quotes against each other. Schedule video calls with top 3 choices. Request samples. If a supplier looks strong, schedule an on-ground visit for your next China trip.

Be suspicious of suppliers who dramatically reduce their prices after the fair to secure your order. This often means they quoted high at the fair to leave room for negotiation, or they are a trading company without manufacturing capability.

## Pre-Fair Preparation: Exhibitor Research Strategy

### Building Your Target List Before Departure

The most effective Canton Fair visits begin weeks before departure. Pre-fair exhibitor research separates structured buyers from casual browsers, and delivers measurably better supplier outcomes. Australian businesses that invest time in pre-fair research consistently identify higher-quality suppliers than those who rely on on-site discovery alone.

The official Canton Fair website publishes booth assignments 2-3 weeks before each session. This catalogue becomes your primary research document. Cross-reference booth numbers with the exhibitor directory to identify manufacturers in your target categories. The directory indicates each exhibitor's main products, export destinations, and business type—use these filters to prioritise factories over trading companies.

For Australian businesses sourcing consumer goods, Phase 2 typically delivers the highest concentration of relevant suppliers. However, Phase 1 electronics suppliers often include components that feed into Phase 2 assembly operations, creating potential for supply chain relationships spanning multiple fair phases.

### Creating Effective Sourcing Briefs

A sourcing brief is a one-page document that communicates your requirements to shortlisted suppliers. Unlike a general advertisement distributed to every booth, a sourcing brief should be shared selectively with your highest-priority prospects—the suppliers who meet your manufacturing criteria and with whom you want to initiate a formal relationship.

Effective sourcing briefs include product category and technical specifications, estimated annual volumes and per-order quantities, target price ranges expressed in FOB or CIF terms, quality standards and reference samples where available, and delivery timeline expectations and payment terms. Include brief descriptions in both English and Mandarin—this investment signals seriousness and respects the cultural context of the commercial relationship.

The brief is a negotiation tool, not a commitment document. Sharing a sourcing brief initiates a structured conversation rather than a binding arrangement. Suppliers who receive well-prepared briefs understand they are engaging with a professional buyer and typically respond with more detailed and credible quotations.

## On-Site Evaluation: Reading Suppliers at the Fair

### Quality Indicators to Watch For

Physical trade shows like the Canton Fair create specific dynamics that reward systematic observation. Suppliers who maintain professional booth presentations, display relevant certifications prominently, and demonstrate detailed product knowledge are generally more credible than those whose booths appear underprepared. However, booth presentation alone does not guarantee factory capability—the critical question is what happens after the fair when you request factory visits.

Australian buyers commonly encounter two categories of Canton Fair exhibitors: genuine manufacturers who operate their own production facilities, and trading companies who connect international buyers with factories without themselves manufacturing anything. Trading companies are not inherently problematic, but they add a layer between buyer and manufacturer that affects communication speed, quality control capability, and pricing transparency.

The factory visit invitation remains the single most useful screening tool. Suppliers who confidently offer factory visits during or immediately after the fair are typically speaking from genuine manufacturing capability. Suppliers who deflect, delay, or offer only video demonstrations of an unspecified facility warrant additional verification before you invest significant time in their quotations.

### Red Flags and Warning Signs

Several patterns should increase your caution when evaluating Canton Fair suppliers. Suppliers who quote prices that seem too low compared to market benchmarks are often trading companies whose quoted prices do not include all costs, or manufacturers using substandard materials to achieve competitive pricing. Australian buyers who have received suspiciously low quotations and later encountered quality issues or undisclosed costs understand why this matters.

Inconsistent information across multiple conversations at the same booth suggests the supplier is either inexperienced or hiding something. If booth staff cannot confirm basic facts about their production capabilities, delivery timelines, or export experience, this inconsistency will only worsen after the fair when communications become less frequent and more asynchronous.

Suppliers who refuse to provide business registration documentation, factory photographs, or export license copies during the fair may have legitimate confidentiality concerns, but may also be遮掩 their actual business relationships. Australian buyers should balance respect for commercial confidentiality with the need for basic due diligence information.

## Pearl River Delta Factory Visit Protocols

### Planning Factory Visits Around the Fair

The Pearl River Delta encompasses Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Dongguan, Foshan, and Zhuhai—a manufacturing belt home to tens of thousands of factories. The Canton Fair venue in Pazhou, Guangzhou, sits at the centre of this region, making combined fair-and-factory-visit trips practical for Australian businesses willing to invest the time.

Factory visits work best when planned during Days 4-5 of fair attendance or in the week following the fair's conclusion. Most Canton Fair exhibitors are available for factory visits during this period because they remain in their normal business locations near Guangzhou. Building factory visits into your travel schedule converts fair contacts into verified manufacturing relationships.

Australian businesses should arrange factory visits through WeChat or email before departing for China. Cold-dropping factories without appointments typically results in limited access, superficial tours, and missed opportunities to meet production managers who can speak to technical capabilities. Pre-arranged visits ensure the right people are available and that your time on-site is used productively.

### What to Assess During Factory Visits

Factory visit assessments should follow a structured protocol covering production capability, quality management systems, and business fundamentals. Production capability assessment includes production line configuration, machinery and equipment condition, workforce scale and specialisation, and production capacity relative to your order volumes.

Quality management assessment focuses on inspection protocols, testing equipment availability, defect classification and handling procedures, and documentation practices. Factories serving international markets typically maintain more developed quality management systems than domestic-only producers, but Australian buyers should verify rather than assume.

Business fundamentals to confirm include business registration and export licensing, export experience with Australia or comparable markets, insurance coverage and liability provisions, and communication protocols for ongoing orders. These fundamentals matter because they determine whether your relationship with the factory will function smoothly once you begin placing regular orders.

## FAQ

**When does the Canton Fair run in 2026?**
The 2026 spring Canton Fair (139th session) runs approximately mid-April to early May 2026 in Guangzhou. The autumn fair runs approximately mid-October to early November 2026. Exact dates are published on cantonfair.org.cn approximately 6 weeks before each session.

**Do I need a visa to attend the Canton Fair?**
Australian citizens need a Chinese visa to enter mainland China. The standard business visa (M or F category) is appropriate for attending trade fairs and factory visits. Apply at least 4-6 weeks before your intended travel date.

**Is the Canton Fair free to attend?**
Yes, overseas buyers can register for free at the official Canton Fair website. On-site registration with a valid passport is also available but involves queues. Pre-registration is strongly recommended.

**Can I combine the Canton Fair with factory visits?**
Absolutely — and it is the recommended approach. Guangzhou is central to the Pearl River Delta manufacturing region. Shenzhen, Dongguan, and Foshan are all accessible within 1-2 hours by car or high-speed train from Pazhou.

**How do I distinguish manufacturers from trading companies at the fair?**
Ask whether the exhibitor operates their own production facility. Manufacturers will confirm their own production and invite factory visits. Trading companies may be less direct about this distinction. Check the exhibitor directory for business type indicators. Note that some genuine manufacturers also trade, so factory visit invitations provide the clearest confirmation.

**What is the minimum order quantity I should expect from Canton Fair suppliers?**
Minimum order quantities vary by product category and supplier. Electronics components typically require higher MOQs than consumer goods. General expectations range from 500 to 2,000 units per order for initial orders, with larger volumes reducing per-unit pricing. Always negotiate MOQs rather than accepting initial terms, particularly for first-time relationships.

**How do I verify supplier quality before committing to orders?**
Request samples before committing to production orders. Most Canton Fair suppliers will provide samples for freight cost reimbursement. Conduct or arrange quality inspection during production where possible. Use third-party inspection services for orders where quality consequences are significant. Request references from existing buyers in comparable markets.

**What payment terms should I negotiate with Canton Fair suppliers?**
Typical payment terms for first orders from new suppliers include 30-50% deposit with balance paid before shipment. Established relationships may allow payment terms such as letter of credit or open account arrangements. Australian buyers should ensure payment terms are documented clearly in a purchase agreement before placing orders.

**How does the Canton Fair compare to other China trade shows?**
The Canton Fair is the largest China trade show by exhibitor count and floor area. Other significant shows include the Shanghai International Furniture Fair (CIFF), the China International Fair for Trade in Services, and various specialised trade shows in Shanghai, Shenzhen, and other cities. The Canton Fair offers the broadest product range, making it ideal for diversified sourcing. Specialised shows offer deeper concentration in specific categories.

**Should I attend the Canton Fair in person or use a sourcing agent?**
In-person attendance provides the most direct supplier assessment capability and access to the full exhibitor range. Sourcing agents are appropriate when you lack travel capacity or need local representation for ongoing factory communication. Many Australian businesses use a hybrid approach: attend in person initially to establish supplier relationships, then use agents for ongoing communication and quality follow-up.
