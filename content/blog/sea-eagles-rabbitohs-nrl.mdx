## 1. Understand the Two Very Different Fan Demographics

Before you source a single product, understand who you are selling to. Manly and Souths fans do not buy the same way.

### Manly Sea Eagles fans: premium, classic, brand-loyal

The Northern Beaches demographic skews toward higher disposable income. Manly fans tend to purchase fewer items but spend more per item. The best-selling categories are replica jerseys (particularly the heritage maroon-and-white strip), premium polos with subtle club branding, and structured caps. Manly supporters are more likely to buy club-branded lifestyle merchandise — beach towels, cooler bags, premium hoodies — that they use year-round, not just on game day.

### South Sydney Rabbitohs fans: volume, colour-forward, deeply tribal

The Rabbitohs supporter base is one of the most intensely loyal in Australian sport. The cardinal red and myrtle green colour combination is worn as an identity marker. Souths fans buy more items per person — scarves, flags, beanies, car accessories, keyrings, stickers. They are more price-sensitive but dramatically higher in purchase frequency. A Souths fan might buy a new scarf every season. A Manly fan might buy one premium polo that lasts three years.

## 2. Match the Factory to the Product — Every Time

The single most common mistake Australian retailers make is using one factory for everything. A knitwear specialist in Jinjiang that produces flawless sublimated jerseys will not necessarily make a passable structured cap. Different merchandise demands different manufacturing clusters.

|---------|-------------------|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Replica jersey (Manly maroon, Souths red/green) | Jinjiang, Fujian (knitwear cluster) | 220-260 GSM polyester, full sublimation, embroidered logo | $5.50-$8.00 AUD |

| Car merchandise (seat covers, sunshades) | Wenzhou, Zhejiang (automotive textiles) | 600D polyester, UV-resistant print, elastic backing | $3.50-$6.00 AUD |

A sports store in Marrickville — squarely in Rabbitohs territory — ordered 600 Souths caps from a general garment factory in 2024. The cheapest quote is rarely the best quote when the factory lacks category-specific expertise. Ask for samples from the exact product category you are ordering. Not similar products. The exact product.

## 3. Nail the Colours — Both Clubs Are Unforgiving

NRL fans can spot a colour mismatch from across Accor Stadium. Manly's maroon and Souths' cardinal red are two of the most distinctive colour profiles in Australian sport — and two of the easiest for Chinese factories to get wrong.

### The colour challenge by club

**Manly Sea Eagles maroon (Pantone 202C).** This is a deep, burgundy-leaning maroon with cool undertones. Factories that substitute a generic dark red produce a brownish, muddy result that looks nothing like the 4 Pines Park faithful expect. The maroon must read as rich and saturated under both natural daylight and stadium floodlights — different lighting conditions can make a poorly matched maroon look completely off.

**South Sydney Rabbitohs colours.** This is the harder of the two briefs. Souths use two distinct, non-adjacent colours: cardinal red (Pantone 200C) and myrtle green (Pantone 342C). These are challenging to reproduce together on a single product. Cardinal red is vivid and warm — a true scarlet — not a deep red or a maroon. Myrtle green is a dark, forest-like green with blue undertones. On a sublimated jersey or a knitted scarf, factories must execute clean colour separation between these two hues. Cheap dye processes cause bleeding at the colour boundary — and a Souths fan will notice immediately.

### Three steps factories must follow

1. **Require physical lab dip samples on the actual production fabric.** A digital photo of a swatch on cotton proves nothing. The dye behaves differently on polyester knit than on cotton.
2. **Approve lab dips in writing before production begins.** Verbal approval over WeChat does not protect you when 500 jerseys arrive with off-colour maroon.
3. **Specify colourfastness standards in the purchase contract.** NRL merchandise is worn, washed, and worn again. Require a minimum Grade 4 colourfastness to washing (ISO 105-C06) to prevent the cardinal red from fading to salmon pink after ten washes.

A retailer in Brookvale — walking distance from Manly's home ground — learned this the hard way. Their first order of 400 Manly jerseys arrived with maroon that was visibly too brown. Customers returned them at a 16 percent rate, many commenting that the colour looked "like an old footy from 1987." The fix on the next order: lab dip approval on the actual 240 GSM polyester knit, not a cotton swatch. Returns dropped to 2 percent.

## 5. Make Minimum Order Quantities Work for Independent Retailers

Most Chinese factories quote minimum order quantities of 500 to 1,000 pieces per design. For a family-owned sports store in Dee Why or Redfern, that is commercially unworkable. But it is a starting point for negotiation, not a fixed rule.

### Three proven MOQ reduction strategies

**Combine SKUs across both clubs.** A factory might require 500 pieces per design — but if your order includes 250 Manly scarves and 250 Souths scarves, you have 500 units in the same product category. Many factories accept the combined total if the order value meets their threshold, even though no single design hits the 500-unit minimum.

**Pool orders through a sourcing network.** A Sydney-based sourcing agent pooled orders from 7 independent sports stores across the Northern Beaches and Inner West in 2025 to place a single order of 4,200 combined Manly and Souths merchandise units. Per-unit cost dropped by 28 percent compared with what each store would have paid individually. Every store received their allocation in branded packaging with their own labelling.

**Use unbranded blanks plus local finishing.** Order blank maroon or red-and-green merchandise from China at $2.20 to $2.80 per unit — commodity blanks carry no MOQ concerns — then arrange local screen printing or embroidery in Sydney for the club logo. This adds $1.50 to $2.50 per unit and keeps total landed cost at roughly 55 percent of what a fully finished Chinese import would cost. It also removes intellectual property risk for retailers concerned about using club insignia without direct licensing.

## FAQ

### How long before a Sea Eagles vs Rabbitohs match week should I place my China sourcing order?

Place your order 12 to 14 weeks before the target match week. This allows 25 to 35 days for production, 18 to 22 days for sea freight from Shenzhen to Sydney, 7 to 10 days for customs clearance and domestic distribution, and 14 days of buffer. If the derby is scheduled in the first 7 rounds (March to mid-April), start the sourcing process in November when the NRL draw is released.

### Does a Sea Eagles or Rabbitohs heritage strip require different manufacturing specifications?

Yes. Heritage or retro jerseys typically use heavier fabric (260-280 GSM versus the standard 220-240 GSM), stitched tackle-twill numbering instead of heat-pressed, and a woven club crest rather than a sublimated logo. These details increase the per-unit cost by $1.50 to $3.00 AUD but command a $15 to $25 retail price premium. Specify heritage construction requirements in the tech pack — do not assume the factory knows the difference between a current-season and heritage jersey.

### What is the minimum budget needed for a first combined Manly and Souths merchandise order?

A small first order of 500 to 800 pieces across 3 to 4 product types — covering both clubs — typically requires $3,500 to $6,500 AUD including production, sea freight, customs clearance, and domestic distribution. Retailers working through a sourcing network can start with as little as $1,800 AUD for a shared-order allocation of 250 to 350 pieces. This is the lowest-risk entry point for an independent sports store testing China sourcing for the first time.

### Do I need an NRL license to sell Manly or Souths fan merchandise?

If you purchase from an NRL-licensed manufacturer, the license sits with the manufacturer. If you source generic club-colour merchandise (maroon and white or red and green products without club logos, wordmarks, or player likenesses), you generally do not need a license. Using any registered NRL trademark — including club logos, the NRL shield, player names, or images — without authorisation risks customs seizure and legal action under Australian intellectual property law. Many independent retailers use a hybrid approach: club-colour blanks from China with locally licensed finishing applied in Australia.

### Which Chinese port is best for shipping NRL merchandise to Sydney?

Shenzhen (Yantian port) is the best option for most NRL merchandise. It is the closest major port to the textile manufacturing clusters in Fujian (jerseys) and Guangdong (caps, accessories), with 18 to 20 days transit to Port Botany in Sydney. Ningbo-Zhoushan is the alternative for orders sourced from Zhejiang province (scarves, flags, enamel pins) with transit times of 16 to 19 days to Sydney. Avoid Shanghai for NRL merchandise shipments — congestion adds 3 to 5 days versus Shenzhen or Ningbo with no cost advantage.

### Can factories handle both Manly maroon and Souths cardinal red in a single production run without cross-contamination?

Yes, but you must verify the factory's colour separation process during sampling. The most common problem with two-club orders is maroon dye particles contaminating red fabric, producing a murky, brownish cardinal red. Reputable factories run separate dye baths for each colour and clean all equipment between production batches. Specify in the contract that each club's colours must pass independent colourimeter testing — a Delta E (colour difference) value of 2.0 or less versus the Pantone standard.
