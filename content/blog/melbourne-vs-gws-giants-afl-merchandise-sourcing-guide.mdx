### 1. Partner With Small-Batch Chinese Factories That Understand AFL Specs

The single biggest barrier for Australian sports retailers is minimum order quantities (MOQs). Large Chinese textile factories typically demand 1,000 to 3,000 units per design. For a small retailer stocking for a single rivalry match, that volume is commercially unworkable.

The solution is to work with mid-tier Chinese factories — typically 30 to 80 employees — that specialise in smaller production runs of 200 to 500 pieces per design. These factories exist in clusters around Ningbo (textiles), Dongguan (headwear), and Yiwu (promotional items), and they are increasingly willing to accommodate Australian orders.

**What to confirm before placing your first order:**

- The factory has produced for English-speaking markets before — ask for references from Australian or UK clients
- They understand AFL-specific requirements: 12-gauge knit density for scarves, 220-260 GSM polyester interlock for jerseys, Australian sizing charts (2XS to 5XL)
- They can match AFL team colours — Melbourne's navy blue and GWS's charcoal and orange are notoriously difficult to get right in polyester dyeing; request lab dips before production begins

### 2. Pool Orders With Other Australian Retailers to Hit Factory MOQs

If your order volume cannot meet even mid-tier factory minimums on its own, the next strategy is to combine orders with other Australian retailers.

Pooling works like this: two to four independent sports retailers agree on a shared production run from the same factory. Each retailer submits their own designs and quantities — typically 200 to 400 pieces each — which are combined into a single manufacturing batch that meets the factory's overall MOQ.

**How Australian retailers are making this work in 2026:**

- A Sydney-based consortium of four family-run sports stores pooled orders to source $78,000 worth of AFL merchandise across 12 designs for the 2026 season — each store paying roughly 25% of what they would have paid alone
- The pool operates through a shared shipping container from Ningbo to Port Botany, further reducing per-unit freight costs from $2.80 to $0.85
- Each retailer maintains their own supplier relationship and quality standards; the pooling agreement covers only the production run and container sharing

The logistics require coordination — someone needs to manage the consolidated shipment and distribute stock on arrival — but the margin improvement typically justifies the effort within a single AFL season.

The blanks arrive in Australia, and you work with a local screen printer or embroidery shop to add team designs on demand.

## Quality Control: The Factory Vetting Checklist That Protects Your Investment

According to the ACCC, product quality disputes cost Australian importers an estimated $47 million annually. When importing AFL merchandise from China, a structured quality control process is not optional — it is the difference between a profitable season and a warehouse full of unsellable stock.

### Pre-Production: Get It Right Before a Single Stitch Is Made

1. **Request lab dips for all team colours.** Melbourne's navy blue and GWS's charcoal grey are specific shades. A factory's "navy" may not match the club's official Pantone. Get colour swatches approved before production starts.
2. **Confirm fabric weight via GSM certification.** Supporter scarves should be 12-gauge knit at minimum; replica jerseys need 220-260 GSM polyester interlock. Ask for a third-party lab report, not just the factory's word.
3. **Verify embroidery file compatibility.** Chinese factories typically work with .dst or .emb file formats. If your logo files are in .ai or .eps, confirm the factory can convert them accurately — and request a stitch-out sample.

### During Production: Stay Visible

- Request mid-production photos at the 30% and 70% completion marks. Reputable factories expect this request and will accommodate it.
- For orders above $5,000 AUD, engage a third-party inspection service such as SGS, Bureau Veritas, or QIMA. The cost — typically $300 to $500 per inspection — is cheap insurance against a shipment of 1,000 scarves with misaligned logos.
- Inspect a random sample of 10-15% of the batch for stitching consistency, colour accuracy, and sizing before authorising final payment.

A Brisbane-based sports merchandise business we worked with in late 2025 learned this lesson the hard way. A shipment of 2,000 supporter caps arrived with logos printed 8mm off-centre — unnoticeable in a sample photo, obvious in a retail display. The $4,200 return freight cost plus the lost season of sales could have been avoided with a $400 pre-shipment inspection.

### Import Compliance for Textile Merchandise

All textile imports into Australia valued above $1,000 AUD require a completed Import Declaration (N10 form) lodged through the Integrated Cargo System (ICS). Key compliance points for AFL merchandise:

- **Tariff classification:** Most textile scarves and caps fall under Chapter 61 or 65 of the Customs Tariff Act, attracting a 5% duty rate
- **Country of origin labelling:** The Competition and Consumer (Country of Origin) Regulations require clear labelling if you plan to resell the merchandise as "Australian designed" or similar
- **ACCC mandatory standards:** If your merchandise range includes children's sizes, you must comply with ACCC mandatory safety standards for children's nightwear and clothing — specifically around flammability and drawstring requirements

Working with a licensed customs broker for your first few shipments is strongly recommended. The cost (typically $150 to $300 per entry) is modest compared to the penalties for incorrect classification or non-compliant labelling.

### Are there restrictions on importing team-branded AFL merchandise from China?

Yes — and this is where many first-time importers get into legal trouble. Officially licensed AFL merchandise requires specific authorisation from the AFL's licensing department. Generic merchandise in team colours (navy scarf, charcoal beanie, etc.) without club logos or trademarked terms does not require licensing but also commands lower retail pricing. Counterfeit merchandise — replicas using unauthorised trademarks — carries significant legal and financial penalties under Australian intellectual property law. We strongly recommend working with a licensing consultant or the AFL's commercial partnerships team before proceeding.
