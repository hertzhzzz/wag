## Step 2: Find Chinese Manufacturers Who Understand Cricket Merchandise

Not all sportswear factories in China can produce cricket merchandise well. The specific requirements matter:

**Colour fastness:** Cricket merchandise is worn outdoors in Australian summer sun. Cheap sublimation prints on polyester jerseys fade after one wash in UV-intensive conditions. Request UV-fade-resistance testing from your factory — reputable sportswear manufacturers in Guangdong and Fujian can provide ISO 105-B02 test results for colour fastness to light.

**Scarf knitting quality:** Chinese factories in the Zhangjiagang area of Jiangsu province specialise in knit scarves. The difference between a quality scarf and a poor one comes down to yarn weight (minimum 180 GSM for a scarf that feels substantial), edge finishing (reinforced stitching, not raw cuts), and print registration (text that sits straight, not slanted).

**Flag construction:** Avoid printed polyester flags that are single-layer with hemmed edges — they look cheap and fray quickly. Specify double-layer knitted polyester with reinforced pole sleeve and brass eyelets at minimum. Factories in Shaoxing (Zhejiang) and Yiwu produce flags at scale and can match the Pantone colours of both the Australian and Pakistani flags precisely.

## Step 3: Navigate Intellectual Property the Right Way

This is the most commonly misunderstood aspect of bilateral series merchandise. Here is what you need to know for the Australian market.

**What is protected:**
- Cricket Australia's official logo, the Southern Cross stars mark, and the baggy green cap design are registered trademarks
- The Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) logo and official team crest are registered trademarks
- Player names, likenesses, and signature marks may be protected under Australian passing-off law
- Official playing jersey designs are protected by design registration or copyright

**What is not protected:**
- The colours green and gold (Australia's sporting colours) used generically
- The colour green used generically (Pakistan's cricket colour)
- The words "Australia" and "Pakistan" in text (they are geographical names)
- Cricket-themed imagery (bats, balls, stumps, wickets) used in original designs
- The year "2026" and the text "AUS vs PAK" as a match descriptor

**The recommended approach for Australian SMEs** is the colour-association design strategy — the same approach used successfully by retailers in the IPL merchandise market and described in our [cricket equipment sourcing guide](/resources/cricket-sports-equipment-china-sourcing-guide). Products use team-adjacent colour schemes with cricket-themed original graphics, series descriptors in plain text, and supporter-focused messaging. No official logos. No player images. No registered trademarks. This approach is legally sound, commercially proven, and requires zero licensing negotiations.

A Melbourne-based online cricket store that adopted this strategy for the 2025 England vs Australia Ashes series sold 1,200 units of colour-associated supporter merchandise across 6 product categories — generating approximately $38,000 in revenue with no licensing costs and no legal issues.

## FAQ

### Is it legal to sell AUS vs PAK cricket merchandise without a Cricket Australia or PCB license?

Yes — provided you do not use registered trademarks, official logos, player images, or official team crests. Cricket Australia's trademarks cover its specific logo marks, the baggy green cap design, and the playing jersey designs. The words "Australia" and "Pakistan" are geographical descriptors, and team colours alone are not trademarkable. The colour-association approach — merchandise in team colours with original cricket-themed graphics and supporter messaging — has been used successfully by Australian retailers for years without legal challenge. If you plan to scale beyond $50,000 in annual cricket merchandise revenue, consult a trademark attorney to review your specific designs.

### What is the minimum investment to test this market?

A minimum viable first order of 200 supporter jerseys, 100 scarves, and 100 flag pairs costs approximately $2,800–$3,500 AUD landed from a Chinese factory. At conservative sell-through rates and pricing, this generates $8,000–$12,000 in retail revenue. Start with one or two product categories in one colour scheme, test during the first match of the series, and scale up based on actual sales data. The key risk is unsold inventory after the series ends — order conservatively for your first series and grow your order volume as you learn your sell-through rates.

### Which Chinese regions produce the best cricket fan merchandise?

For sublimated sportswear (jerseys, t-shirts): Guangdong province, specifically the Dongguan and Guangzhou textile clusters. For knitted scarves: Zhangjiagang in Jiangsu province. For flags and promotional accessories: Yiwu in Zhejiang province — the world's largest wholesale market for small commodities, where flag MOQs can be as low as 100 units. For bucket hats and headwear: the Yangzhou area in Jiangsu has specialised hat factories that can handle custom embroidery and printing. Request samples from factories in each region before committing to a bulk order — price and quality vary significantly even within the same industrial cluster.

### How do I handle customs classification for cricket merchandise?

Cricket supporter jerseys fall under tariff heading 6110.30 (knitted synthetic fibre garments) at 5% duty. Knitted scarves are classified under 6117.10 at 5% duty. Printed flags fall under 6307.90 (other made-up textile articles) at 5% duty. Bucket hats are classified under 6505.00 (headgear) at 5% duty. All imports attract 10% GST on the landed value. Ensure your Chinese factory labels commercial invoices with the correct HS codes — a misclassified shipment can add 5–10 days in customs processing. If your total order value exceeds $1,000 AUD, you will need a customs broker or to self-assess using the Integrated Cargo System (ICS).

### What is the best single product to start with if I am testing the market?

Start with scarves. They are the lowest manufacturing complexity (knitted text + two-colour pattern), the lowest per-unit cost ($3.50–$5.50 FOB), and the easiest to sell at cricket grounds where fans actively look for match-day souvenirs. A scarf with "AUSTRALIA vs PAKISTAN 2026" in block text with green-and-gold on one end and green on the other is a simple design that factories can produce to a consistent quality standard. Scarf MOQs start at 200–300 units, keeping your first-order investment below $1,200 landed.
