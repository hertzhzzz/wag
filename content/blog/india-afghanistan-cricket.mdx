Most bilateral cricket merchandise guides focus on established rivalries: Australia vs England, India vs Pakistan, Australia vs India. ## Product Quality Standards for Cricket Fan Gear

Cricket merchandise sold to Australian consumers must meet a quality threshold that Chinese factories do not automatically achieve. Specifying standards in your purchase order prevents the most common quality failures.

**Colour fastness.** ISO 105-B02 Grade 4+ is the minimum for cricket jerseys and caps that will be worn outdoors in Australian summer conditions. India blue that fades to pale blue after eight washes, or Afghanistan red that bleeds onto white trim after rain exposure, generates returns and negative reviews. Request a colour fastness test report for the specific fabric-and-dye combination before bulk production.

**Sublimation registration.** Fine details — the India tricolor on collars and cuffs, Afghanistan's vertical stripe pattern, player names and numbers — require multi-colour sublimation with tight registration. A useful quality check is to request a pre-production sample featuring the most complex design element (typically the tricolor stripe detail at 2-3mm width) and inspect it under magnification for colour bleeding between adjacent stripes.

**Seam construction.** Cricket jerseys worn outdoors, washed frequently, and subjected to Australian UV levels need reinforced flatlock stitching at shoulder seams and side panels. A single-needle overlock stitch — the factory default for budget sportswear — separates within 12-15 wash cycles. Specify flatlock stitching in the purchase order.

**Embroidery density.** Embroidered motifs — tiger or lion graphics for team supporter caps, cricket bat-and-ball icons, text elements — need minimum stitch counts. A 6cm x 4cm embroidered "INDIA" text element requires minimum 8,000 stitches. A more complex lion or tiger motif at 6cm x 8cm requires 12,000+ stitches. Below these thresholds, embroidery reads as sparse and low-quality at arm's length.

## FAQ

### Do I need a BCCI or ACB license to sell India or Afghanistan cricket merchandise in Australia?

You need a licensing agreement to use official team crests, the BCCI logo, the Afghanistan Cricket Board logo, player names combined with images, or exact replica designs of official playing jerseys. For small to medium Australian retailers, the practical and legally sound approach is colour-association merchandise: supplier jerseys in India blue and orange or Afghanistan red, green, and black, with original cricket-themed graphics and supporter-oriented text. The national colours are not trademarkable. The country name is a geographical descriptor. Original artwork is protected as your own intellectual property. If your annual turnover in a single national-team product category exceeds $50,000, consult a trademark attorney to review your specific designs against the relevant registered marks.
