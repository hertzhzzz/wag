## 2. Adelaide Crows Tricolor Production: What Chinese Factories Routinely Get Wrong

The Adelaide Crows colour scheme — navy blue, red, and gold — is one of the most technically demanding in the AFL. Over twelve site visits to Chinese textile factories in 2025 and 2026, WAG has observed a consistent pattern: approximately 60% of first-run samples from new factories fail on Crows gold tone accuracy.

### The Gold Problem

Chinese dye houses default to what they call "sport gold" — a bright, almost yellow-gold used in Chinese school uniforms. Adelaide Crows gold is a deeper, warmer tone: Pantone 1235 C (in printed applications) or matched to the official club PMS specification. Most Chinese textile mills do not stock this specific dye formulation.

**The fix:** Before placing any Adelaide Crows-themed order, four things must happen:

1. **Provide Pantone codes in your purchase order**, not just colour descriptions. The phrase "navy, red, and gold" will be interpreted as "dark blue, bright red, and bright yellow" by a factory that has never produced AFL merchandise.
2. **Request a lab dip for every colour** before production begins. A lab dip is a small dyed fabric sample that the factory produces for your approval. It costs nothing and prevents a container of gold-coloured scarves arriving in Port Adelaide when you ordered Crows gold.
3. **Specify lightfastness rating of ISO 105-B02 Grade 4+** for all Crows colours. Outdoor stadium merchandise fades fast under Australian sun. The gold pigment is particularly susceptible — it can shift to mustard within three washes if the factory uses cheap reactive dyes.
4. **Approve physical samples, not photographs.** Camera white balance and screen calibration make digital colour approval unreliable for tricolor schemes. Have samples couriered to Adelaide. The $60 DHL cost is cheaper than a $4,200 return freight bill.

### Embroidery Density for the Crow Logo

The Crows' stylised crow head logo requires higher stitch density than most AFL club logos. Where a basic Carlton monogram might need 8,000-10,000 stitches, a correctly executed Crows logo demands 14,000-18,000 stitches for the level of detail fans expect. Factories that underbid on embroidery density produce logos that look "patchy" at close range — a complaint that surfaces repeatedly in Australian retailer quality disputes.

| Embroidery Spec | Minimum Requirement | Common Factory Default |
|-----------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| Stitch count (crow logo) | 14,000+ stitches | 8,000-10,000 stitches |
| Thread colours | 8-colour minimum | 6-colour default |
| Backing material | Tear-away (caps), cut-away (jerseys) | Tear-away for everything |
| Underlay stitching | Required for caps | Often skipped |

Specify stitch count and backing type in your technical pack. Better yet, send the factory a physical sample of an officially licensed Crows cap and say: "Match this embroidery quality."

## 3. Geelong Cats Horizontal Hoops: Why They Are Harder to Manufacture Than They Look

Geelong's iconic navy-and-white hoops appear simple. They are not. As we examined in our [Carlton vs Geelong AFL merchandise sourcing guide](/resources/carlton-vs-geelong-afl-merchandise-sourcing-guide), horizontal hoop designs create three manufacturing problems that vertical stripes — like Adelaide's tri-panel guernsey — avoid entirely.

### The Registration Problem

In cut-and-sew garment production, hoop patterns must align perfectly across side seams. A 2mm misalignment at the seam creates a visible "step" in the hoop pattern that is immediately obvious to anyone wearing or looking at the garment. Vertical stripes, by contrast, run parallel to side seams and are far more forgiving.

A Port Adelaide-based apparel brand learned this the hard way in 2024. A shipment of 800 Geelong-style hooped supporter jerseys arrived with seam misalignment on 22% of units. The factory had not used a registration mark system during cutting. The cost: $6,800 in rejected stock and a three-week production delay for the remake.

### Hoop Width Consistency

Geelong's hoops are not all equal width. The club's official guernsey features alternating navy and white bands at specific ratios. Chinese factories accustomed to producing generic striped fabric will default to equal-width bands unless you provide an explicit hoop spacing diagram.

**Specify in your order:**
- Exact width of each navy hoop (typically 45-50mm on adult guernseys)
- Exact width of each white hoop
- Number of complete hoops visible on the front panel
- Whether hoops wrap continuously around the body or stop at side seams

### How do I ensure Adelaide Crows gold matches across different product types?

Gold inconsistency across product categories is the most common quality complaint in AFL merchandise. The solution is to specify the same Pantone code across all product types (scarves, caps, jerseys, flags) and require the factory to produce a unified lab dip showing the gold tone on all fabric substrates before production begins. Different fabrics take dye differently, so seeing them side by side is essential.

### Is it legal to produce Adelaide Crows and Geelong Cats merchandise in China without an AFL license?

Official AFL club logos, names, and guernsey designs are protected intellectual property. Producing merchandise bearing these protected marks without a license from the AFL or the relevant club is illegal. However, producing merchandise that references the rivalry in generic terms — such as navy/red/gold colour schemes without logos, "Adelaide vs Geelong" text in non-trademarked fonts, or original fan designs — is legally permissible. Consult an IP lawyer before commissioning any production.

For sea freight, place your order 8-10 weeks before the match date. For air freight, order 4-5 weeks before.
