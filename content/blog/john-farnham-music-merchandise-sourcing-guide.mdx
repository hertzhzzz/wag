With a career spanning six decades, multiple ARIA Awards, and the highest-selling Australian album of all time with "Whispering Jack," Farnham's cultural significance cannot be overstated. ## Intellectual Property Considerations

### Understanding Artist Rights

Licensing is the central legal consideration in music merchandise. Every artist's name, image, likeness, song lyrics, and album artwork are protected intellectual property. For John Farnham, these rights are managed by the artist's management team and record label, with licensing arrangements that control how his brand can be used on commercial products.

The most straightforward path for merchandise related to the tribute concert is through official licensing agreements with the event promoters or Farnham's representatives. However, for many small and medium businesses, the cost and complexity of official licensing may be prohibitive.

### Alternatives to Official Licensing

Several legitimate approaches exist for businesses that want to participate in the music memorabilia market without carrying official artist licences. These include:

Original artwork inspired by Australian music that references musical themes without using specific artist trademarks. Retro music-themed apparel celebrating Australian music of the 1980s and 1990s as a cultural phenomenon. Concert and live music culture products that celebrate the experience of attending live performances. Music-inspired design work that draws on musical motifs rather than specific artist branding.

When in doubt, the safest approach is to seek legal advice from an Australian solicitor specialising in intellectual property law before committing to production. The costs of addressing an infringement claim far exceed the cost of legal guidance at the planning stage.

### Licensed Merchandise Opportunities

For businesses prepared to pursue the licensing route, the required steps include identifying the correct rights holder for the specific product category, preparing a merchandise proposal with product designs and quality specifications, negotiating licence terms including royalty rates and territory, executing a formal licensing agreement with quality control provisions, and producing and selling under the terms of the agreement.

Licensing for artist merchandise in Australia typically involves royalty rates of 8-15% of wholesale revenue, with minimum guarantee requirements that vary based on product category and exclusivity.

## Sourcing from Chinese Manufacturers

### Apparel Manufacturing in China

China's apparel manufacturing sector remains the most cost-effective source for music merchandise, particularly for the decorated apparel categories that dominate concert merchandise. Key manufacturing regions include Guangdong province, particularly Guangzhou and Dongguan, for the broadest range of apparel manufacturing capabilities. Zhejiang province for accessories and printed products. Fujian province for sportswear and fleece products suitable for hoodies and outerwear.

Chinese manufacturers have extensive experience with music merchandise decoration techniques including screen printing for high-volume t-shirt production (most cost-effective for runs over 500 pieces), direct-to-garment (DTG) digital printing for smaller runs with complex artwork, embroidery for caps and premium apparel items, and sublimation for all-over print designs on polyester garments.

### Quality Considerations for Music Merchandise

Music merchandise sold in Australia must meet the same quality standards as any apparel product. Key quality factors when sourcing from China include:

Fabric quality that does not pill, shrink excessively, or lose colour after washing. Print quality that does not crack, peel, or fade within the first 6-12 months of normal wear. Size consistency that matches Australian measurement expectations. Care instructions that are accurate and labelled correctly in English.

Pre-production sampling and third-party quality inspection before shipment are strongly recommended for any apparel merchandise order.

### Beyond the Event Window

While concerts create demand spikes, music merchandise can generate year-round revenue through online sales channels. Australian businesses should plan for post-event inventory management:

Pre-sell merchandise online before the event to gauge demand and reduce inventory risk. Hold back 20-30% of inventory for post-event sales through online channels. Consider limited edition runs with numbered certificates that maintain value after the event. Develop evergreen designs that can sell year-round rather than event-specific items.

## Building a Music Merchandise Business

### Sourcing Partners and Relationships

Long-term success in music merchandise sourcing depends on building strong relationships with Chinese manufacturing partners. The most effective approach involves consolidating production with a small number of trusted factories rather than spreading orders across many suppliers. This allows for better pricing, priority production slots, and more responsive quality management.

Many Australian music merchandise businesses work through sourcing agents based in China who manage factory relationships, quality control, and logistics on their behalf. For businesses new to China sourcing, this approach reduces the learning curve and common import mistakes.

### Distribution Channels

Music merchandise reaches customers through multiple channels, each with distinct advantages. Online stores offer the highest margins and direct customer relationships with national reach, but require investment in marketing and customer acquisition. Concert venue stalls provide high-traffic sales opportunities with event-driven demand, but involve logistic complexity and venue fees. Wholesale supply to record stores and gift shops creates steady distribution with lower marketing costs, but at reduced margins. Market stalls at music festivals and events provide direct customer interaction with seasonal timing.

Most successful music merchandise businesses operate multiple channels, using events for customer acquisition and online for ongoing sales.

## FAQ

### Do I need a licence to sell John Farnham merchandise?

If your products use John Farnham's name, image, likeness, or references specific song lyrics and album artwork, you need authorisation from the rights holder. Products that celebrate Australian music in a general sense without specifically referencing Farnham do not require licensing, but the line between inspiration and infringement can be unclear. Legal advice is recommended before committing to production.

### What are the most profitable categories in music merchandise?

T-shirts offer the strongest combination of high demand, low production cost, and premium retail pricing, delivering margins of 70-80%. Headwear and accessories offer similar margins but lower per-unit revenue. Limited edition collectibles can achieve extremely high margins but carry higher inventory risk if demand is overestimated.

### What minimum order quantities do Chinese manufacturers require for music merchandise?

T-shirt MOQs typically start at 200-500 pieces per design. Hoodies and outerwear require 300-500 pieces. Accessories like caps, pins, and patches can be available at 100-300 pieces. Small collectibles like buttons or stickers can be produced in quantities as low as 100-200 pieces. Trading companies can sometimes offer lower minimums by consolidating orders but at higher per-unit costs.

### How long does it take to produce music merchandise in China?

Standard production runs 30-45 days from order confirmation, covering fabric procurement, cutting and sewing, decoration, quality inspection, and packing. Sample production adds 10-14 days. Sea freight to Australia takes 15-25 days. Air freight reduces shipping to 3-7 days. Total lead time from order to delivery is typically 8-12 weeks by sea or 6-8 weeks by air.

### Can I print music lyrics on merchandise without a licence?

Song lyrics are protected by copyright, and printing them on merchandise without a licence generally constitutes infringement. The copyright owner (typically the songwriter or publisher) controls the right to reproduce lyrics on commercial products. Fair dealing exceptions for criticism or review are very narrow and unlikely to cover commercial merchandise.

### What is the best way to test the market for music merchandise?

Start with a small initial order of 200-300 t-shirts across a few design options. Use pre-sale campaigns to validate demand before committing to full production. Test at a single event or concert before expanding to multi-channel distribution. Consider print-on-demand for initial market testing before investing in full production runs.

### How do Australian consumer laws apply to music merchandise?

Music merchandise sold in Australia must comply with Australian Consumer Law, including requirements for acceptable quality matching the price paid, accurate product descriptions and sizing information, clear returns and refund policies, and country of origin labelling. These requirements apply regardless of where the product was manufactured. The retailer is responsible for compliance, not the manufacturer.

### What are the Australian customs classification and duty rates for music merchandise?

Most music merchandise falls under textile and apparel HS codes, with duty rates ranging from 5-10% depending on the specific product category and fabric composition. Cotton t-shirts typically attract 5% duty, while synthetic fibre products may be higher. GST of 10% applies to the total landed cost including duty. Correct classification is important as incorrect codes can lead to customs penalties.
