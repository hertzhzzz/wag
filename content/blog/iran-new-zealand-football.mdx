### 1. Team Melli Supporter Jerseys and Apparel

Iran's national team is called Team Melli — Persian for "The National Team" — and its visual identity is built around the tricolour of the Iranian flag: green, white, and red. The home kit is traditionally white with red and green accents. The away kit is typically red. This colour palette is distinctive in international football — no other major football nation uses the green-white-red combination as its primary identity — and it creates visually recognisable merchandise that stands out in any retail display.

**The Colour Specification: Getting the Tricolour Right**

The Iranian flag's colours are constitutionally defined: green (Pantone approximately 355 C to 356 C), white, and red (Pantone approximately 186 C to 199 C). For Team Melli kit reproductions and supporter merchandise, the white base is the dominant field, with red and green appearing as accent stripes, collar details, and graphic elements. Getting the specific shades right is essential — Iranian-Australian buyers will immediately recognise incorrect colour values.

Most Chinese sportswear factories in Dongguan and Guangzhou can match standard Pantone colours for sublimated polyester production. The key specification is a pre-production lab dip — a small dyed fabric swatch — that confirms the green and red accent colours match the expected range. Lab dips cost approximately USD 30-50 and eliminate the risk of a full production run arriving in the wrong shade.

**Design Boundaries: What You Can and Cannot Use**

The Iran Football Federation (IFF) crest — featuring a stylised football, the Iranian flag colours, and Persian text — is a registered trademark of the IFF and cannot be reproduced without licensing. The official kit design, produced through Iran's technical sponsorship arrangement (currently with Merooj, an Iranian sportswear brand), is also protected.

What Australian retailers can legally produce without licensing:

- White supporter jerseys with green and red accent striping in the tricolour pattern
- "IRAN" text in original block or stylised typography (not the IFF's registered wordmark)
- "TEAM MELLI" text — while commonly used by Iranian football fans, this phrase in original typography is generally not trademark-restricted for commercial use (confirm with a trademark attorney for your specific design)
- Persian-inspired geometric patterns in green, white, and red
- Match-specific text: "Iran vs New Zealand 2026"
- Original cheetah-inspired graphics — Iran's football culture associates the Asiatic cheetah with the national team, and original artistic interpretations of this motif are available for supporter merchandise

Minimum 160 GSM sublimated polyester. Full sublimation printing — where dye penetrates the polyester fibres rather than sitting on the surface — is essential for white-base jerseys with accent colours, because screen-printed colour on white fabric will degrade visibly after 20-plus washes. Sublimation ensures the white remains crisp and the green and red accents remain colour-fast through extended wear.

## Key Quality Specifications for Football Fan Merchandise from China

Not all Chinese factories produce football merchandise to the standard Australian consumers expect. These specifications separate premium merchandise from the returns-and-negative-reviews category.

**Fabric weight:** Supporter jerseys must be at least 160 GSM. Below 140 GSM, the fabric is translucent and feels cheap against the skin. The difference between 140 GSM and 160 GSM is approximately USD 0.50-0.80 per unit — the single highest-return quality investment available. White jerseys for New Zealand supporters are particularly unforgiving of thin fabric; anything below 160 GSM on a white jersey will be visibly see-through in Australian daylight.

**Colour fastness:** Request a wash test as part of the pre-production sample process. The green and red accent colours on Team Melli merchandise carry the highest risk of dye migration — red dye bleeding into white fabric during washing is a common factory defect that is entirely preventable with proper dye fixation. The sample should be washed three times at 40 degrees Celsius and inspected for colour bleeding and fading. Factories in Dongguan and Guangzhou routinely perform wash testing as part of their quality control process for international orders.

**Seam reinforcement:** Knit scarves require reinforced edge stitching — hemmed and double-stitched to prevent unravelling after repeated use. Flags require double-stitched hems and reinforced pole pockets. The per-unit cost difference for these specifications is negligible (USD 0.10-0.20) and the customer experience difference is significant.

**Print durability for sublimated jerseys:** Full-sublimation printing — dye infused into the polyester fibres — extends print life from approximately 20 washes (screen print) to 50-plus washes. For premium-priced supporter jerseys at AUD 49.95 and above, sublimation is not optional. For budget-tier products at AUD 34.95 or below, high-quality screen printing is acceptable if properly cured.

**Packaging:** Individual polybag packaging for each item adds approximately USD 0.05-0.15 per unit and protects merchandise from moisture damage during transit. For premium items, consider header cards with "Iran vs New Zealand 2026" branding printed in English. These add USD 0.20-0.50 per unit and support the premium retail positioning.
