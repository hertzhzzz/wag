### Quality Considerations for Football Merchandise

Australian consumers expect certain quality standards from football merchandise, and Korean-Australian customers in particular have high expectations given their familiarity with Korean-manufactured apparel and accessories. When sourcing from Chinese factories, pay attention to:

- **Fabric weight and feel**: Korean national team jerseys use lightweight polyester blends with specific moisture-wicking properties. Request GSM (grams per square metre) specifications in your RFQ and compare against reference products.
- **Print and embroidery quality**: Sublimation printing is the standard for football jerseys and produces durable, colourfast results. For caps and patches, ask for embroidery stitch count samples. Higher stitch counts produce crisper, more durable designs.
- **Colour accuracy**: Korea's national team red is a specific shade (Pantone 186 C, the same red used on the Korean flag). Provide Pantone references to your supplier to ensure colour matching across production runs.
- **Size grading**: Korean body proportions differ from standard Western sizing. When targeting the Korean-Australian community, consider requesting size runs that include smaller sizes (85-90 cm chest) alongside standard Australian sizes.

## FAQ: Son Heung-min Merchandise for Australian Retailers

### Is it legal to sell unofficial Son Heung-min merchandise in Australia?

Yes, provided the products do not use registered trademarks without authorisation. Products that reference Son by descriptive text ("Tottenham captain," "Korea No. 7") without using official club crests, national team badges, or manufacturer logos generally fall within acceptable commercial practice. Designs that incorporate original artwork, Korean-language slogans, or stylised number references present lower legal risk than products that attempt to replicate official licensed merchandise. We recommend consulting a legal professional experienced in intellectual property law for specific product designs.

### What is the minimum order quantity for football jerseys from Chinese factories?

Most Chinese factories that produce football jerseys require a minimum of 100 units per design for sublimated polyester shirts. Some suppliers in Guangdong and Zhejiang accept orders as low as 50 units for simple printed T-shirts or accessory items. Caps and scarves typically have minimums of 100-200 units. The key is matching the MOQ to the product category that matches your risk tolerance.

### How do shipping costs affect the profitability of low-MOQ football merchandise?

Air freight is the most practical shipping method for low-volume orders (under 500 kg). At current rates of approximately $8-$12 per kilogram from China to Australia, a 10 kg shipment of scarves or caps costs approximately $80-$120 in freight. For a product with an FOB cost of $2.00 and a retail price of $25, the freight cost adds approximately $1.00-$1.50 per unit, leaving adequate margin. Sea freight is more economical for larger orders but extends transit time to 20-30 days and requires order volumes that typically exceed 1,000 units per SKU.

### Which Son-related products sell best in the Australian market?

Based on data from Australian retailers who have tested football merchandise targeting Asian communities, the highest-selling categories are accessories (caps and scarves outsell apparel by approximately 3:1 in the first six months of a new product line), followed by Korea national team jerseys, and then casual T-shirts with Korean-language football designs. Scarves in particular show surprisingly high velocity, likely because they serve as low-cost, high-visibility identity markers for Korean-Australian fans attending matches or community events.

### What quality issues should I watch for when sourcing from Chinese factories?

The most common quality issues in Chinese-manufactured football merchandise are fabric colour variation between production runs, sublimation ink fading after repeated washing, loose embroidery threads on patches and crests, and inconsistent sizing across different production lots. Mitigate these risks by requesting pre-production samples before committing to full order quantities, specifying Pantone colour references in your purchase orders, and using a third-party quality inspection service for orders above $2,000.
