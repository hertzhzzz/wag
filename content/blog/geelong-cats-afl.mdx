## 2. The Navy-and-White Hoops — Why Horizontal Stripes Are the Hardest Design in the AFL to Manufacture

Geelong's iconic navy-and-white horizontal hoops appear deceptively simple. A set of alternating coloured bands wrapping around the torso. What could go wrong? As examined in our [Gold Coast vs Brisbane AFL QClash](/resources/gold-coast-brisbane-afl) guide — which covers colour-matching challenges for the Suns and Lions — the answer is: a great deal, and most of it is invisible until the finished garment arrives.

### The Registration Problem

In cut-and-sew garment production, horizontal hoop patterns must align perfectly across side seams. A 2-millimetre misalignment at the seam creates a visible "step" in the hoop pattern that is immediately obvious to the wearer. Vertical stripes — the dominant design in AFL guernseys — run parallel to side seams and are far more forgiving because the eye does not track across a seam perpendicular to the stripe direction.

The Port Adelaide-based apparel brand referenced in our West Coast vs Port Adelaide sourcing guide experienced this failure on 22 per cent of a shipment of 800 hooped supporter jerseys in 2024. The factory had not used a registration mark system during cutting. The cost was $6,800 in rejected stock.

### Production Specification Checklist for Geelong Hoops

| Manufacturing Requirement | Minimum Acceptable | Common Factory Default | Impact of Failure |
|-------------------------|-------------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Side seam hoop alignment | +/- 1mm registration | +/- 3mm or no registration | Visible step in hoop pattern |
| Navy hoop width (adult guernsey) | 48mm +/- 1mm | 50mm (equal-width default) | Wrong visual proportion |
| White hoop width (adult guernsey) | 22mm +/- 1mm | 25mm (equal-width default) | Wrong visual proportion |
| Hoop wrap at side seams | Continuous around body | Stop at side seam panel | Visible break under arm |
| Navy fabric dye spec | Pantone 282 C | Generic dark navy | Colour mismatch with official gear |
| Dye lightfastness | ISO 105-B02 Grade 4+ | Grade 3 | Fading within 6 months of sun exposure |

The key distinction: Geelong's hoops are not all equal width. The club's official guernsey features wider navy bands alternating with narrower white bands at a ratio of approximately 2.2:1. Chinese factories accustomed to producing generic hooped striped fabric will default to equal-width bands unless the purchase order explicitly specifies the navy-to-white width ratio.

### Navy Consistency Across Fabric Types

The navy used in Geelong Cats merchandise — approximately Pantone 282 C — must be consistent across different products: polyester guernseys, acrylic knit scarves, cotton-twill caps, and woven polyester flags. Different fabric substrates take dye differently. A navy that looks correct on a polyester guernsey may read as purple on an acrylic scarf or as green-black on cotton twill.

The solution is the same for any multi-product merchandise range: require the factory to produce a unified strike-off showing the navy colour on all intended fabric substrates before approving bulk production. This is standard practice for European football clubs that source from Chinese factories, but it is rarely requested by Australian retailers who are new to direct sourcing.

### Embroidery Requirements for the GFC Monogram and Cats Logo

The Geelong Cats branding system includes two primary marks: the classic GFC monogram (used on heritage and training merchandise) and the modern cat's head logo. Each requires different embroidery specifications.

The GFC monogram requires 8,000-10,000 stitches at a moderate density — achievable by most Chinese embroidery shops without special instruction. The cat's head logo, with its detailed facial features and whisker lines, requires 14,000-18,000 stitches and 8-10 thread colours. Factories that underbid on embroidery density produce cat logos that look "blurry" at close range because the stitch count is too low to render the fine detail.
