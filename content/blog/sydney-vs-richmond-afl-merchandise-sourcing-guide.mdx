## Sourcing from Chinese Factories

The good news: both Swans' red and white and Tigers' yellow and black are relatively simple colour schemes for Chinese manufacturers to match. This makes sourcing easier than for teams with complex multi-colour kits.

**Colour matching specifications to provide to your factory:**
- Swans red: Pantone 186C (a standard red that Chinese textile mills handle routinely)
- Swans white: Pantone 11-0601 (Brilliant White — specify optical brightener level)
- Tigers yellow: Pantone 109C (bright yellow — this can be tricky; request a lab dip)
- Tigers black: Pantone Black 6 (deepest black — easier, but ensure UV resistance)

A retailer in the Sydney CBD who stocks both Swans and Tigers merchandise orders combined production runs from a single factory in Guangdong. By splitting the MOQ across 4 SKUs (Swans scarf + Tigers scarf + Swans cap + Tigers cap), they meet the factory's 2,000-unit minimum while only needing 500 units per design.

**Factory discovery process:**
1. Search Alibaba for "AFL scarf manufacturer" or "sports scarf supplier China"
2. Filter for Gold Supplier status and 3+ years on platform
3. Send your spec sheet with Pantone references to 5–8 factories
4. Request samples from the top 3 based on response quality and pricing
5. Place a trial order of 200–300 units per SKU before committing to larger volumes

## When to Order Around the AFL Fixture

The AFL fixture is released in November. Sydney vs Richmond typically plays once or twice per season, usually between April and August (winter months when scarf and beanie demand is highest).

**Optimal timeline for winter match merchandise:**
```
November  → AFL draw released → identify match dates
December  → Factory shortlist + sample requests
January   → Sample evaluation + order placement
February  → Production (25–30 days)
March     → Sea freight departure
April     → Stock arrives → ready for match weeks
```

**If you missed the sea freight window:** Air freight takes 5–8 days from Guangzhou to Sydney. A top-up order of 300 scarves by air costs approximately $400–$600 in freight, compared to $100–$200 by sea. When the alternative is empty shelves during a match week that could generate $8,000–$15,000 in revenue, air freight is clearly worth it.

## FAQ

### Which sells better — Swans or Tigers merchandise?

Richmond has a slightly larger national fan base (estimated 1.2M vs Swans' 950K self-identified fans), but both sell well in their home markets. If your store is in Sydney, stock both equally; in Melbourne, lean 60:40 toward Tigers.

### Can I sell both teams' merchandise in one factory order?

Yes — combining both SKUs into a single production run helps meet factory MOQs. Most Chinese manufacturers accept split SKUs within a single order. A combined order of 500 Swans scarves + 500 Tigers scarves is often treated as 1,000 units for MOQ purposes.

### What's the minimum investment to start?

A combined first order of 500 Swans + 500 Tigers scarves costs approximately $2,500–$3,500 AUD landed. At $29.95 retail, the potential revenue is $29,950 — a 7–10x return on the initial investment.

## The Multi-Team Ordering Strategy

One of the most effective strategies for sourcing AFL merchandise from China is the "multi-team consolidate" approach. Instead of ordering one team's merchandise at a time, place a single order that covers 3–4 teams. Here's why this works:

**Scenario:** You need 500 Swans scarves (MOQ 1,000) and 500 Tigers scarves (MOQ 1,000). Individually, neither meets the factory minimum. Combined into one order of 1,000 scarves split across two designs, both qualify. The factory produces 500 of each, you pay the per-unit price for a 1,000-unit order, and both teams' merchandise hits your shelves.

A retailer in Melbourne who uses this strategy combines 4 AFL teams across caps, scarves, and flags in each production run, meeting factory MOQs while keeping individual team stock levels at 200–500 units per SKU.

## Testing Demand Before Committing to Large Orders

The smartest approach for first-time AFL merchandise importers is to test demand before placing large orders. Here's a low-risk strategy:

1. **Order 100–200 units per SKU via air freight** (3–5x higher freight cost but fast delivery and no minimum quantity issues)
2. **Sell at full retail for 2–3 match weeks** — if sell-through exceeds 80%, you have confirmed demand
3. **Place the main sea freight order** for the next match cycle at 40–60% lower landed cost
4. **Use the air freight stock as buffer inventory** for peak match weeks

A retailer in Melbourne's AFL heartland used this approach for their first Tigers order: 150 scarves by air ($2.40/unit freight premium), sold out in 9 days, then ordered 1,500 by sea for the following season at a per-unit cost 55% lower than the air freight batch.
