## 2. Black and White — Why the Simplest Colour Scheme Is the Hardest to Manufacture

Collingwood's iconic black-and-white stripes appear deceptively simple. Two colours. Vertical stripes. What could go wrong? As covered in our [Geelong Cats AFL](/resources/geelong-cats-afl) guide — which examines the horizontal hoop registration problem that makes Geelong's guernsey the hardest in the AFL to produce — the answer is: a great deal, and most of it is invisible until the finished garment arrives under Australian sunlight.

### The Black-Fading Problem

Black polyester fabric is among the most difficult textiles to dye to a lightfastness standard sufficient for Australian conditions. Under prolonged UV exposure — which is typical for a guernsey worn to outdoor MCG games, washed weekly during the season, and hung on a clothesline in the Australian sun — standard-grade black polyester fades to a charcoal grey within approximately 40 to 60 wash-and-wear cycles. For a Collingwood supporter who wears their guernsey to 14 home games and washes it after each, the garment reaches visible fade within a single season.

The solution is not more dye. It is better dye. Disperse dyes formulated to ISO 105-B02 Grade 5 or higher — what the textile industry calls "high-lightfastness black" — retain colour saturation through 80 to 100 wash-and-wear cycles. The cost difference is approximately $0.40 to $0.60 per metre of fabric, or roughly $0.50 to $0.80 per guernsey. Chinese factories that default to standard-grade black polyester will produce a guernsey that looks correct out of the box but fades within months. Specifying ISO 105-B02 Grade 5-plus black dye in the purchase order eliminates this failure mode.

### The White-Yellowing Problem

White polyester has the opposite problem: it does not fade, it yellows. White fabric yellowing is caused by a chemical reaction between the optical brightening agents used to achieve "optical white" and atmospheric pollutants — particularly nitrogen oxides from vehicle exhaust, which are present at higher concentrations in Melbourne than in most Australian cities due to the city's traffic density and geographic basin topography.

A Collingwood guernsey manufactured with standard optical-brightened white polyester and worn in Melbourne will show visible yellowing in the white stripes within approximately 8 to 12 months. The yellowing is chemically irreversible — it cannot be washed out — and it produces a cream-coloured stripe that reads as "dirty" against the black stripes, destroying the high-contrast look that defines Collingwood's visual identity.

The solution is to specify anti-phenolic yellowing treatment — a chemical finishing process applied to the white polyester fabric before cutting — and to test the finished fabric using the ISO 105-X18 phenolic yellowing test before bulk production. Chinese factories that do not routinely serve the European or North American sportswear market are often unaware of this requirement because the Asian domestic market accepts yellowing as normal wear.

"Magpies" as a standalone term, when not combined with protected club branding elements, may fall outside the scope of trademark protection in certain contexts. The exact legal boundaries depend on the specific product design, the jurisdiction, and the enforcement position of the AFL and the club.
