## 2. Finding a Factory That Understands AFL Specifications

Not all Chinese factories understand Australian rules football merchandise. The dimensions, fabric weights, and design requirements for AFL gear differ from American football or soccer products.

### What to look for in a factory

When vetting suppliers for AFL merchandise, confirm these five specifications first:

1. **Knit density for scarves** — AFL scarves need a minimum 12-gauge knit for the weight and warmth Australian fans expect in winter months
2. **Jersey fabric weight** — 220–260 GSM polyester interlock, the industry standard for player-quality replicas
3. **Embroidery capability** — 12-colour max thread count for club logos and AFL branding details
4. **Size grading** — Australian sizing (2XS–5XL) is distinct from Asian sizing charts — confirm this upfront
5. **Colour fastness certification** — ISO 105-B02 lightfastness rating of 4+ for outdoor wear

Request samples in navy and white. Carlton's navy blue and Geelong's white with blue hoops are notoriously difficult colours to get right in polyester dyeing. A factory that handles these well can handle any AFL colour scheme.

## 3. Managing Minimum Order Quantities (MOQs)

The single biggest barrier for Australian SMEs sourcing AFL merchandise is MOQs. Large factories typically require 1,000–3,000 pieces per design. For a small sports retailer stocking for a single rivalry match, that's commercial suicide.

Here are three ways Australian businesses can work around high MOQs:

### Shared container partnerships

Pool orders with 2–3 other Australian sports retailers to meet factory MOQs. Each business gets 300–500 pieces of their own design at a shared manufacturing run. A Sydney-based consortium of 4 family-run sports stores used this model to source $78,000 worth of AFL merchandise across 12 designs — each store paying 25% of what they would have alone.

### Multi-design runs

Some Chinese factories allow combining multiple SKUs (different team designs, same product type) into a single production run. Instead of 1,000 Carlton scarves, you order 300 Carlton + 350 Geelong + 350 Collingwood scarves as one batch. This works best with factories that use digital printing rather than traditional screen printing for logo application.

### Blank stock with local customisation

Order blank scarves, caps, or flags from China (cheaper, no minimum on unbranded items), then have a local Australian screen printer add team logos. One Adelaide retailer used this strategy to reduce per-unit cost on supporter flags from $9.50 to $3.20 while maintaining MOQ of just 200 pieces.

## 4. Quality Control: The Checklist That Saves You Money

According to the ACCC, product quality disputes cost Australian importers an estimated $47 million annually. When sourcing AFL merchandise from China, a structured quality control process is your best defence.

### Pre-production checks

- Request lab dips for all team colours before production starts
- Confirm fabric weight via GSM testing (use a local testing lab or ask for third-party certification)
- Check embroidery file compatibility — Chinese factories typically use .dst or .emb formats

### During production

- Request mid-production photos — factories are accustomed to this
- Use a third-party inspection service (SGS, Bureau Veritas, or QIMA) for orders above $5,000 AUD
- Inspect 10–15% of the batch for stitching quality, colour consistency, and sizing accuracy

### Final inspection before shipment

A Brisbane-based sports merchandise business we worked with implemented this checklist after receiving a shipment of 2,000 supporter caps with misaligned logos. Pre-shipment inspection would have caught the issue before it left Shenzhen, saving $4,200 in return freight.
