### Intellectual Property Considerations

This is the most important section for anyone sourcing international football merchandise. You must understand what you can and cannot produce.

You can produce: merchandise in national team colours using the country names, flag colour sequences (stripes, blocks), and match-specific text like "FRANCE vs COTE D'IVOIRE 2026." Country names and flag colour arrangements are not trademarked — they are national symbols in the public domain.

You cannot produce: merchandise featuring the official French Football Federation (FFF) crest, the Federation Ivoirienne de Football (FIF) logo, player names, player images, official team jersey designs, or the FIFA logo. These are registered trademarks.

The rule is straightforward: if your merchandise uses colours and country names to show supporter affiliation, it is legal fan merchandise. If it replicates official federation branding, it is a trademark violation. ### Sea Freight vs Air Freight: The Cost-Speed Tradeoff

For a typical community-level order of 1,000–2,000 units (scarves, flags, keyrings), the shipping cost difference is significant:

| Shipping Method | Transit Time | Estimated Cost (1,500 units) | When to Use |

Sea freight is the standard choice when production finishes 6+ weeks before match day. Air freight becomes necessary when timelines compress — a common scenario with international friendlies that receive late confirmation. The air freight premium of $1,300–$1,800 reduces overall margins but preserves the sales window. Factor this contingency into your pricing model.

### Customs Classification for Football Merchandise

Correct tariff classification ensures smooth customs clearance. Football supporter scarves (knitted acrylic) fall under HS heading 6117.10 at 5% duty. Supporter jerseys (synthetic fibre, sublimated) are classified under 6110.30 at 5% duty. Printed flags are classified under 6307.90 at 5% duty. All imports attract 10% GST on the landed value (FOB cost + freight + duty).

Ensure your Chinese factory labels commercial invoices with the correct HS codes. A misclassified shipment can add 5–10 business days to customs processing and may incur inspection fees. If your total order value exceeds $1,000 AUD, you will need either a licensed customs broker or to self-assess using the Integrated Cargo System (ICS). For community clubs sourcing their first order, engaging a customs broker for approximately $150–$250 is the lower-risk approach.
