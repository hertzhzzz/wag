## Colour Matching: Getting the Island Colours Right

Colour accuracy is critical for cricket merchandise serving diaspora communities. Unlike generic sportswear where slight colour shifts are acceptable, Sri Lankan and West Indies fans are highly sensitive to accurate team colours. An off-shade blue reads as "not Sri Lanka" and a maroon that leans burgundy rather than crimson loses the Windies identity.

### Sri Lanka Blue

Sri Lanka's cricket blue is a distinctive mid-blue with a slight purple undertone, distinct from the lighter blues of teams like India or the darker navy of Australia. The closest Pantone reference is Pantone 301C, but Sri Lankan cricket colours have shifted slightly over the years — the current ODI kit uses a custom blue close to Pantone 294C.

**Sourcing requirement:** Request two lab dips from your Chinese textile factory — one at Pantone 301C and one at Pantone 294C. Compare both against reference images of the current Sri Lanka playing kit. A factory in Shaoxing that produces sublimated sportswear for Southeast Asian markets will typically have experience matching these colours. The lab dip cost of $20–$35 per colour is trivial compared to the cost of receiving 500 supporter jerseys in the wrong shade of blue.

### West Indies Maroon

West Indies maroon — Pantone 209C — is a deep, warm crimson with brown undertones. It is one of the most recognisable team colours in world cricket and manufacturing it accurately requires specific dye formulations.

Chinese textile factories commonly produce maroon as a general colour, but general maroon tends toward a cooler, more purple shade than the warm Pantone 209C that identifies West Indies cricket. When sourcing Windies merchandise, specify Pantone 209C explicitly and request a physical lab dip. Do not rely on digital colour references — monitor calibration differences between your screen and the factory's screen can produce significant colour mismatches.

A Perth-based retailer who imported West Indies merchandise for the 2025 series received 350 "maroon" t-shirts that were closer to burgundy. The factory had interpreted "maroon" as the standard Chinese textile industry colour (approximately Pantone 7421C) rather than the West Indies-specific Pantone 209C. The retailer had to discount the entire batch at 30% off to clear inventory — a $2,100 lesson in colour specification.
