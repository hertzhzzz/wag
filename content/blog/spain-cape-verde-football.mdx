## Avoiding Common Pitfalls

### Intellectual Property: What You Can and Cannot Print

National team crests and federation logos are protected trademarks. You cannot legally reproduce the RFEF crest (the crowned shield with football and the letters "F-F-E"), the Federcao Cabo-verdiana de Futebol (FCF) logo, or the official adidas Spain kit design.

**What you can produce legally:**

- Designs using national flag colours and motifs (public domain)
- Match-specific typography: "Spain vs Cape Verde World Cup 2026"
- Original football imagery that does not reproduce protected marks
- Generic national identifiers: "ESPANA", "La Roja", "Blue Sharks", "Cabo Verde"
- The ten-star circle motif as an original interpretation of the Cape Verdean flag (not a direct flag reproduction)

**Critical precaution.** Before submitting designs to a Chinese factory, have them reviewed for trademark risk. A single customs seizure of trademark-infringing merchandise can destroy the entire profit margin of a production run. Chinese manufacturers are generally familiar with IP boundaries for major European national teams, but always communicate your requirements explicitly in writing.

### Quality Control Must-Checks

Request a pre-production sample and inspect for these defects before approving bulk production for Spain vs Cape Verde football merchandise:

- **Colour bleeding in split designs**: Red-to-blue transition zones on half-and-half scarves where threads migrate across the colour boundary
- **Shade mismatch on Spain red**: Red that skews orange or pink rather than the deep crimson of La Roja
- **Star count errors on Cape Verde designs**: Nine stars instead of ten, or misaligned star arrangement
- **Uneven knitting density**: Thin patches visible when the scarf is held up to light
- **Print registration errors**: Misaligned text or flag graphics on printed t-shirts and caps

A third-party quality inspection before shipment costs USD 150-300 and is the cheapest insurance available for an import order.

### Tariff and ChAFTA Considerations

As of June 2026, textile imports into Australia attract a 5% customs duty under Chapter 62 of the Customs Tariff Act. The China-Australia Free Trade Agreement (ChAFTA) allows for preferential duty rates — potentially reducing or eliminating this 5% — if your supplier provides a valid Certificate of Origin. Always request this document. It takes a Chinese factory 15 minutes to prepare and can save hundreds of dollars on a single shipment.
