## Why Women's Sizing Matters

Standard unisex merchandise doesn't fit women well — and this is where most retailers lose sales. According to a 2025 survey by Retail Sport Australia, 64% of women who attended a cricket match said they would buy merchandise if it fit properly, but only 22% found unisex items acceptable.

When sourcing from Chinese factories:
- Specify women's sizing charts: XS (6) through 2XL (18) in Australian women's sizes
- Request a "fitted" cut rather than "relaxed" or "unisex" — this means narrower shoulders, smaller armholes, and a defined waistline
- Provide reference garments (a well-fitting women's t-shirt from a known brand) as a sample template
- Request graded samples across all sizes before committing to bulk production

A retailer in Melbourne added women's-fit cricket tees in WBBL team colours (no logos, just colour associations) and saw a 180% increase in female customer traffic and a 250% increase in average basket size among female customers.

## How to Source Colours That Match Team Palettes

When sourcing women's cricket merchandise for specific teams (West Indies maroon, Pakistan green), colour matching is critical. Chinese textile manufacturers can match any Pantone colour — but you must provide the reference:

- **West Indies maroon:** Pantone 209C (deep maroon/crimson — distinctive for Windies branding)
- **Pakistan green:** Pantone 356C (vibrant dark green — can be difficult to reproduce accurately)
- **Australia gold:** Pantone 116C (for any Australian women's team merchandise)

**Request lab dips** — small fabric swatches dyed to your specified Pantone — before committing to bulk production. A lab dip costs $15–$30 per colour but prevents the costly mistake of a full production run with the wrong colour. One Melbourne retailer who skipped this step received 800 t-shirts in "muddy green" instead of Pakistan green — a $5,200 mistake that required the entire batch to be re-dyed at additional cost.
