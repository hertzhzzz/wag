## 3. Manufacturing Modra-Era Retro Gear: What Chinese Factories Need to Get Right

Producing convincing retro AFL merchandise requires more than printing an old logo on a modern jersey. The sensory experience of 1990s AFL guernseys — the heavier fabric, the thicker embroidery, the wider cut — is what nostalgia buyers are paying for, and Chinese factories that have never produced retro sportswear will default to modern specifications unless you explicitly specify otherwise.

### Fabric Weight: The Single Most Important Specification

A modern replica AFL guernsey from a club shop weighs approximately 180-200 GSM (grams per square metre) and uses 100% polyester with moisture-wicking treatment. A 1990s-era Crows guernsey used a heavier knit, typically 280-320 GSM, often a polyester-wool or polyester-acrylic blend with no moisture-wicking treatment. The weight difference is immediately perceptible when a fan holds both garments side by side.

### The Number 6: Heat-Press vs Screen Print vs Tackle Twill

Modra's iconic number 6 must be applied using a method appropriate to the era. Modern AFL guernseys use heat-pressed vinyl numbers that are lightweight, durable, and quick to produce. 1990s-era guernseys, however, used screen-printed numbers on the replica versions and stitched tackle twill numbers on the player-issue versions.

For a retro product, the screen-printed number is the historically accurate choice for replica-grade merchandise, while tackle twill numbers create a premium tier product that commands a higher retail price. Heat-pressed vinyl numbers — while cheaper and easier to produce — will be immediately recognised by discerning buyers as inauthentic and will reduce the perceived value of the product.

| Number Application Method | Production Cost (per unit) | Era Authenticity | Recommended Retail Price Tier |
|---------------------------|---------------------------|-----------------|------------------------------|
| Heat-pressed vinyl | $0.60-0.80 | Low (modern) | Not recommended for retro products |
| Screen print | $0.40-0.60 | High (replica-grade) | $119-129 |
| Tackle twill stitch | $2.80-3.50 | High (player-issue grade) | $149-169 |

## FAQ

### Is it legal to produce Tony Modra merchandise without an AFL license?

Player names and likeness rights present a different legal landscape than club logos and trademarks. Tony Modra's name, likeness, and playing number are aspects of his personal brand. Club logos, the Adelaide Crows name, and the AFL logo are intellectual property owned by the respective clubs and league. Retailers should consult an Australian IP lawyer to understand the specific boundaries. In practice, many nostalgia merchandise products focus on era-specific designs that reference the player and the period without reproducing protected club marks — for example, a navy, red, and gold guernsey with Modra's number 6 in a period-accurate font, without the Crows logo. This approach sits in a legally grey area, and we strongly recommend legal review before commissioning any production.
