## 2. Richmond Yellow and Black — Manufacturing the Tigers' Signature Look

The Richmond Tigers colour scheme — yellow and black — is arguably the simplest in the AFL. Two colours. High contrast. Instantly recognisable. One might assume this makes Richmond merchandise the easiest to manufacture. In practice, the simplicity creates its own distinct set of challenges.

### The Yellow Problem

Yellow is among the most difficult colours to produce consistently in textile manufacturing. Pantone 109 C and Pantone 116 C — the two most common references for Richmond's yellow — are bright, warm yellows that require specific pigment formulations and tight temperature control during the dye bath process. Chinese factories that produce sports merchandise in bulk default to a cooler, greener yellow that is common in Chinese athletic uniforms. When this default yellow is substituted for Richmond's colour, the result is merchandise that looks closer to lime or chartreuse — immediately wrong to anyone who has seen the Tigers play.

The dye fastness issue compounds the colour problem. Yellow pigments are among the most UV-sensitive in textile manufacturing. A Richmond Tigers scarf or cap that tests perfectly yellow in the factory may fade to a pale, washed-out shade after four hours in direct sunlight at the MCG. Australian UV levels are significantly higher than those in China's textile-producing regions, and factories that have not exported to Australia routinely underestimate the lightfastness requirements.

### Production Specifications for Richmond Tigers Merchandise

| Parameter | Required Specification | Common Factory Default |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------------------|
| Yellow | Pantone 109 C or 116 C | Generic "sport yellow" (greener) |
| Black | Pantone Black 6 C (deepest black) | Pantone Black 3 C (charcoal-ish) |
| Yellow lightfastness | ISO 105-B02 Grade 4+ | ISO 105-B02 Grade 3 standard |
| Embroidery thread (yellow on black) | 2-ply polyester, matching Pantone | Single-ply, thinner contrast |
| Embroidery thread (black on yellow) | Matte black finish | Glossy black, reads as synthetic |
| Optical brightener (yellow) | None — shifts yellow toward green | Sometimes added by default |

Specify these parameters in your technical pack. Do not assume a factory that produces generic sports merchandise understands AFL-specific colour requirements. Send a physical colour swatch book page showing both Richmond yellow and Richmond black on the actual fabric substrate you intend to use.

### The Advantage of Simplicity

Despite these challenges, Richmond's two-colour scheme is fundamentally easier to produce correctly than the multi-colour kits of most AFL clubs. Where Brisbane requires three colours (maroon, blue, gold) and the Gold Coast Suns require three (red, gold, blue), Richmond needs two — and the high contrast between them means minor colour variations are less noticeable. A Richmond Tigers scarf that is slightly warm yellow against deep black still reads as "Richmond." A Brisbane Lions scarf where the maroon drifts toward burgundy reads as "wrong."

This manufacturing advantage translates directly to cost. A Richmond Tigers scarf costs $4.40 to $4.80 per unit FOB from a Chinese factory — approximately 5 to 10 percent less than a Brisbane Lions three-colour scarf, which requires additional dye lots and colour-change setup time. For retailers combining both clubs' merchandise in a single order, this cost differential is worth understanding when planning your SKU mix.

### The Maroon Question

Maroon is the most frequently misunderstood colour in AFL merchandise, a challenge documented extensively across WAG's AFL sourcing guides.
