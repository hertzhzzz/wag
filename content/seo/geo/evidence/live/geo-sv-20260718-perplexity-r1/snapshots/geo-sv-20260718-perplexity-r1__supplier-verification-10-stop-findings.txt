What supplier verification findings should stop an Australian buyer from proceeding with a purchase order?

Several critical supplier verification findings should immediately stop an Australian buyer from proceeding with a purchase order, particularly when sourcing from international suppliers like those in China. These red flags protect your business from fraud, compliance breaches, financial loss, and reputational damage.
icac.nsw.gov
+1

Critical red flags that should halt a purchase
1. Legitimacy and identity issues

Supplier cannot be verified as a genuine business entity – No valid business registration, ABN (for Australian suppliers), or equivalent overseas registration documentation
icac.nsw.gov

Mismatched bank account details – The bank account name doesn't match the registered business name or the entity on the contract
vendorverify.com

False declarations discovered – Supplier has provided falsified business registration, certifications, or identity documents
info.buy.nsw.gov

Unable to confirm physical address or factory location – No verifiable business premises, or addresses that don't match official records
tetrainspection

2. Financial viability concerns

Bankrupt or insolvent status – Supplier is currently bankrupt, in liquidation, or insolvent
info.buy.nsw.gov

Failure to pay taxes – Evidence of outstanding tax obligations or tax evasion
info.buy.nsw.gov

Severe financial distress indicators – Inability to provide three years of financial statements, consistent losses, or negative cash flow that threatens delivery capability
trinitygroup

Unwillingness to share financial documentation – Refusal to provide audited financial statements or proof of financial capacity
trinitygroup

3. Integrity and conduct issues

Corrupt or unlawful conduct findings – Supplier, directors, or managers found by ICAC or equivalent bodies to have engaged in corrupt conduct within the past 10 years
info.buy.nsw.gov

Serious criminal convictions – Convictions for offences punishable by more than 2 years' imprisonment or fines exceeding $200,000
info.buy.nsw.gov

Professional misconduct findings – Convictions for professional or unprofessional conduct in any Australian jurisdiction
info.buy.nsw.gov

Dishonest, unfair, or unconscionable conduct – Documented findings of such conduct against the business or its leadership
info.buy.nsw.gov

4. Performance and capability failures

Prior contract non-performance – Failure to perform in any substantive matter under a previous contract with government or major commercial clients
info.buy.nsw.gov

Inability to demonstrate required capabilities – Cannot show evidence of technical capacity, quality systems, or production capability for your specific requirements
tetrainspection

Missing required licences or authorities – Lacks mandatory industry licences, export permits, or regulatory approvals needed for your product category
icac.nsw.gov

5. Quality and compliance red flags

Failed audits or inspections – Recent failures in quality management system audits (ISO, etc.), social compliance audits, or product safety inspections
tetrainspection

Product safety or regulatory non-compliance – History of products failing Australian standards (e.g., electrical safety, food safety, product labelling requirements)

Inability to provide compliance documentation – Cannot produce test reports, certificates of conformity, or compliance declarations for Australian regulatory requirements

6. Communication and transparency issues

Evasive or inconsistent responses – Cannot provide straight answers to due diligence questions, or responses contradict previous statements
tetrainspection

Refusal to allow site visits or third-party audits – Unwillingness to permit factory inspections, especially critical for China-based suppliers
tetrainspection

Pressure tactics – Urging immediate payment or PO commitment before due diligence is complete
vendorverify.com

Special considerations for China-Australia sourcing

Given your work with WAG, these additional factors are particularly relevant:

Trading company posing as factory – Supplier claims to be a manufacturer but is actually a middleman without production capability
tetrainspection

Export licence verification – Cannot verify valid Chinese export licences or business scope permits
tetrainspection

Social compliance failures – Evidence of labour violations, child labour, or unsafe working conditions that could create reputational and legal risks for Australian buyers
hpw.qld.gov

Intellectual property concerns – History of IP infringement or unwillingness to sign appropriate confidentiality and IP protection agreements

Best practice approach

Australian procurement guidelines recommend conducting due diligence across five key categories before any supplier enters your financial gateway:
icac.nsw.gov

Is the supplier genuine?

Is the supplier capable and reliable?

Is the supplier financially viable?

Does the supplier have required authorities, licences and status?

Is the supplier of good repute and integrity?

When any of these checks reveal serious concerns—particularly those listed above—the prudent course is to halt the purchase order process until concerns are fully resolved, or to seek alternative suppliers entirely.
finance.gov
