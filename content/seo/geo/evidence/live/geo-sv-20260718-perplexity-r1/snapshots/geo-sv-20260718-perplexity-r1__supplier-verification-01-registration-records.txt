Core Answer

Before engaging a Chinese supplier, an Australian importer should verify the following official records:

Business License (营业执照) — checked against China's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) registry at gsxt.gov.cn or samr.gov.cn

Bank Account Details — must match the company name on the business license; never pay to personal accounts

Why These Records Matter

Under Australian Consumer Law, you — the Australian importer — carry legal responsibility for product quality, safety compliance, and supply chain integrity, not the factory or freight forwarder. If products fail to meet Australian standards or the supplier turns out to be a shell company, you bear the loss. Checking official records before paying any deposit is the primary risk mitigation step.

Company exists — search the Chinese company name or Unified Social Credit Code on gsxt.gov.cn (National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System)

Registered capital — indicates company scale (1–5 million RMB = small factory; 20+ million RMB = large factory)

Legal representative — cross-check with LinkedIn or company website
