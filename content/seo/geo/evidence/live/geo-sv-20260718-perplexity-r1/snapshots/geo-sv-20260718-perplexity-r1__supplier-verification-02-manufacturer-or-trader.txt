How can an Australian buyer distinguish a Chinese manufacturer from a trading company?
Completed 2 steps

An Australian buyer can distinguish a Chinese manufacturer from a trading company by checking the registered business scope (生产/制造 vs 贸易/批发), verifying the factory address and conducting live video audits, comparing pricing/MOQ and customization capability, and requesting export records plus certificates that match the company name.
linkedin
+3

Quick definitions

Manufacturer (factory): Owns and operates production equipment, employs production staff, and makes the product (or core components) in-house.
linkedin
+1

Trading company: Buys from factories and resells to overseas buyers; often adds services like consolidation, quality control, and logistics but does not own the production line.
linkedin
+1

Practical ways to tell them apart

Use several signals together; no single check is decisive.

1. Business license and registered scope (most reliable paper check)

Ask for the company’s business license (营业执照) and look up the Unified Social Credit Code on Chinese registries such as Qichacha (企查查) or Tianyancha (天眼查), or the official National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (gsxt.gov.cn).
linkedin
+2

Read the business scope (经营范围):

Manufacturer: includes words like 生产 / 制造 / 加工 (production/manufacturing/processing).
linkedin
+2

Trading company: mainly 贸易 / 批发 / 进出口 (trade/wholesale/import‑export) with no production wording.
linkedin
+2

Also note the registered address: an office-tower address for a claimed “factory” is a red flag; factories are usually in industrial zones or manufacturing hubs.
linkedin
+2

2. Factory address and on‑site verification

Ask for the exact factory address and check it on maps (Google/Baidu).
linkedin
+1

Whenever possible, visit the factory or send a local agent. If that’s not feasible, insist on a live video call to:

See the production line, equipment, and workers in action.
linkedin
+1

Ask them to show today’s production board, molds, and QC area.
xsourcinggroup

Polished pre-recorded videos are easy to share; real-time access is harder to fake.
xsourcinggroup

3. Product range and specialization

Manufacturers usually have a narrower, deeper product range focused on what they actually make.
sourcingarts
+1

Trading companies often show a very broad catalog across unrelated categories (e.g., electronics, home goods, apparel) because they source from many factories.
sourcingarts
+1

4. Pricing, MOQ, and flexibility

Manufacturers typically offer more competitive unit prices but require higher MOQs and batch production.
linkedin
+1

Trading companies often accept smaller orders and provide flexibility but at a marked-up price.
linkedin
+1

Be aware: a large trader can sometimes beat a factory’s price on small/medium orders due to aggregated volume.
xsourcinggroup

5. Customization and OEM/ODM capability

Manufacturers can usually modify materials, dimensions, tooling, and processes, and offer true OEM/ODM services.
linkedin
+1

Trading companies may claim customization but often have limited control and must go back to the factory for changes, leading to slower responses and higher costs for bespoke requests.
linkedin
+1

6. Certificates, test reports, and export records

Check quality certificates (e.g., ISO 9001, CE, FDA, BRC, HACCP) and ensure the certificate holder name matches the business license.
linkedin
+1

Review test reports (e.g., SGS, Intertek) and confirm the factory name on the report.
v-trust

Use customs/export data or third‑party platforms (e.g., ImportGenius, Panjiva) to see if the company has direct export records as the exporter of record.
linkedin
+1

7. Payment details and bank account name

The bank account name on the proforma invoice should match the licensed company name.
xsourcinggroup

Requests to pay into a personal account or a different company are strong red flags.
xsourcinggroup

8. Online presence and platform badges

Company profiles on websites or platforms (Alibaba, Global Sources) often state whether they are a manufacturer or trading company; read beyond marketing blurbs.
v-trust

Platform badges like “Verified Manufacturer” can be useful as a first filter but are not conclusive; still check the license and do a video/physical audit.
xsourcinggroup

When a trading company might be the better choice

Sometimes a trader is preferable even if you can identify them:

You need multiple product categories in one container or shipment.
xsourcinggroup

You have no Mandarin-speaking staff and want help with coordination, QC, and logistics.
xsourcinggroup

Your order volumes are moderate, and the trader’s aggregated buying power gives you a better net price or service level.

Get business license; check scope for 生产/制造 vs 贸易 on gsxt.gov.cn or Qichacha/Tianyancha.
