Why generic checklists fail

Generic checklists often miss critical, product-specific failure modes, leading to inconsistent inspections and unexpected defects reaching your customers. They tend to be too broad, lacking the technical detail needed to catch issues unique to your product category—whether it’s seam strength in soft goods, voltage tolerance in electronics, or chemical compliance in cosmetics.

Steps to build a product-specific checklist
1. Start with your product specification sheet

Use your approved tech pack, engineering drawing, or product specification document as the single source of truth. Every critical-to-quality (CTQ) attribute—dimensions, materials, colors, functions, labeling—should be traceable back to this document.

Example structure for a product-specific checklist
Section	Example Items (Customized per Product)
Product Info	SKU, batch/lot number, production date, factory line
Packaging & Labeling	Barcode scan test, country of origin label, retail box crush test, manual included
Visual & Dimensional	Color match vs Pantone 19-4052, length 150 ± 2 mm, no visible glue marks, logo centered ± 1 mm
Materials & Construction	Fabric 280 GSM ± 10, stitching 8–10 SPI, no loose threads > 5 mm
Function & Performance	Button clicks > 10,000 cycles, app pairs within 30 seconds, load test 5 kg for 10 min
Safety & Compliance	CE mark present, no sharp edges > 0.5 mm, REACH SVHC test report attached
Defect Log	Photo + description of each defect, classified as critical/major/minor
Final Disposition	Pass / Fail / Hold for rework, inspector signature
