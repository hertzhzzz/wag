In quality inspection, defects are classified into three severity levels—critical, major, and minor—based on their impact on safety, functionality, and appearance. This classification determines whether a shipment passes or fails under AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) standards.

Criteria	Critical Defect	Major Defect	Minor Defect

Critical Defects

Critical defects are the most severe and are unacceptable under any condition. They include:

Safety hazards that could cause injury or harm to users

Non-compliance with mandatory regulations (e.g., toxic materials, electrical safety failures)

Structural failures that could lead to product collapse

Sharp edges or conditions endangering life or safety

+2

The default AQL tolerance for critical defects is 0, meaning even one critical defect found in a sample typically results in automatic shipment rejection.

Major Defects

Major defects affect the functionality, usability, or marketability of the product. These include:

Product does not function properly for its intended purpose

Missing key components or incorrect dimensions affecting usability

Packaging failure that compromises product integrity

Obvious appearance disfiguration that affects salability

+2

The typical AQL tolerance for major defects is 2.5. For example, in a sample of 200 pieces, up to 10 major defects may be acceptable; 11 or more would result in rejection.

Minor Defects

Minor defects are cosmetic or aesthetic issues that do not reduce the product's usability or function. Examples include:

Surface scratches, spots, or dirt marks

Minor color variation within tolerance

Slight labeling misalignment

Small imperfections on non-central parts of the product

+2

The default AQL acceptance limit for minor defects is 4.0. In a sample of 200 pieces, up to 14 minor defects may be acceptable; 15 or more would fail inspection.

Where This Classification Is Used

This defect classification system is standard in:

Pre-shipment inspection (PSI)

During production inspection (DPI)

Factory audits and quality assessments

AQL sampling based on ISO 2859 standards
