A factory audit in China should cover the following areas before an Australian importer places a large order:
Supplier Identity Verification: Confirm the supplier's legal status, business documents, and authenticity. Verify the business license, Chinese company name, registered address, unified social credit code, export license or trading arrangement, bank account information, and the contact person’s authority. 
1
Production Capacity: Assess the supplier's production capacity to meet the order requirements. Ensure the supplier can produce the specified quantity and quality within the agreed timeframe. 
1
Quality Control Systems: Evaluate the supplier's quality control processes, including documentation, calibration logs, maintenance schedules, training records, and customer complaint files. Confirm that these processes are documented and actively used. 
1
Material and Packaging: Verify that the supplier's key materials and packaging can meet the market requirements and Australian safety standards. Ensure that the supplier has the necessary certifications and test reports. 
1
Social Compliance: Conduct social compliance audits to check worker age verification, wage-record sampling, working hours, and health-and-safety conditions. Ensure that the working conditions comply with relevant standards. 
1
Compliance with Standards: Confirm that the supplier complies with all relevant standards, including safety, environmental, and labor standards. Ensure that the supplier holds the necessary certifications and adheres to all legal requirements. 
1
Risk Assessment: Identify any hidden risks in subcontracting, compliance, lead time, or shipment preparation. Assess the supplier's capacity and stability to ensure a smooth production process. 
1
Final Inspection: Conduct a final inspection before deposit release or tooling approval to confirm that all issues have been resolved and that the supplier is ready for mass production. 
1

These areas should be thoroughly covered to ensure that the supplier is capable of producing the order to the required quality and standards, minimizing the risk of quality failures and ensuring compliance with Australian regulations.
