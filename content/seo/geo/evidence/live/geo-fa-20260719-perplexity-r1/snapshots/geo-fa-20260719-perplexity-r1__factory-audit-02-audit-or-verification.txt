A factory audit, supplier verification, and product inspection are three distinct stages of supply chain quality control, each serving a different purpose:

Supplier verification confirms the supplier is a legitimate, legally registered business before you engage them.

Factory audit evaluates the supplier's systems, capabilities, and compliance to assess whether they can reliably produce quality products.

Product inspection checks the actual finished goods to ensure they meet your specifications before shipment.

Here's how they differ in detail:

Core Differences
Aspect	Supplier Verification	Factory Audit	Product Inspection
Primary Focus	Business legitimacy and legal status	Systems, capabilities, and compliance	Finished product quality
Timing	Before engagement (pre-contract)	Before or during production	After production, before shipment
What's Checked	Business license, registration, legal existence	Production capacity, quality systems, social compliance, equipment	Product dimensions, function, appearance, packaging, defects
Outcome	Go/no-go decision on working with supplier	Risk assessment and capability rating	Accept/reject decision on shipment
Duration	Document review (hours to days)	On-site visit (1–2 days)	On-site sampling (hours to 1 day)
Supplier Verification

Supplier verification is the first gate in your sourcing process. It's an independent assessment that confirms:

Legal business registration and license validity

Company ownership structure

Business scope matches what they claim to supply

No red flags in legal or compliance history
tetrainspection

This is typically a document-based check rather than an on-site visit. For your WAG business coordinating China-Australia sourcing, this would involve verifying business licenses (营业执照), checking company registration through official Chinese databases, and confirming the supplier's stated business scope aligns with the products they're offering.

Factory Audit

A factory audit evaluates the supplier as an organization—its systems, capability, and compliance—not the finished products. Key characteristics:
tradeaiders

Systems-focused: Assesses quality management systems (e.g., ISO 9001 alignment), production processes, and organizational structure
tetrainspection

Capability assessment: Reviews equipment, workforce skills, production capacity, and R&D capability

Compliance check: May include social compliance (labor practices), environmental compliance, and security standards

Long-term improvement: Drives corrective action plans for systemic improvements
qualityinspection

Factory audits require auditors with strong social skills to extract information tactfully from auditees, and the findings often influence whether to establish or continue a business relationship. A single failed audit might be enough to stop a business relationship entirely.
qualityinspection

For your factory visit itineraries with WAG clients, this is the comprehensive assessment you'd conduct when clients want to evaluate whether a supplier can handle their ongoing production needs.

Product Inspection

Product inspection is product-focused and checks the actual goods that have been manufactured:

Strict checklist: Inspectors follow detailed checklists against your product specifications

Sampling-based: Uses statistical sampling (often AQL standards) to check a representative portion of the shipment

Defect identification: Identifies functional defects, cosmetic issues, dimensional problems, packaging errors

Short-term fixes: Drives immediate correction plans for the specific shipment
qualityinspection

Inspectors need to be familiar with the specific product being checked and often work under stressful circumstances with potential disputes with factory staff. Unlike audits, inspections require extensive photo documentation of defects and product conditions.
qualityinspection

This is what you'd arrange when your clients have production underway and need to ensure the goods meet quality standards before they're shipped to Australia.

When to Use Each

For your China-Australia sourcing workflow:

Supplier verification: Before introducing any new supplier to WAG clients—confirm they're legitimate businesses

Factory audit: When clients are evaluating suppliers for ongoing partnerships or large orders—assess capability and systems

Product inspection: When production is complete and goods are ready to ship—verify actual product quality

These three processes work together as a comprehensive risk management strategy, with each addressing different types of risk at different stages of the sourcing relationship.
