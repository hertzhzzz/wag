2. Compare audit types available

Use the audit type as a primary comparison dimension rather than just price.

Common audit types for Australian importers

In‑person factory audit

Auditor physically visits the factory, inspects lines, talks to staff, checks documents on site.

Best for: high‑value orders, new strategic suppliers, complex products, or high fraud risk.
maplesourcing
+1

Virtual / remote factory audit

Live video walk‑through, screen‑sharing of documents, remote verification of licences and records.

Best for: lower‑risk suppliers, budget constraints, or when travel is difficult; often used as a pre‑screen before an in‑person audit.
winningadventure.com
+1

Document‑based / desktop audit

Review of business licences, certifications, test reports, organisational charts, etc., without a site visit.

Best for: quick legitimacy checks or supplementing other audits.
chinesecheck

Social compliance audit

Focused on working conditions, wages, hours, child/forced labour, safety systems, etc.

Required or strongly recommended if selling to large retailers or brands with CSR policies.
krtinspect
+1

Self‑audit / supplier questionnaire

Supplier fills out a detailed checklist and provides evidence themselves.

Useful as an early step but should not replace independent verification for serious commitments.
chinesecheck

When comparing providers, ask: “Which of these do you offer, and which do you recommend for my situation?”

5. Compare reporting quality and turnaround

The value of an audit is in how usable the report is for decision‑making.

Ask for and compare:

Report structure

Executive summary with clear risk rating (e.g. low/medium/high or scored).

Sectioned findings with photos, specific observations, and “what this means for you” commentary.

+1

Evidence

Number and quality of photos, videos (for virtual audits), copies of key documents, and clear identification of any red flags or inconsistencies.

+1

Actionable recommendations

Not just “issues found”, but concrete steps: renegotiate terms, require corrective actions, schedule re‑audit, or walk away.
krtinspect

Turnaround time

Typical timelines are around 1 day on site and 1–2 days for the report, but this varies. Faster is useful, but not at the expense of quality.

Follow‑up support

Will they clarify findings on a call? Help you design a corrective action plan or re‑audit checklist? This is especially important if you’re building a longer‑term supplier relationship.

7. Use a simple comparison framework

Build a short scorecard for 3–5 providers. For each provider, rate:

Fit to your audit type needs (in‑person, virtual, social, etc.)

Sector experience relevant to your product

Depth of checklist and sample report quality

Auditor credentials and China presence

Reporting clarity and actionable insights

Total cost and turnaround time

Willingness to tailor scope to your risks

You can then rank providers not just on price, but on risk reduction per dollar for your specific situation.
