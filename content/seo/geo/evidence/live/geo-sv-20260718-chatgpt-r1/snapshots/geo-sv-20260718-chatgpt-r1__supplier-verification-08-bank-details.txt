## Prompt (exact)
How should I verify bank account details before paying a new Chinese supplier?

## User message (UI)
How should I verify bank account details before paying a new Chinese supplier?

## Assistant answer (UI text)
Before paying a new Chinese supplier, verify the bank account details through independent channels and confirm that the payment recipient is the same legal entity you contracted with. Many supplier fraud cases involve legitimate-looking invoices with changed bank details.

1. Match the bank account to the supplier’s legal entity

Request:

Chinese company legal name (Chinese characters)

Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码)

Registered address

Business licence copy

Bank account name (账户名称)

Bank account number

Bank name and branch (开户行)

Check that:

Item	Must match
Contract party	Supplier’s registered Chinese company name
Invoice issuer	Same company
Bank account holder	Same company (preferably)
Business licence	Same legal entity

Avoid paying a personal account or an unrelated company account unless you have a documented reason and additional verification.

2. Verify the company’s official registration

Use China’s official corporate registry:

National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System

Search using the supplier’s:

Chinese company name

Unified Social Credit Code

Confirm:

Company status is active (存续/在业)

Legal representative

Registered capital

Business scope

Registration date

Address

A recently created company with little history requires more caution.

3. Confirm the bank details directly with the supplier

Do not rely only on email attachments.

Use a second communication channel:

Call the supplier’s known business phone number

Use a video call

Contact the sales manager through a verified company domain email

Ask:

“Please confirm your company bank account details verbally. We received the payment instructions and need to verify before transfer.”

This helps detect invoice interception scams.

4. Check whether the account is a company account

For international payments, request:

Bank account certificate (银行开户许可证 or equivalent bank information document)

Company stamped payment instruction letter (盖公章)

The document should show:

Company name

Bank name

Account number

Official company stamp

Be cautious if they provide only:

Screenshot of online banking

Personal bank details

WhatsApp message with changed account numbers

5. Verify SWIFT and international payment information

For overseas payments, confirm:

Beneficiary name exactly

SWIFT/BIC code

Bank name

Bank branch

Currency account (USD/EUR/AUD etc.)

The beneficiary name should normally match the registered company name.

Small differences matter:

❌ Guangzhou ABC Trading Ltd
✅ 广州ABC贸易有限公司

Ask your bank if the beneficiary name matches before releasing funds.

6. Watch for common payment fraud signals

High-risk indicators:

“Our bank account changed because of tax reasons”

“Pay our Hong Kong company instead”

“Pay our employee’s account temporarily”

“Use a different account for this order”

Last-minute bank detail changes before payment

Pressure to pay immediately

Supplier refuses video verification

Treat any bank change request as suspicious until independently confirmed.

7. Start with a controlled payment

For a first transaction:

Avoid paying 100% upfront

Use staged payments:

20–30% deposit

Balance after inspection/photos/testing

Consider:

Letter of Credit (L/C)

Escrow

Trade assurance mechanisms

Third-party inspection before final payment

8. For larger payments, perform enhanced verification

For payments above roughly AUD $10,000–50,000+, consider:

Paid supplier verification report

Factory audit

Bank account verification service

On-site inspection

A verification service should confirm:

Legal company existence

Factory/business location

Ownership/management links

Export capability

Bank account ownership

Production capability

The key rule: never trust bank details from a single email or PDF. Verify the beneficiary independently before every first payment and every account change.
