## Prompt (exact)
What warning signs suggest that a Chinese supplier's business licence or certificates may be false?

## User message (UI)
What warning signs suggest that a Chinese supplier's business licence or certificates may be false?

## Assistant answer (UI text)
Warning signs that a Chinese supplier’s business licence or certificates may be false, altered, expired, or misleading include:

1. Business licence inconsistencies

Company name mismatch

The Chinese legal name on the business licence does not exactly match:

the quotation

sales contract

invoice (发票)

bank account holder

customs documents

Incorrect Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码)

The 18-digit code is missing, has incorrect formatting, or does not match official records.

Suspicious registration details

The registered address appears to be:

a residential apartment for a supposedly large factory

a virtual office

unrelated to manufacturing activity

Business scope mismatch (经营范围)

Example:

Supplier claims to manufacture LED lighting equipment.

Business scope only covers trading, consulting, or unrelated products.

A supplier may legally trade products without manufacturing them, but they should not falsely claim factory ownership.

Recent registration with no operating history

A company registered only a few months ago claiming "20 years of manufacturing experience" is a red flag.

2. Document quality issues

Low-resolution scans

Blurry licences, inconsistent fonts, missing seals, or uneven text alignment.

Edited PDFs or images

Signs include:

different font styles in the same document

inconsistent spacing

copied stamps/seals

unnatural backgrounds

metadata showing recent editing

Missing official stamps

Chinese official documents normally contain government-issued formatting and seals.

Incorrect terminology

Examples:

wrong Chinese legal terms

simplified/traditional Chinese inconsistencies

incorrect government department names

3. Certificate authenticity problems
ISO certificates

Warning signs:

Certificate issued by an unknown certification body

Certification body cannot be found in accreditation databases

Certificate number cannot be verified

Scope is too broad:

"Manufacturing of all electronic products"

"Global quality management"

Certificate covers a product category unrelated to the supplier

Verify:

issuing organisation

certificate number

validity dates

audit scope

Product certifications

Examples:

CE

RoHS

FCC

UL

RCM (Australia/New Zealand)

Warning signs:

Supplier provides only a logo instead of a certificate/report

Test report does not show:

manufacturer name

model number

testing laboratory

standards used

Certificate belongs to another company

Example:

Supplier sells LED fixtures but provides an RCM certificate under another unrelated Chinese company.

This may indicate:

reseller behaviour

certificate misuse

counterfeit documentation

4. Bank account warning signs

High-risk indicators:

Payment requested to:

personal account

unrelated company account

Hong Kong company without explanation

Bank account name does not exactly match the registered Chinese company name.

Example:

Business licence:

Guangzhou ABC Lighting Technology Co., Ltd.

Payment request:

Shenzhen XYZ Trading Limited

Possible explanations:

legitimate trading company relationship

export agent arrangement

But the supplier should provide written evidence.

5. Factory-related warning signs

If claiming to be a manufacturer:

Refuses a live video factory inspection

Only shows:

showroom

warehouse

office

Factory photos:

appear professionally staged

contain no employees

lack production equipment

Cannot answer technical manufacturing questions:

production capacity

machinery

QC process

raw material suppliers

6. Verification red flags during checks

A supplier should be investigated further if:

Issue	Risk
Licence name differs from contract	Contract enforcement problems
Certificate holder differs from supplier	Certification misuse
Address cannot be found	Fake company possibility
Website domain created recently	Possible short-term trading operation
Refuses company registration details	Transparency issue
Pushes urgent payment	Fraud risk
Price far below market	Possible quality/document problems
7. Recommended verification steps for Australian importers

Before paying a deposit:

Obtain:

Chinese business licence (营业执照)

Unified Social Credit Code

company bank account details

export licence information (if applicable)

certificates and test reports

Verify:

company registration through China’s official National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (国家企业信用信息公示系统)

certificate issuer databases

laboratory accreditation records

Match all names:

business licence

contract

invoice

bank account

certification documents

For high-value orders:

conduct third-party factory inspection

request production samples

use staged payments linked to inspection results

The biggest warning sign is usually not one incorrect document, but multiple inconsistencies between the legal entity, payment entity, factory identity, and certification holder.
