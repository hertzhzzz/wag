How should an Australian business compare supplier verification services for work in China?

## User message (UI)
How should an Australian business compare supplier verification services for work in China?

## Assistant answer (UI text)
An Australian business comparing supplier verification services for work in China should compare verification depth, evidence quality, independence, local capability, and risk coverage, not just price.

1. Compare the verification scope
Area	Basic service	Strong service
Company registration	Checks business name only	Verifies Chinese legal entity, Unified Social Credit Code, registration status
Ownership	Limited	Checks shareholders, legal representative, registered capital
Factory existence	Online check	Physical factory visit with photos/video/GPS evidence
Manufacturing capability	Supplier claims	Production lines, machinery, workers, capacity verification
Export ability	Self-declaration	Checks export licences, customs capability, export history
Certifications	Document review	Validates issuing body, certificate number, test reports
Financial risk	Usually absent	Credit checks, litigation, debt indicators
Reputation	Reviews only	Industry references and customer verification
2. Check who performs the verification

A strong provider should disclose:

Who visits the factory

Whether inspectors are employees or subcontractors

Their China coverage (cities/provinces)

Whether they speak Mandarin

Whether reports are produced locally or remotely

Conflict-of-interest controls

Avoid services that only:

Forward supplier documents

Copy Alibaba profiles

Provide AI-generated summaries

Accept supplier-provided photos as proof

3. Compare evidence provided

A useful verification report should include:

Company identity

Chinese company name

Business licence copy

Unified Social Credit Code

Registration authority record

Legal representative

Registered address

Factory evidence

Factory entrance photo

Production floor photos

Machinery list

Employee estimate

Warehouse inspection

Production workflow

Commercial evidence

Export history

Main markets served

Customer references (where possible)

Product samples reviewed

Risk findings

Warning signs

Inconsistencies

Recommended actions

4. Evaluate service levels

A typical comparison:

Service type	Suitable for	Typical limitation
Online supplier check	Small trial orders	Cannot confirm factory reality
Video factory audit	Medium-risk suppliers	Supplier may control the environment
Third-party factory inspection	Larger purchases	Costs more
Full due diligence	High-value contracts	Longer process

For Australian importers, a physical verification is usually justified when:

First order exceeds roughly A$10,000–50,000

Custom tooling is involved

Product safety/compliance matters

Supplier requires large deposits

Supplier refuses normal verification requests

5. Check industry experience

Prefer providers with experience in your product category:

Examples:

Electronics → compliance testing, CE/RCM documentation, component tracing

Machinery → factory capability and engineering review

Furniture → materials, production capacity, QC processes

Lighting/AV → certifications, electrical safety, export history

A generic supplier checker may miss industry-specific risks.

6. Compare pricing against risk exposure

Do not compare only:

"Supplier verification = A$200 vs A$800"

Compare:

"Verification cost vs potential loss"

Example:

Order value: A$80,000

Deposit: 40% = A$32,000

Verification cost: A$500–1,500

A proper verification may prevent a six-figure loss.

7. Questions to ask verification providers

Before hiring, ask:

Do you verify the Chinese legal entity directly?

Do you physically visit factories?

Are visits announced or unannounced?

Can you verify bank account ownership?

Do you check whether the supplier is a manufacturer or trading company?

Do you verify certificates with issuing organisations?

What evidence is included in the final report?

Can you inspect samples before shipment?

Do you have experience with Australian import requirements?

Recommended comparison framework

Score each provider out of 100:

Category	Weight
Legal verification	20
Physical factory inspection	25
Industry expertise	15
Evidence quality	15
Independence	10
Compliance knowledge	10
Price/value	5

For most Australian businesses importing from China, the strongest option is usually a local China-based inspection company or due-diligence provider that combines government record checks with physical factory verification. A purely online verification service is best treated as an initial screening step, not final approval.
