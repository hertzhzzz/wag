## Prompt (exact)
How can an Australian buyer distinguish a Chinese manufacturer from a trading company?

## User message (UI)
How can an Australian buyer distinguish a Chinese manufacturer from a trading company?

## Assistant answer (UI text)
There is no single definitive test. Many legitimate Chinese suppliers operate both manufacturing and trading businesses. The best approach is to verify multiple independent indicators.

1. Check the Chinese business registration

Request the supplier's:

Chinese business licence (营业执照)

Chinese company name

Unified Social Credit Code (18-digit number)

Then verify:

Business scope (经营范围) – Does it include manufacturing or only import/export and trading?

Registered address – Industrial parks are more consistent with factories than CBD office buildings.

Registered capital – Not conclusive, but very small capital can indicate a small trading operation.

2. Ask direct manufacturing questions

A genuine manufacturer can usually answer questions such as:

How many production lines do you operate?

How many factory workers are employed?

What is your monthly production capacity?

What machinery do you own?

Which manufacturing processes are performed in-house?

Which processes are outsourced?

Can you provide a factory layout?

Trading companies often answer these questions vaguely or need to "check with the factory."

3. Request factory evidence

Ask for:

Factory exterior photos showing the company name

Production workshop photos

Warehouse photos

Production line videos

Quality-control area

Packaging area

Request today's newspaper or today's date to avoid recycled marketing photos.

4. Conduct a live video factory tour

Schedule a video call.

Ask the supplier to walk through:

Reception

Production floor

Raw material storage

Finished goods warehouse

QC inspection area

Shipping/loading area

Live tours are much harder to fake than promotional videos.

5. Ask technical questions

Manufacturers generally understand:

Material specifications

Production tolerances

Manufacturing limitations

Process improvements

Quality-control methods

Trading companies frequently relay answers from engineers.

6. Review certifications carefully

If the supplier claims certifications (such as ISO 9001), check:

Is the certificate issued to the same legal entity?

Does the address match?

Does the scope cover manufacturing?

Sometimes the certificate belongs to a different factory.

7. Compare quotations

Manufacturers often provide:

Lower MOQ flexibility

Better pricing on large orders

Faster engineering feedback

Better customization capability

Trading companies may:

Offer a wider product range

Quote products from many unrelated industries

Be less flexible on technical changes

8. Ask who owns the factory

A useful question is:

"Is your company the legal owner of the manufacturing facility, or do you work with partner factories?"

Many trading companies will answer honestly if asked directly.

9. Use independent factory audits

For significant purchases, arrange a third-party audit before placing production.

An audit typically verifies:

Factory existence

Production capability

Employee numbers

Equipment

Quality systems

Export history

For Australian importers, this is often one of the highest-value verification steps.

10. Look for common warning signs

Possible indicators of a trading company include:

Thousands of unrelated products

"We can make anything"

No factory address

Refusal to allow factory visits

Technical questions always require checking with someone else

Salespeople cannot explain production details

Different company names appear on quotations and certificates

Important caveat

Being a trading company is not inherently a problem.

Many excellent Chinese trading companies:

Work with audited factories

Provide stronger English communication

Handle quality inspections

Consolidate shipments from multiple manufacturers

Manage sourcing across several product categories

For many Australian importers, a capable trading company can deliver better overall service than dealing directly with multiple factories.

Practical due diligence for Australian buyers

Before committing to a supplier, consider verifying:

The legal business registration in China.

Factory ownership or manufacturing capability.

Export experience with Australia.

Relevant product certifications (e.g. Australian compliance where applicable).

Third-party factory audit for larger orders.

Product inspection before shipment.

Using these checks together provides a much more reliable assessment than relying on a supplier's claim that they are a "manufacturer."
