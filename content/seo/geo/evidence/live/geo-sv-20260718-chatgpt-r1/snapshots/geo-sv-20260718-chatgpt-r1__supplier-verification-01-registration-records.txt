## Prompt (exact)
What official records should an Australian importer check before engaging a supplier in China?

## User message (UI)
What official records should an Australian importer check before engaging a supplier in China?

## Assistant answer (UI text)
Before engaging a Chinese supplier, an Australian importer should verify both the supplier's legal existence in China and their ability to export products that comply with Australian requirements.

1. Verify the Chinese company's legal registration

Check the supplier's official business registration through China's government records.

Important records include:

Business License (营业执照)

Official company name (Chinese)

Unified Social Credit Code

Legal representative

Registered capital

Establishment date

Business scope

Registration status (active, revoked, cancelled)

Company Registration Record

Shareholders

Ownership structure

Registered address

Branch offices

Historical changes

Useful government source:

China's National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (国家企业信用信息公示系统)

2. Review the company's compliance history

Look for:

Administrative penalties

Serious violations

Abnormal operating status

Court judgments

Enforcement actions

Bankruptcy proceedings

These can indicate financial or legal risk.

3. Confirm export capability

Not every manufacturer is legally equipped to export.

Verify whether the supplier has:

Export rights

Customs registration

Export licence (where applicable)

Experience exporting to Australia

Request examples of:

Previous Bills of Lading

Export declarations

Australian customers (if they can disclose them)

4. Verify tax information

Request:

VAT registration

VAT invoice capability (增值税专用发票)

Taxpayer classification

This helps confirm they are operating legitimately.

5. Confirm manufacturing capability

If they claim to be a manufacturer, request:

Factory address

Production licence (if applicable)

Factory audit reports

ISO certifications

Employee count

Production capacity

Many "manufacturers" are actually trading companies.

6. Check intellectual property records

Search for:

Registered trademarks

Patents

IP infringement cases

This reduces the risk of purchasing products that infringe third-party rights.

7. Review litigation records

Search for:

Contract disputes

Product quality disputes

Labour disputes

Debt collection cases

A company involved in frequent litigation may present elevated commercial risk.

8. Verify product certifications

Depending on the products:

For Australia:

RCM (where applicable)

EMC reports

Electrical safety reports

AS/NZS compliance

Test reports from accredited laboratories

For international products:

CE

FCC

RoHS

REACH

UL (where relevant)

Always verify that certificates correspond to the exact product being purchased.

9. Financial due diligence

Request evidence such as:

Years in operation

Major customers

Annual export markets

Factory photographs

Banking information

Trade references

Commercial credit reports can also provide insight into financial stability.

10. Verify shipping information

Request copies of previous:

Bills of Lading

Commercial invoices

Packing lists

Certificates of Origin

These help demonstrate genuine export activity.

11. Australian import requirements

Before placing an order, ensure:

The goods are permitted to enter Australia.

They comply with applicable Australian standards.

They satisfy any quarantine or biosecurity requirements administered by the relevant Australian authorities.

Required product markings, labelling, and documentation are available.

Practical due diligence checklist
Area	What to verify
Legal identity	Business licence, registration status, legal representative
Ownership	Shareholders, registered capital, company history
Compliance	Administrative penalties, court cases, abnormal operations
Export capability	Customs registration, export experience
Manufacturing	Factory existence, audit reports, production capacity
Product compliance	Test reports, Australian certifications
Financial health	Credit reports, trade references
Logistics	Previous export documents
IP	Trademark and patent ownership
Australian compliance	Import eligibility, applicable standards and biosecurity requirements

For higher-value purchases or long-term supplier relationships, many importers also engage an independent inspection or audit firm to perform an on-site factory audit before committing to production. This can help verify that the factory, equipment, workforce, and quality systems match the supplier's claims.
