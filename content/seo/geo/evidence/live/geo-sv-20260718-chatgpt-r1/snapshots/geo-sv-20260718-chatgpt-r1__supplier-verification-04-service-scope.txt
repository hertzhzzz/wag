## User message (UI)
What should be included in a paid supplier verification service for an Australian importer?

## Assistant answer (UI text)
For an Australian importer sourcing from China, a paid supplier verification service should go well beyond checking whether a business registration exists. Its purpose is to reduce commercial, operational, legal, and quality risk before purchase orders are placed.

A comprehensive verification package would typically include:

Category	What should be verified	Why it matters
Company identity	Legal company name (Chinese), Unified Social Credit Code, registration status, registered address, legal representative, registered capital, establishment date	Confirms the supplier is a legitimate registered business.
Export capability	Whether the company can legally export directly or relies on an export agent	Determines who will actually appear as the exporter on customs documentation.
Business scope	Products the company is legally permitted to manufacture or trade	Ensures the goods fall within its registered activities.
Ownership	Parent company, subsidiaries, beneficial ownership (where available)	Identifies related-party risks and corporate structure.
Financial status	Registered capital, operating status, bankruptcy proceedings, abnormal business listings	Helps identify financially distressed suppliers.
Litigation	Court cases, enforcement actions, administrative penalties, IP disputes	Reveals legal and compliance risks.
Tax status	Tax registration and abnormal tax records (where publicly available)	Indicates regulatory compliance.
Manufacturing capability	Factory address, production lines, machinery, employee count, production capacity	Distinguishes manufacturers from trading companies.
Quality systems	ISO certifications, quality management procedures, calibration systems	Demonstrates process maturity.
Product certifications	CE, FCC, RCM, UL, RoHS, REACH, or other relevant approvals	Confirms products can meet destination-market requirements.
Export history	Main export markets, shipment frequency, customs records (where available)	Shows actual exporting experience.
Customer references	Existing overseas customers (when permission is granted)	Provides independent confidence.
Supply chain	Key raw material suppliers and subcontractors	Identifies concentration risks.
ESG & labour	Environmental permits, labour compliance, audit reports (SMETA, BSCI, SA8000, etc.)	Important for larger importers and retailers.
Insurance	Product liability insurance and business insurance	Useful if defects or recalls occur.
Intellectual property	Trademark ownership, patent disputes, infringement history	Important for branded or OEM products.
Factory verification

A higher-tier service should include an on-site audit rather than relying only on document review.

The audit should verify:

Physical existence of the factory

Production actually occurring

Number of workers

Equipment matching claimed capabilities

Warehouse inventory

Incoming quality inspection

Final quality inspection

Packaging process

Health and safety conditions

Photographs and videos

GPS location confirmation

Export compliance

For Australian importers, verification should specifically confirm:

Ability to issue commercial invoices and packing lists correctly

Export customs registration

Experience exporting to Australia

Experience with Australian standards

Understanding of RCM (where applicable)

Ability to provide certificates of origin if required

Risk assessment

Rather than simply presenting raw information, the report should include an overall assessment, for example:

Identity risk

Financial risk

Production risk

Quality risk

Export risk

Compliance risk

Overall supplier rating (e.g. Low / Medium / High risk)

Each rating should be supported by evidence.

Documentation package

The final deliverable should include:

Company registration documents

Business licence translation

Corporate ownership chart

Factory photographs

Production capability summary

Certification verification

Litigation search results

Sanctions screening

Verification of contact details

Recommendations and identified red flags

Optional premium services

For larger purchases, additional services can provide further assurance:

Initial factory audit before ordering

Pre-production capability assessment

During-production inspections

Pre-shipment inspection

Container loading supervision

Product testing at accredited laboratories

Ongoing supplier monitoring with alerts for changes such as deregistration, litigation, or ownership changes

For Australian SMEs importing from China, the strongest value typically comes from combining desktop due diligence, an on-site factory audit, and pre-shipment quality inspection. This approach addresses the three largest sources of risk: verifying the supplier is legitimate, confirming it can manufacture the product as claimed, and ensuring the shipped goods meet the agreed specifications before they leave China.
