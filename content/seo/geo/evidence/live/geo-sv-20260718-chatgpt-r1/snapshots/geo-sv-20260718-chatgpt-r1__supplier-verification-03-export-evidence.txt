What evidence should I request to verify that a Chinese supplier can legally export my product?

## User message (UI)
What evidence should I request to verify that a Chinese supplier can legally export my product?

## Assistant answer (UI text)
When importing from China into Australia, do not rely on a supplier's statement that they are "licensed to export." China no longer requires a general export licence for most products, but exporters still need the correct registrations and must comply with any product-specific export controls.

Request the following evidence:

Evidence	Why it matters	What to verify
Chinese Business Licence	Confirms the company legally exists	Company name, Unified Social Credit Code, business scope
Customs Registration (Customs Filing)	Shows the company is registered with Chinese Customs to conduct import/export activities	Registration number matches the company
Export Declaration Examples	Demonstrates actual export experience	Remove commercial information if necessary, but check exporter name and product type
VAT Export Invoices (redacted)	Indicates genuine export transactions	Company name matches the supplier
Bill of Lading (redacted)	Confirms they have previously shipped internationally	Exporter or shipper name should match
Product-specific export permits (if applicable)	Required for controlled goods	Verify validity and applicability
Manufacturer authorisation (if trading company)	Confirms authority to export the manufacturer's products	Signed agreement or authorisation letter

Also ask for:

HS code they intend to declare.

Country of origin declaration.

Factory address.

Production photos or videos.

Recent shipping destinations (Australia, EU, US, etc.).

Verify whether export controls apply

Some categories require additional approvals, including:

Military or dual-use goods.

Chemicals.

Medical devices.

Food and agricultural products.

Timber and endangered species products.

Cultural relics.

If your product falls into one of these categories, request copies of the relevant export approvals before placing an order.

Cross-check independently

Do not rely solely on documents provided by the supplier. You should:

Verify the company registration details against Chinese government records.

Confirm the company name is identical across:

Business licence

Customs registration

Proforma invoice

Bank account

Check whether the exporter on shipping documents matches the contracting party.

Confirm the HS code is appropriate for the product.

Ensure there are no Chinese export restrictions or Australian import prohibitions affecting the goods.

A practical request to send the supplier

Please provide the following documents for our compliance review:

Your current Chinese Business Licence.

Your China Customs import/export registration (or customs filing certificate).

Your intended HS code for our product.

Two recent export shipment documents (Bill of Lading or Export Declaration, with customer details redacted if necessary).

Any export permits or licences required for this product.

If you are a trading company, a letter authorising you to export on behalf of the manufacturer.

We use these documents solely to complete our supplier due diligence before placing an order.

For Australian importers, this should be combined with Australian-side checks (such as tariff classification, biosecurity requirements, sanctions screening, and product compliance requirements like RCM, electrical safety, or other applicable standards) before the first shipment.
