In a quality inspection, critical, major, and minor defects are categories used to classify product issues by their impact on safety, function, appearance, compliance, or customer acceptance. These categories are commonly used with AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) inspection standards.

Defect Level	Definition	Typical Impact	Examples
Critical defect	A defect that creates a safety risk, violates legal requirements, or makes the product dangerous or unusable.	Product failure, injury risk, regulatory non-compliance, brand damage.	Electrical product with exposed live wires; incorrect medical device function; counterfeit safety certification; dangerous sharp edges; wrong voltage rating causing fire risk.
Major defect	A defect that significantly affects product function, performance, durability, or customer usability, but is not normally a direct safety hazard.	Customer dissatisfaction, returns, reduced product value.	Product does not operate correctly; missing important component; incorrect dimensions affecting use; damaged packaging causing product protection issues; significant cosmetic damage.
Minor defect	A small issue that does not significantly affect function, safety, or usability but falls outside agreed quality standards.	Cosmetic concern or small deviation from specification.	Minor scratches; slight colour variation; small printing error; loose thread; minor packaging imperfections.
Practical inspection approach for importers

For an Australian importer inspecting goods from a Chinese factory:

Critical defects

Usually have a zero tolerance requirement.

Any confirmed critical defect normally results in inspection failure.

Supplier must investigate and provide corrective action before shipment.

Major defects

Controlled through AQL limits.

Common AQL levels:

AQL 1.5: stricter quality requirement

AQL 2.5: common commercial standard

AQL 4.0: more tolerant standard

Minor defects

Usually accepted in higher quantities because they do not affect product performance.

Often managed through a higher AQL limit (for example AQL 4.0).

Example: LED lighting product inspection
Issue Found	Classification
Product fails electrical safety test	Critical
Incorrect wattage compared with purchase specification	Major
Scratched aluminium housing visible to customer	Minor
Missing Australian compliance label	Critical or Major depending on regulatory requirement
Slightly uneven packaging print	Minor
For purchase orders, buyers should define:

The defect classification rules before production starts.

Acceptable AQL levels.

Zero-tolerance defects.

Testing requirements (electrical, safety, performance).

Required corrective action process after inspection failure.

A well-written inspection checklist should convert these categories into specific acceptance criteria for the exact product rather than relying only on generic AQL definitions.
