An Australian buyer should arrange a during-production inspection (DPI) rather than relying only on a pre-shipment inspection (PSI) when there is a meaningful risk that defects, delays, or process problems could become expensive or impossible to fix after production is complete.

Use during-production inspection when:
1. The order is large or high-value

A DPI is recommended when the financial exposure is significant, such as:

Large purchase orders

Custom-made products

Private-label goods

First orders with a new factory

Products requiring tooling or specialised processes

Finding problems halfway through production allows the factory to correct them before the entire batch is completed.

2. The product has complex manufacturing steps

DPI is useful for products involving multiple production stages, for example:

Electronics assembly

Furniture with multiple components

Machinery

Lighting and AV equipment

Garments requiring dyeing, cutting, and stitching

Products requiring testing or certification

A PSI may identify failures only after thousands of units are already produced.

3. Quality problems cannot be easily reworked

Arrange DPI if defects would require:

Re-manufacturing

Re-ordering components

Factory shutdowns

Expensive repairs

Long delays before shipping

Examples:

Wrong materials used

Incorrect dimensions

Failed safety testing

Incorrect colour batches

Missing certifications

4. The factory is new or not fully trusted

For a first purchase from a Chinese supplier, DPI helps verify:

Production has actually started

Materials match specifications

The factory is following approved samples

The supplier is not outsourcing without approval

Production capacity is realistic

5. Delivery deadlines are strict

If late delivery would cause commercial damage, DPI can check:

Production progress against schedule

Whether bottlenecks exist

Whether raw materials are available

Whether manpower and equipment are sufficient

A PSI performed at the end of production may discover delays too late to recover.

6. The product requires regulatory compliance

Australian importers should consider DPI where compliance is critical, such as:

Electrical products requiring Australian compliance checks

Products requiring test reports or certifications

Safety-related products

Children's products

Medical or industrial equipment

The inspector can verify compliance-related processes before shipment.

Typical timing for a during-production inspection

A common approach:

Production stage	Inspection purpose
10–30% complete	Check materials, components, first production output
30–70% complete	Identify production issues while correction is still possible
Before final assembly/packing	Verify critical workmanship and specifications
Before shipment	Conduct final PSI

For many Chinese factory orders, the most useful point is around 30–50% production completion.

DPI vs PSI decision
Situation	Recommended inspection
Small repeat order from trusted supplier	PSI only
First order with unknown factory	DPI + PSI
Large custom production	DPI + PSI
Simple stocked product	PSI may be enough
Tight delivery deadline	DPI
High compliance risk	DPI + PSI
Expensive tooling or moulds	DPI
Practical recommendation for Australian importers

A common risk-control sequence is:

Supplier verification / factory audit before purchase order

During-production inspection after production begins

Pre-shipment inspection before final payment

Container loading inspection for high-risk shipments

For a first-time Chinese supplier or a high-value order, waiting until pre-shipment inspection is usually too late because the buyer may discover problems only after the factory has already committed most resources and the shipment deadline is approaching.
