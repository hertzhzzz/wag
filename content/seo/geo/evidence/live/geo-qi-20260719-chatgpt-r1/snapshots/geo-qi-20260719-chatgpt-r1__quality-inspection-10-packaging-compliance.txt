For an AU-NZ importer, a China inspection should not only check product quality; it should also verify that packaging, labels, and compliance requirements are ready for Australian and New Zealand market entry. These checks should be added as explicit inspection points in the inspection checklist rather than left to the factory.

1. Packaging checks
Carton and shipping packaging

Inspect:

Carton dimensions and weight match the agreed specification

Carton strength is suitable for sea/air transport

Export cartons are dry, clean, and undamaged

Correct carton markings:

Product name

SKU/model number

Quantity per carton

Country of origin marking (where required)

Handling marks (fragile, keep dry, orientation arrows)

Pallet requirements:

Pallet size and material

Heat treatment requirements for wooden pallets (ISPM 15 if applicable)

Secure wrapping and strapping

Inner packaging

Check:

Product protection against scratches, moisture, impact

Foam, bags, inserts, protective films match approved samples

Accessories are packed correctly

User manuals and warranty cards included

No missing components

Packaging quantity verification

Confirm:

Units per inner carton

Cartons per master carton

Total shipment quantity

Spare parts/accessories quantities

2. Product labelling checks

The inspection should compare physical labels against approved artwork.

Check:

Product identification

Brand name

Model number

SKU

Batch/lot number (if required)

Serial number (if applicable)

Country of origin

Verify:

"Made in China" or equivalent origin marking where required

Origin information is consistent across:

Product label

Packaging

Commercial documents

Consumer information

For Australian and New Zealand markets, check:

English language instructions

Warning statements

Safety information

Correct units:

Metric measurements

Australian/New Zealand spelling where relevant

Barcode and traceability

Verify:

Barcode scans correctly

Barcode matches product database/SKU

Batch coding is readable

Labels are permanent and durable

3. Australian compliance checks

The inspection checklist should include product-specific regulatory verification.

Examples:

Electrical products

Check:

Correct Australian plug type (Type I)

Correct voltage/frequency marking:

220–240V

50Hz

Required regulatory markings

Supplier has evidence of applicable compliance obligations

For regulated electrical equipment, verify documentation related to the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission requirements and applicable Australian electrical safety schemes.

Radio/wireless products

Check:

Required markings and documentation for wireless equipment

Supplier has appropriate conformity evidence for the Australian market

Toys and children's products

Check:

Age grading

Safety warnings

Small parts warnings

Test reports

Materials compliance documentation

Cosmetics, food-contact items, and chemicals

Check:

Ingredient/material declarations

Safety documentation

Required warnings

Batch information

4. New Zealand compliance checks

For NZ-bound goods, include:

English labelling

Product safety warnings

Electrical compliance requirements where applicable

Documentation consistency between Australia and New Zealand requirements

For products sold across both markets, many importers use a combined AU/NZ compliance review because requirements often overlap but are not identical.

5. Documentation verification during inspection

Ask the inspector to verify factory records:

Document	Check
Product specification sheet	Matches approved sample
Packing list	Matches actual carton contents
Test reports	Correct model/product
Certificates	Valid issuer and dates
User manual	Correct language/version
Artwork approval	Matches final production
Material declarations	Correct product
6. Common inspection failures for AU-NZ imports

Typical issues found:

Chinese-only manuals included

Incorrect plug type fitted

Missing safety warnings

Wrong barcode printed

Packaging artwork differs from approval sample

Missing compliance labels

Fake or unrelated test reports

Product model on certificates does not match shipment

Carton labels inconsistent with packing list

Accessories missing

7. Recommended inspection checklist structure

A practical AU-NZ inspection checklist:

Product specification verification

Appearance and workmanship

Function testing

Quantity verification

Packaging inspection

Label and artwork verification

Compliance marking verification

Documentation review

Carton loading inspection

Photo evidence collection

For higher-risk products, add a pre-production compliance review before manufacturing begins, because fixing labels or packaging after production is often expensive.
