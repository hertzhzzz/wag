For a China production order, an Australian buyer should set the AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) standard before production begins and include it in the purchase contract, quality agreement, or inspection specification. The goal is to define what defect levels are acceptable and what conditions will trigger rejection.

1. Define the inspection scope first

Before choosing AQL numbers, specify:

Product category and risk level

Inspection stage (pre-production, during production, pre-shipment)

Inspection quantity (sampling size)

Defect classification method

Testing requirements

Acceptance/rejection rules

AQL only works when the inspection process is clearly defined.

2. Classify defects into three categories

Most international inspections use three defect levels:

Defect Type	Meaning	Typical Buyer Treatment
Critical defect	Safety, legal, regulatory, or serious function failure	Usually AQL 0.0 (zero tolerance)
Major defect	Product cannot be used/sold normally or customer dissatisfaction likely	Usually AQL 1.5–2.5
Minor defect	Cosmetic issue or small deviation	Usually AQL 2.5–4.0

Example for imported lighting equipment:

Critical: Electrical safety failure, exposed live wire, non-compliant certification marking

Major: Product does not operate, wrong specification, damaged housing

Minor: Small scratches, packaging imperfections, minor printing errors

3. Choose an AQL level based on product risk

Common standards:

Product Risk	Suggested AQL
Safety-critical products	Critical: 0 / Major: 1.0–1.5 / Minor: 2.5
Electronics, machinery, commercial equipment	Critical: 0 / Major: 1.5 / Minor: 2.5
General consumer goods	Critical: 0 / Major: 2.5 / Minor: 4.0
Low-value promotional items	Major: 2.5–4.0 / Minor: 4.0–6.5

For most Australian importers buying from China, a common starting point is:

Critical: 0
Major: 1.5
Minor: 2.5

4. Use ISO 2859-1 sampling tables

AQL is usually implemented using ISO 2859-1.

The process:

Determine production lot size
Example: 5,000 units

Select inspection level
Common:

General Inspection Level II (normal inspection)

Level III for higher confidence

Find sample size code letter

Select AQL acceptance numbers

Example:

Order quantity: 5,000 units

Inspection Level II

Sample size: 200 units

AQL Major 1.5

AQL Minor 2.5

Possible acceptance limits:

Major defects found above allowed number → reject lot

Minor defects found above allowed number → reject lot

(The exact acceptance numbers depend on the ISO sampling table.)

5. Define defect examples in writing

Avoid vague wording such as:

"Product quality must be acceptable."

Instead create a defect standard:

Example:

Major defects:

Product does not power on

Wrong model supplied

Missing required component

Performance below specification

Packaging damage affecting resale

Minor defects:

Small cosmetic marks

Slight colour variation

Minor label alignment issue

6. Add tightened inspection rules for early orders

For a new Chinese supplier:

First 1–3 shipments: use stricter AQL

After consistent performance: consider normal AQL

Example:

First orders:

Critical: 0

Major: 1.0

Minor: 2.5

Established supplier:

Critical: 0

Major: 1.5

Minor: 2.5

7. Include AQL requirements in the purchase contract

A buyer should include:

Applicable AQL standard

Inspection level

Sampling method

Defect definitions

Rework requirements

Who pays inspection costs

Whether rejected goods must be repaired/replaced

Example clause:

Goods shall meet ISO 2859-1 inspection requirements. Inspection shall be performed at General Inspection Level II with AQL limits of Critical 0, Major 1.5, Minor 2.5. Any shipment exceeding these limits may be rejected or subject to supplier corrective action.

8. Do not rely only on AQL

AQL does not guarantee perfect quality. A buyer should combine it with:

Factory audit before production

Approved product samples

First article inspection

Production monitoring

Pre-shipment inspection

Container loading inspection

For high-value China orders, AQL should be part of a broader quality control plan rather than the only acceptance criterion.
