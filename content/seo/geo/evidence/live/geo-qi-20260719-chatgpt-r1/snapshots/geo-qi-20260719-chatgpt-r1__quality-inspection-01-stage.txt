For an Australian importer buying from a Chinese factory, the best pre-shipment inspection (PSI) stage is usually when production is 100% complete and at least 80–100% of goods are packed for export, but before the shipment leaves the factory.

This is the most common inspection stage because it allows the buyer to verify the actual finished order before paying the final balance or authorising shipment.

Recommended inspection stages
Inspection stage	When to use	What it checks	Suitable for
Pre-production inspection (PPI)	Before manufacturing starts	Raw materials, specifications, production plan, supplier readiness	New suppliers, high-risk products
During production inspection (DPI/DUPRO)	Around 20–50% production completed	Workmanship, assembly process, early defects, production progress	Large orders, complex products
Pre-shipment inspection (PSI)	Finished goods completed before shipment	Quantity, appearance, functionality, packaging, labelling, export readiness	Most Australian import orders
Container loading inspection (CLI)	During container loading	Correct goods loaded, quantities, packaging condition, seal numbers	High-value shipments, repeat problems
For most Australian importers: use PSI before final payment

A typical process:

Factory completes production.

Inspector visits the factory.

Inspector selects samples using an acceptable quality limit (AQL) sampling method.

Inspector checks:

Product specifications against purchase order

Dimensions and materials

Workmanship defects

Functional testing

Safety requirements

Packaging and carton markings

Quantity confirmation

Shipping documents

Buyer reviews inspection report.

Buyer approves shipment and releases remaining payment.

Do not rely only on pre-shipment inspection when:
1. The product has safety or compliance risks

Examples:

Electrical equipment

Lighting products

Children's products

Machinery

Automotive components

Use pre-production + during-production + final inspection because defects discovered at the end may be expensive to correct.

2. The order value is high

For a large Australian import order, consider:

Factory audit before purchase order

Production monitoring during manufacturing

Final PSI before shipment

Container loading check

3. The supplier is new

A first order from an unknown Chinese factory should usually include:

Supplier verification

Factory audit

Pre-production inspection

Pre-shipment inspection

Australian importer decision rule
Situation	Recommended inspection
First order with new Chinese supplier	Factory audit + PSI
Repeat supplier with good history	PSI
Order over AUD $50k	DPI + PSI
Custom-made products	PPI + DPI + PSI
Electronics/regulated products	Technical inspection + compliance verification
Previous quality issues	DPI + PSI + loading inspection

For most Australian businesses importing manufactured goods from China, the minimum risk-control point is a third-party pre-shipment inspection completed before final payment and before the container leaves the factory.
