Before approving a Chinese factory for production, an Australian buyer should require closure of any audit findings that create material risk to product quality, legal compliance, delivery reliability, or supply-chain control. Not every minor observation needs to block approval, but the following findings should generally be resolved before issuing a production purchase order.

1. Factory identity and ownership issues (approval blockers)
Finding	Why it must be resolved
Factory cannot provide valid business licence or legal entity information	Risk of dealing with an unrelated trading company or fraudulent supplier
Registered business name does not match contracts, invoices, or bank details	Creates payment and legal enforcement risk
Factory refuses site access or restricts key areas during audit	Indicates lack of transparency
Undisclosed subcontracting or production at another facility	Buyer loses control over quality and compliance

Required closure evidence:

Verified business licence

Confirmed factory address

Contract entity matches payment recipient

Written approval of any subcontractors

2. Quality management system failures

These should be resolved before approval:

Critical findings

No documented quality procedures

No incoming material inspection

No in-process quality checks

No final inspection process

No traceability system

No handling process for defective products

A factory without basic quality controls may produce acceptable samples but fail during mass production.

Required closure evidence:

Quality manual or procedures

Inspection records

Defect handling records

Corrective action reports

Production quality checkpoints

3. Production capability gaps

Approve only after confirming the factory can meet your order requirements.

Red flags:

Factory cannot demonstrate similar product experience

Machinery shown during audit is insufficient

Key processes are outsourced without disclosure

Production capacity claims cannot be supported

No production planning system

Required closure evidence:

Production line demonstration

Equipment list

Capacity calculation

Previous customer examples (where possible)

Production schedule capability

4. Material and supplier control problems

Resolve before approval:

No approved supplier list

No incoming material inspection

Unknown material sources

No batch or lot tracking

Materials stored incorrectly

This is especially important for:

Electronics

Lighting equipment

Machinery

Safety-related products

Products requiring Australian compliance certification

Required closure evidence:

Approved supplier records

Material certificates

Incoming inspection reports

Storage controls

5. Product compliance and certification risks

Production should not begin if the factory cannot prove compliance requirements.

Examples:

Missing Australian regulatory documentation

Fake or unverifiable certificates

Incorrect use of CE/RoHS/RCM markings

No test reports for regulated products

Certification belongs to another factory or model

Required closure evidence:

Valid test reports

Certification ownership confirmation

Product specification sheets

Compliance declarations

6. Factory labour and ethical compliance issues

Serious findings should block approval:

Child labour indicators

Forced labour concerns

Unsafe working conditions

Missing worker safety procedures

Illegal employment practices

For Australian companies, these issues also create reputational and contractual risks.

Required closure evidence:

Safety training records

Emergency procedures

Workplace inspection records

Labour documentation

7. Production process weaknesses

Before approval, verify:

No documented work instructions

Operators rely only on verbal instructions

No process controls for critical steps

No calibration system for measuring equipment

No maintenance records

Required closure evidence:

Work instructions

Process flow charts

Equipment calibration certificates

Maintenance logs

8. Financial and delivery reliability concerns

Not all financial findings are visible in a factory audit, but operational warning signs matter:

Block approval if:

Factory frequently changes payment accounts

Factory depends heavily on one subcontractor

Factory has severe production delays with existing customers

Factory cannot explain workforce fluctuations

Required closure evidence:

Stable banking information

Production planning records

Capacity confirmation

Findings that usually do not block approval

These can often be accepted with a corrective action plan:

Minor housekeeping issues

Slight documentation gaps

Cosmetic facility problems

Small storage improvements

Non-critical training records missing

Example:

"Warehouse labels are inconsistent but materials remain traceable."

This is different from:

"Materials cannot be traced to production batches."

Recommended approval rule for Australian buyers

Use a simple classification:

Finding level	Approval decision
Critical	Factory approval blocked until closed
Major	Closure required before production
Minor	Accept with corrective action deadline
Observation	Monitor during future audits

Before releasing a large production order, the buyer should ideally have:

Completed factory audit report

Corrective action plan

Evidence of closure for critical/major findings

Approved sample or pilot production result

Pre-production inspection process agreed

Contractual quality requirements documented

The factory audit should be treated as a risk approval gate, not just a supplier scoring exercise.
