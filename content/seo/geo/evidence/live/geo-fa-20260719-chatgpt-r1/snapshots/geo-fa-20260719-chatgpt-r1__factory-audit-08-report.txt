A factory audit report should give an Australian buyer enough evidence to decide whether to approve, conditionally approve, negotiate improvements, or reject a supplier. It should go beyond a checklist score and explain operational risks.

A useful factory audit report should include:

1. Executive Summary

Factory name, legal entity name, location, audit date

Auditor name/company

Product category and scope of audit

Overall rating or risk classification

Key findings:

Critical issues

Major non-conformities

Strengths

Recommended next action

Example decision summary:

✅ Approved — suitable for production

⚠️ Approved with corrective actions

❌ Not recommended — unacceptable risks

2. Supplier Identity Verification

Confirm the supplier is the actual business being audited.

Include:

Registered company name

Chinese business licence details

Unified Social Credit Code

Factory address

Ownership information (where available)

Years operating

Export experience

Main export markets

Contact persons and roles

Red flags:

Audit location differs from registered address

Factory cannot provide legal documents

Supplier claims to manufacture products they cannot demonstrate

3. Factory Profile and Capacity Assessment

The report should verify whether the factory can meet the Australian buyer's order requirements.

Include:

Factory size and production areas

Number of employees

Production lines

Working hours/shifts

Monthly production capacity

Current utilisation rate

Main equipment list

Production bottlenecks

The auditor should compare:

Buyer Requirement	Factory Capability
10,000 units/month	Factory capacity
60-day delivery	Production schedule
Custom packaging	Available capability
4. Production Process Review

Document the actual manufacturing process.

Include:

Process flow diagram

Raw material receiving

Cutting/forming/assembly

Testing procedures

Packaging

Finished goods storage

Production controls

Evidence:

Photos

Production records

Work instructions

Machine operation observations

5. Quality Management System (QMS)

Assess whether quality is controlled systematically.

Include:

Incoming Quality Control (IQC)

Supplier material inspection

Incoming inspection records

Material approval process

In-Process Quality Control (IPQC)

Inspection points

Operator checks

Defect recording

Final Quality Control (FQC)

Final inspection procedures

Sampling standards

Test reports

Release approval process

Check:

Is QC independent from production?

Are defects tracked?

Are corrective actions documented?

6. Product Testing and Compliance Capability

Important for Australian importers.

Include:

Available testing equipment

Calibration records

Product-specific testing procedures

Compliance certificates

Previous test reports

Depending on product category:

Electrical safety

EMC testing

Material safety

Chemical compliance

Australian regulatory requirements

7. Subcontracting and Supply Chain Assessment

The report should identify whether production is outsourced.

Include:

Which processes are subcontracted

Subcontractor locations

Supplier approval process

Control over outsourced production

Warning signs:

Factory refuses to disclose subcontractors

Production area appears inconsistent with claimed capacity

No traceability for outsourced processes

8. Workforce and Management Review

Include:

Management structure

Production supervisors

Quality team size

Employee turnover indicators

Training records

Worker competency programs

9. Social Compliance and Workplace Conditions

Depending on buyer requirements:

Review:

Working hours

Worker records

Safety equipment

Emergency exits

Fire safety

Environmental controls

For Australian retailers or corporate buyers, this section may be required for supplier approval.

10. Documentation Review

The auditor should list documents reviewed.

Examples:

Business licence

ISO certificates

Production orders

Inspection records

Training records

Calibration certificates

Material certificates

Export documents

The report should indicate:

Verified

Not available

Expired

Requires follow-up

11. Photos and Evidence

A strong report includes dated photos of:

Factory exterior

Production lines

Warehouse

Raw materials

Finished products

QC stations

Testing equipment

Safety facilities

Photos should support findings, not just show the factory looks clean.

12. Risk Assessment Table

A decision-focused report should include:

Area	Risk Level	Finding	Impact	Action
Capacity	Medium	Production near full utilisation	Possible delays	Confirm schedule
QC System	High	No traceability records	Quality risk	Require improvement
Compliance	Low	Certificates verified	Low risk	Proceed
13. Corrective Action Plan (CAPA)

For problems found, include:

Finding

Root cause

Required corrective action

Responsible person

Deadline

Verification method

Example:

Final inspection records missing for previous three batches. Supplier must implement batch inspection records before mass production approval.

14. Final Recommendation

The auditor should provide a sourcing recommendation:

Approve

Supplier meets requirements.

Conditional Approval

Proceed only after corrective actions.

Reject

Risk too high due to:

Fake capability

Poor quality controls

Compliance failures

Lack of transparency

For an Australian importer placing a large order, the most valuable parts are usually factory identity verification, production capacity evidence, QMS evaluation, subcontracting checks, compliance capability, and risk-based recommendations rather than the audit score alone.
