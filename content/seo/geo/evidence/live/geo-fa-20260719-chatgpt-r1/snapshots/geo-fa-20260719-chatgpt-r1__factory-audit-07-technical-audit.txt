A buyer should use a technical audit instead of (or in addition to) a general factory audit when the main risk is not whether the factory is a real operating business, but whether it can consistently produce the product to the required technical specification.

A general factory audit checks overall supplier capability. A technical audit goes deeper into engineering, manufacturing controls, process capability, and product-specific risks.

Use a technical audit when:
Situation	Why a technical audit is more suitable
The product has complex specifications	The auditor needs to verify engineering capability, tolerances, materials, processes, and testing methods
Product failure could create safety or compliance issues	Focus is needed on quality controls, validation testing, and regulatory requirements
You are placing a large first order	You need confidence that the factory can actually manufacture your design at scale
The supplier claims proprietary manufacturing capability	A technical review can test whether the claimed processes and equipment exist
You have experienced quality problems before	The audit can identify root causes in production rather than just administrative weaknesses
The product requires custom tooling or moulds	You need to assess tooling design, maintenance, and production stability
The product requires certification	The audit should verify that certification requirements are integrated into production controls
You are transferring production from another factory	A technical audit checks whether the new supplier can match existing quality standards
Examples where a technical audit is preferable
Electronics

A general audit may confirm:

Factory exists

Workers are employed

Basic QC procedures exist

A technical audit should additionally examine:

PCB assembly process

Component sourcing controls

SMT equipment calibration

Firmware/version control

Reliability testing

Failure analysis procedures

Machinery or industrial equipment

A technical audit should review:

Engineering drawings

CNC capability

Welding procedures

Material traceability

Pressure testing

Factory acceptance testing

Lighting and AV equipment

A technical audit should check:

LED driver quality controls

Electrical safety testing

EMC testing capability

Thermal management testing

IP rating validation

RCM-related documentation readiness for Australia

Signs you need a technical audit

Consider upgrading from a general audit if the supplier:

Cannot explain their production process clearly

Outsources critical processes without disclosure

Has inconsistent samples versus mass production

Cannot provide inspection/test records

Has frequent customer complaints

Makes broad quality claims but provides little evidence

Cannot identify key process control points

When a general factory audit is enough

A standard factory audit is usually sufficient for:

Simple consumer products

Low-value orders

Established suppliers with a strong track record

Products where defects are easy to detect before shipment

Repeat orders with stable production history

For an Australian importer

A common approach is:

Supplier verification
Confirm legal entity, ownership, export capability, and business legitimacy.

General factory audit
Confirm factory existence, workforce, capacity, management systems, and basic controls.

Technical audit
Apply when product complexity, compliance requirements, or order value justify deeper engineering review.

Pre-shipment inspection / production inspection
Verify the actual batch before shipment.

For high-value imports from China, especially custom products, machinery, electronics, lighting, or regulated goods, a technical audit is often the step that prevents expensive failures after production has already started.
