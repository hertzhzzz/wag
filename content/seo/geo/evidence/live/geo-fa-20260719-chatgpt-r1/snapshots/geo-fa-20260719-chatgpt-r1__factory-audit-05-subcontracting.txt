During a factory audit, an Australian buyer should treat unexpected subcontracting as a major supply-chain risk because it can affect quality control, delivery reliability, intellectual property protection, compliance obligations, and product traceability. Key warning signs include:

1. Production areas do not match the supplier’s claims

Warning signs:

Factory size appears too small for the order volume quoted.

Machinery shown during previous discussions is missing or unused.

Production lines look inactive despite claimed high output.

Workers cannot explain the product manufacturing process.

The factory only performs simple assembly while claiming full manufacturing capability.

Audit checks:

Compare stated production capacity with:

Number of machines

Number of workers

Shift patterns

Monthly output records

2. Evidence of third-party production inside the factory

Warning signs:

Materials, cartons, labels, or semi-finished goods with another company’s name.

Separate production records for different company names.

Workers mention "partner factories" or "outside processing".

Finished goods are stored in mixed batches from different locations.

Audit checks:

Inspect:

Incoming material records

Production work orders

Batch tracking documents

Warehouse labels

3. Supplier refuses access to production areas

Warning signs:

Auditor is only shown:

Office

Sample room

Showroom

Empty workshop area

Factory claims certain areas are "restricted" without a valid reason.

Audit schedule avoids production hours.

Why it matters:
A supplier may be presenting a trading company office or sample facility rather than the actual manufacturing site.

4. Quality control is outsourced

Warning signs:

No internal QC staff.

Inspection reports are created by another company.

Workers cannot explain defect handling procedures.

Supplier relies entirely on buyer inspections.

Audit checks:
Request:

Incoming inspection records

In-process inspection reports

Final inspection reports

Non-conformance reports

5. Production records reveal external factories

Warning signs:

Production schedules reference different factory names.

Purchase orders are issued to unrelated manufacturers.

Material movement records show transfers between locations.

Documents to review:

Production plans

Manufacturing orders

Supplier lists

Outsourcing agreements

Subcontractor approval records

6. Inconsistent answers from management and workers

Warning signs:

Management says "everything is made here."

Workers say certain processes happen elsewhere.

Sales staff cannot explain manufacturing steps.

Different departments give conflicting production timelines.

Audit technique:
Ask the same question separately to:

Factory owner

Production manager

QC manager

Line workers

7. Lack of subcontractor controls

If subcontracting exists, it is not automatically a problem. The risk is uncontrolled subcontracting.

Acceptable evidence:

Approved subcontractor list

Written subcontracting agreements

Quality requirements transferred to subcontractors

Supplier audits of subcontractors

Traceability records

Warning signs:

"We use several factories but don't track them."

No subcontractor names provided.

No quality checks after outsourced production.

8. Pricing is unrealistically low

Warning signs:

Quote is significantly below comparable suppliers.

Supplier cannot explain cost advantages.

Supplier avoids discussing manufacturing processes.

Possible explanations:

Outsourcing to cheaper factories

Lower-grade materials

Uncontrolled production

9. Certifications do not match the actual production site

Warning signs:

ISO, BSCI, Sedex, CE, or product certificates show another company name/address.

Certificate holder is different from the audited factory.

Factory claims certificates belong to "our group company."

Audit checks:
Verify:

Certificate holder

Factory address

Scope of certification

Audit dates

10. No traceability system

For Australian importers, especially for regulated products, check whether the factory can trace:

Raw materials → Production line → QC inspection → Finished goods → Shipment

Warning signs:

No batch numbers

Manual records only

Cannot identify which materials went into a shipment

Cannot provide historical production records

Questions an Australian buyer should ask during the audit

"Do you outsource any manufacturing processes?"

"Which processes are completed outside this factory?"

"Can you provide your approved subcontractor list?"

"How do you control subcontractor quality?"

"Can we audit subcontractors if required?"

"Which company name appears on production records and export documents?"

"Who owns the tooling, moulds, and production data?"

Red flags that should trigger a pause before placing an order
Finding	Risk level
Supplier hides subcontracting	Critical
Actual factory cannot be verified	Critical
Certificates belong to another company	High
No production traceability	High
Unapproved subcontractors used	High
Minor outsourced processes disclosed and controlled	Medium
Approved subcontractors with audits and records	Low

For a large Australian purchase order, the audit report should clearly state which processes happen at the audited factory, which processes are outsourced, and who is responsible for final quality control before any deposit is paid.
