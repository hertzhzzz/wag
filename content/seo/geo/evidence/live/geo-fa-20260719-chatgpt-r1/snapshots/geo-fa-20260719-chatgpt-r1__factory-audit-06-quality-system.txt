An Australian importer assessing a Chinese factory’s quality management system (QMS) should verify whether the factory has repeatable processes that prevent defects, not just whether it holds certificates. A factory audit should examine the system from incoming materials through production, inspection, traceability, and corrective actions.

1. Verify quality certifications and their validity

Request and verify:

ISO 9001 certificate:

Certificate number

Certification body

Scope of certification

Expiry date

Actual factory address covered

Product-specific certifications where relevant:

Electrical safety

EMC compliance

Material certifications

Industry-specific approvals

Warning signs:

Certificate scope does not cover the product being purchased

Certificate belongs to a different company/address

Factory cannot provide audit reports from the certification body

Certificates are only marketing documents

2. Review the factory’s quality organisation

Assess whether quality responsibility is independent from production.

Check:

Area	Evidence to review
Quality manager	Employment record, role description
QC inspectors	Training records, inspection responsibilities
Production staff	Quality training records
Escalation process	Defect reporting flow
Authority	Ability to stop production

Red flag:

The production manager also controls final quality approval.

3. Examine incoming material controls

A reliable factory should control supplier materials before production begins.

Review:

Incoming Quality Control (IQC) procedures

Supplier approval process

Material inspection records

Raw material certificates

Batch identification system

Rejected material records

Ask:

How are defective materials handled?

Are suppliers scored?

Can they trace materials back to suppliers?

Example:
For a lighting importer, verify controls over:

LEDs

Drivers

Aluminium housings

Wiring components

Electronic components

4. Audit production quality controls

Review whether quality checks happen during manufacturing.

Look for:

Production documents

Work instructions

Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

Assembly drawings

Process control sheets

Inspection checkpoints

In-process inspection

Check:

First article inspection

Random production checks

Automated testing records

Operator self-inspection

Defect recording

Questions:

At what production stage are defects detected?

What happens when a defect rate increases?

Who approves production changes?

5. Assess final inspection procedures

A factory should have a documented final inspection system.

Review:

Final Quality Control (FQC) checklist

Sampling standards

Inspection reports

Product testing records

Packaging inspection

Common standards:

ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 (AQL sampling)

Customer-specific inspection plans

Check:

Does the factory inspect every shipment?

Are inspection results linked to purchase orders?

Are failed inspections documented?

6. Test traceability capability

Traceability is critical for Australian importers because defects may appear after products arrive.

Verify whether the factory can trace:

Production date

Production line

Operator/team

Raw materials batch

Inspection results

Shipment batch

Ask for a sample traceability exercise:

"Select one finished product from last month's shipment and show every record connected to it."

A strong factory should complete this quickly.

7. Review defect and corrective action processes

Assess their handling of problems.

Request examples of:

Non-Conformance Reports (NCR)

Corrective Action Reports (CAR)

Root cause analysis

Customer complaint records

Good systems use methods such as:

5 Why analysis

Fishbone diagrams

Preventive actions

Red flags:

"We rarely have defects"

No historical complaint records

Problems are fixed by rework only

No documented root cause analysis

8. Check calibration and testing equipment

Verify:

Equipment list

Calibration certificates

Calibration frequency

Test procedures

Operator training

Examples:

Electrical safety testers

Torque testers

Dimensional gauges

Environmental testing equipment

Warning signs:

Expired calibration labels

Missing equipment records

Testing equipment owned by another company

9. Evaluate supplier management

A factory's quality depends on its own suppliers.

Review:

Approved supplier list

Supplier audits

Supplier defect statistics

Incoming rejection rates

Supplier corrective actions

Ask:

"What were your three biggest supplier quality problems last year and how did you fix them?"

10. Verify quality performance data

Request historical data:

Defect rate (PPM or percentage)

Customer complaints

Returns

Warranty claims

On-time quality performance

Internal audit results

Avoid accepting only verbal claims.

11. Conduct a factory audit with practical tests

During an on-site audit, perform:

Document check

QMS manuals

Inspection records

Training records

Factory walkthrough

Production lines

QC stations

Warehouse

Finished goods area

Live sampling

Pick random products and ask:

Show inspection records

Show production history

Show test results

Production simulation

Ask the factory to demonstrate:

How a defect is identified

How production is stopped

How corrective action starts

Minimum acceptable QMS before placing a large order

For a high-value Australian import order, the factory should normally demonstrate:

Requirement	Expected evidence
Valid QMS certification	ISO certificate + scope
Documented QC process	SOPs + inspection plans
Incoming inspection	Material records
In-process inspection	Production QC reports
Final inspection	Shipment inspection records
Traceability	Batch records
Corrective actions	NCR/CAR examples
Equipment control	Calibration records
Trained QC team	Training evidence

A factory that can produce quality documents but cannot demonstrate them in actual production should be treated as higher risk. The strongest assessment combines document review + factory walkthrough + production record verification + independent product inspection before shipment.
