These three services address different stages of the sourcing process and different types of risk. They complement one another rather than replace one another.

Service	Primary purpose	When to use	Main question answered
Supplier verification	Confirm the supplier is a legitimate, suitable business	Before requesting samples or placing orders	"Is this the right company to work with?"
Factory audit	Assess the factory's operational capability and management systems	Before placing a significant order or selecting a long-term supplier	"Can this factory consistently produce what I need?"
Product inspection	Verify that finished or in-process goods meet requirements	During production or before shipment	"Did this batch meet my specifications?"
Supplier verification

Supplier verification is primarily a due diligence exercise. It typically includes:

Business licence verification

Unified Social Credit Code confirmation

Legal representative and registered address

Business scope

Export qualifications

Bank account verification

Basic reputation and litigation checks

Confirmation that the company actually exists

This helps reduce the risk of fraud, fake suppliers, or paying the wrong entity.

Factory audit

A factory audit is an on-site evaluation of the manufacturer's ability to produce consistently. A comprehensive audit usually examines:

Factory ownership and legal status

Production capacity

Manufacturing processes

Machinery and maintenance

Workforce size and skills

Quality management systems

Incoming material controls

In-process and final quality controls

Calibration of measuring equipment

Warehouse and inventory management

Traceability systems

Corrective action procedures

Social compliance and workplace safety (if required)

Environmental management (if relevant)

The auditor also looks for warning signs such as:

Production lines inconsistent with claimed capacity

Excessive outsourcing

Poor document control

Weak quality records

Disorganised production flow

Inadequate maintenance

Poor housekeeping

The outcome is an assessment of operational capability, not just legal legitimacy.

Product inspection

A product inspection evaluates the actual goods being produced. Common inspection stages include:

Pre-production inspection

During-production inspection

Pre-shipment inspection

Container loading supervision

Inspectors check:

Quantity

Dimensions

Appearance

Functional testing

Packaging

Labelling

Workmanship

Compliance with the approved specification

Acceptable Quality Limit (AQL) sampling

A product inspection only evaluates the sampled batch—it does not indicate whether the factory is well managed overall.

How they fit together

For an Australian importer sourcing from China, a typical risk-management sequence is:

Supplier verification to confirm the supplier is legitimate.

Factory audit for shortlisted suppliers before committing to substantial orders.

Product inspections on each production run before shipment.

Which is most important?

The answer depends on the purchase:

Small trial order (under about AUD 10,000): Supplier verification and a pre-shipment inspection are often sufficient.

Large purchase order: Use all three services.

Long-term manufacturing relationship: Conduct an initial factory audit, perform supplier verification, and continue with regular product inspections.

No single service replaces the others. A factory can be genuine but produce inconsistent quality, while a shipment can pass inspection even though the factory has weak management systems. Using supplier verification, factory audits, and product inspections together provides much stronger protection against commercial, operational, and quality risks.
