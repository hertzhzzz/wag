An Australian business comparing factory audit services in China should compare the audit methodology, auditor capability, evidence quality, reporting standard, and suitability for the product risk rather than only comparing price.

1. Compare the type of audit offered

Different providers may use the term “factory audit” for very different levels of investigation.

Audit type	Suitable when	Key checks
General factory audit	First-time supplier assessment	Factory identity, facilities, workforce, equipment, basic QMS
Technical audit	Complex or regulated products	Engineering capability, processes, testing, technical controls
Social compliance audit	Brand/customer requirements	Labour practices, safety, environmental compliance
Manufacturing process audit	High-volume orders	Production flow, bottlenecks, process controls
Supplier verification	Before supplier engagement/payment	Legal entity, ownership, export capability

For example, a buyer importing electrical equipment, machinery, lighting, or safety-critical products should usually require more than a basic factory capability report.

2. Evaluate auditor qualifications

Ask the audit company:

Are auditors based in China or travelling internationally?

What industries do they specialise in?

Do they have experience with your product category?

Are auditors trained in ISO-based auditing methods?

Are audits performed by employees or outsourced inspectors?

Is the same auditor available for follow-up inspections?

A low-cost general inspector may identify obvious issues but miss technical manufacturing risks.

Workforce and facilities

4. Compare evidence provided in the report

A useful audit report should include:

Strong evidence

Timestamped factory photos

Production line photos

Equipment photos

Document samples

Interview notes

Capacity calculations

Risk ratings

Weak evidence

Generic descriptions

Stock photos

Checklist with only pass/fail results

No explanation of findings

A buyer should be able to make a sourcing decision without needing another investigation.

5. Compare independence and conflict controls

Ask:

Does the auditor receive payment only from the buyer?

Does the service provider also sell factories or sourcing services?

Can the supplier influence the auditor?

Are audit findings reviewed internally?

Independent auditing reduces the risk of suppliers presenting a controlled or staged factory visit.

6. Compare pricing structure

Typical cost differences come from:

Auditor experience

Audit duration

Factory location

Product complexity

Report detail

Follow-up support

A cheaper audit may only confirm that a factory exists. A more expensive audit may identify risks that prevent a failed order.

Compare:

Cost item	Question
Audit fee	What is included?
Travel charges	Are they separate?
Report fee	Included or additional?
Follow-up audit	Available?
Translation	Included?
Urgent service	Extra cost?
7. Check turnaround time

Ask:

How quickly can the audit be scheduled?

When will the report be delivered?

Can urgent audits be completed?

Are corrective actions tracked?

For seasonal products, delays can directly affect sales.

8. Review sample reports before purchasing

Before selecting a provider, request a sample anonymised report and check:

Does it clearly identify risks?

Are findings prioritised?

Are photos included?

Are recommendations practical?

Does it help decide whether to place an order?

9. Match audit depth to order risk

A practical approach:

Situation	Recommended audit
First order under AUD $10,000	Supplier verification + basic audit
AUD $10,000–100,000 order	Factory audit + sample inspection
Large recurring orders	Technical/process audit + ongoing inspections
Custom products	Technical audit + production monitoring
Regulated products	Compliance-focused audit
10. Recommended comparison scorecard

Australian buyers can score providers:

Category	Weight
Auditor industry experience	20%
Audit checklist quality	20%
Evidence/report quality	20%
Independence	15%
Price/value	10%
Turnaround time	10%
Follow-up support	5%

The best factory audit service is usually not the cheapest; it is the one that provides enough verified information to decide whether the supplier can consistently deliver the required product quality, compliance, and volume.
