In quality inspection, defects are classified into three categories: Critical, Major, and Minor. These classifications are essential for evaluating product quality, determining acceptance or rejection under AQL standards, and reducing the risk of defective shipments in global supply chains. Here's a brief overview of each defect type:
Critical Defects: These are safety-related issues that can cause harm or regulatory failure and result in automatic rejection. Examples include electrical hazards, physical safety risks, and regulatory violations.
2
Major Defects: These affect product functionality, usability, or compliance and often lead to shipment rejection or rework.
2
Minor Defects: These do not affect function but impact appearance or finish and are usually acceptable within AQL limits.
2
Understanding these defect types is crucial for importers, sourcing managers, and third-party inspection companies working with manufacturing suppliers in Asia. It ensures that the inspection process is clear and consistent, leading to better product quality and compliance.
